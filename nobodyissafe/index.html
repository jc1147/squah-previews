Who is doing this

A desk with instruments
and skin in the game.

I'm James. I own marketing agencies, and I built SGEN, the software stack they run on. The agencies in these files are my competitors. That is not a confession; it is the qualification. A critic has opinions. A competitor has instruments, benchmarks, and every reason to be exact, because the people being measured would love nothing more than to catch this desk wrong.

I'm done watching shops in my industry bill small businesses every month for visibility the map says does not exist. So this desk measures an agency's entire client book, not the three logos on the homepage: geo-grid scans of every listing, speed tests on every site, template forensics on every “custom” design. If an agency's results are good, the file says so. So far, the files say something else.

Nobody pays to be excluded and nobody pays to be included. That part has not changed and never will. The rest of the model is printed on the door: the files are free, your own listing is free, and if what you find in yours makes you want it fixed, my consultants are one booking away. That is the whole model, in daylight: the receipts cost nothing, and the offer at the end of them has a name on it.

Roster discovery

We find every client an agency serves: showcase pages, template fingerprints, the code they leave behind in page source. The whole book, not the highlight reel.

Geo-grid scans

Each business is scanned across a grid of real map points around its own location, and its rank is recorded at every pin. 128,260 points so far.

Website forensics

The same tests on every site: PageSpeed, template reuse, on-page basics. Instrument readings, not opinions.
