3. Who arrives

Two kinds of people end up in a conversation with this desk.

A. Clients of a measured agency. The primary audience. Usually a fence-sitter: paying an agency named in a book, suspicious that the retainer is not producing, not yet sure enough to cancel. Their business is one of the 953 rows. Their file already contains their verdict and their finding lines; the booking popup on their listing page pre-checks their own findings. Every one of these conversations starts personalized, because the record already is (ammo: eyecarepro/every-listing-has-findings).

B. Agencies. The measured agencies themselves, or other agencies who have seen the hub. They are not a sales target and they are not treated as one. See Posture B.

4. The two conversation postures

Posture A: the client of a measured agency (the investigator posture)

You are not a pitch. You are the person who can pull their file. Rules:

  1. Read the file, do not perform it. Quote the book's own published lines. The headline sentences in section 2 are already public; quoting a published record beats asserting an opinion every time.
  2. Receipts before claims. The order is always: pull their listing page, read their finding lines, open their competitor report. Then talk.
  3. Concede what the file concedes. The ECP book shows 352 of 710 practices in the top 3 somewhere (ammo: eyecarepro/in-top3). The DB book names 13 winners (ammo: demandboost/winners-13). The RRH book names all 5 storefronts that rank (ammo: rankreallyhigh/pattern-the-only-five-found). Winners are called winners in the file, and their remaining findings still print (ammo: demandboost/winner-cards-still-flagged). A rep who hides the concessions breaks the desk's whole position.
  4. No adjectives the file does not earn. "Slow" is not a claim; "9,387 milliseconds median phone paint against Google's 2,500 millisecond bar" is (ammo: eyecarepro/median-mobile-lcp).
  5. Never disparage beyond the record. Everything negative we say is a measured, re-runnable number with a source. Nothing else gets said.
  6. The offer is always the same. Your file is free. It stays free. If you want it fixed, book a consultation with the consultants. Nothing else is on the menu.

Posture B: an agency (the registrar posture)

When a measured agency, or anyone speaking for one, contacts the desk:

  1. The file is not negotiable and not for sale. Say the anchor verbatim: "Nobody pays to be excluded and nobody pays to be included". The files are free and nobody named in one can pay to change one.
  2. Disputes go to the data, not to a rep. Every number in every book carries an extraction command that re-runs against the source, and every scanned business links a third-party scan report. A rep's job is to point at the receipt, not to argue.
  3. Do not pitch them. Do not counsel them. No selling SGEN to a measured agency, no advice on how to look better in the next scan. Either reads as the record being for sale.
  4. Escalate. Anything past "here is the receipt" goes to the owner. Reps do not speak for the desk on record contents, corrections, or legal noise.
5. The consult flow: from arrival to close

The mechanics that exist today, and only those. What exists: the hub, the three books, 953 per-listing pages, per-listing competitor-report links, and a live booking popup on the hub and books with a requested-time grid. What does not exist yet: the real domain (nobodyissafe.com is dark; DNS and hosting are owner-owed), the form delivery endpoint, the scheduler URL, the desk email inbox, and any sending infrastructure (ammo: rankreallyhigh/funnel.honesty, demandboost/funnel.honesty). The live surface is the GitHub Pages preview tree. Scripts and reps must never promise mechanics off this list.

Step 1: Arrival. Today there is no inbound pipe: the booking form's endpoint is owner-owed and may deliver nowhere. So a conversation starts one of two ways: the rep initiates it, or a prospect who saw a shared link reaches out through a channel the owner personally provided. Treat every arrival as warm: their file already exists.

Step 2: Pull the row, live. Find the prospect's business in the hub finder or go straight to /l/<agency>/<domain>.html. This happens in front of them or on the call, not before, whenever possible. Watching their own name come up with findings attached is the moment the desk stops being a stranger. The training bar is any listing in under 60 seconds (see sales/TRAINING.md).

Step 3: Read the file together. Verdict word first, in plain speech (translation tables are in sales/EDUCATION.md). Then their finding lines, verbatim off the card. Then open their competitor report link: a third-party page, not ours, showing who actually holds their map.

Step 4: The offer. One sentence: the file is free, and if they want the problems in it fixed, the next step is a consultation with James's consultants. Use the booking popup on their listing page and let them pick a requested time. Honesty rule: until the owner wires the endpoint, a popup submission may not deliver anywhere, so the rep records the requested time in the ledger (section 7) and personally carries the booking to the consultants. Make no promise a human will not personally keep. Never promise an email sequence, a reminder flow, or "the system will follow up," because no such system exists.

Step 5: The consult. Held by James's consultants. The rep's product is a held consult: right person, their file already read, their receipt links already in hand. Consultants own everything from there, including scope and price.

Step 6: The close. A close is a signed engagement with James's consultants or SGEN. Not a nice conversation, not a booked slot, not a cancelled competitor contract. Signed engagement. Everything before that is pipeline.

Not ready? Fence-sitters who will not book yet get armed instead of chased: walk them through the questions in sales/agencies/<slug>/fence-sitter-questions.md, the ones their incumbent will struggle to answer, and leave them their own listing link. The file does the waiting. No drip exists, so do not imply one; if the rep intends to check back personally, say when, then do it.

6. The pricing conversation

The posture is simple because the truth is simple:

  • The file is free. Every file, every listing, every competitor report link. Forever. That is the anchor and it is non-negotiable: "Nobody pays to be excluded and nobody pays to be included".
  • Nothing about the record is for sale. Not removal, not softening, not a rescan on a friendlier phrase. A rep who hints otherwise has ended their usefulness to this desk.
  • The consult is the product. The prospect's decision is whether to put their file in front of people who fix what it describes.
  • Reps quote no prices. No engagement price list exists in this repo, and inventing one violates the truth rule. Scope and price belong to the consultants at the consult. The honest answer to "what does it cost" is: "The file is free. What fixing it costs depends on what your file says, and that is exactly what the consultation is for."

9. What can sink this desk

Four failure modes, all rep-controllable:

  1. An invented number. One unverifiable stat in one call gives every measured agency the rebuttal they want. If it is not in an ammo sheet, it is not said.
  2. A paid-file rumor. Any behavior that lets anyone claim the record is purchasable. The anchor is the defense; behave so it stays true.
  3. A promised mechanism that does not exist. "You'll get a confirmation email" when no endpoint is wired. Every broken micro-promise spends the desk's only asset, which is that its claims check out.
  4. PII leakage. No client emails, no phone numbers, no owner personal names in any conversation or deliverable. Business names and public measured results only. The insight CSVs contain contact columns; they are off-limits to sales work (ammo: demandboost/laws.pii).

1. The battleground: the map pack, not the blue links

When someone types a near-me phrase into Google on a phone, the result that matters is the map block at the top: a map plus three named businesses. The DemandBoost book puts it plainly in its own copy: the map pack, what a patient actually sees, is positions 1 to 3 (ammo: demandboost/median-position, rendered book copy). Position 4 might as well be position 40. That is why the books measure map rank, not organic rank: for a local buyer with intent, the pack is the storefront window and organic is the back shelf.

Two facts make this the right fight:

First, the near-me and plain-language phrases are where the demand is. Google Trends, US, past 12 months, 53 weekly points per pair (source file: Marketing/nobodyissafe-hub/trends-data.json):

2. How a geo-grid scan works

A single Google search happens from a single place, and local results change block by block. So a grid scan does the honest version of "where do we rank": it lays a grid of pins over the business's own market and runs the same search at every pin.

The instrument, as used in all three books:

  • 121 pins per grid, an 11 by 11 lattice centered on the business. At each pin, the search phrase is run as if standing at that spot, and the business's map rank at that spot is recorded. 121 pins is the constant across every scan in every book (ammo: eyecarepro/pins-per-grid, demandboost/grid-methodology).
  • One phrase per book, no shopping. Every ECP grid ran "glasses near me" and only that; scans on other phrases exist on disk and were excluded from the book so nothing is a phrase-shopped best case (ammo: eyecarepro/one-phrase). Every DB grid ran "chiropractor near me" (ammo: demandboost/grid-methodology). Every RRH grid ran "weed near me", with a documented second pass on "dispensary near me" over the same pins (ammo: rankreallyhigh/flip-same-place-ids).
  • Radius calibrated per market, and printed. A rural practice is not scanned like a downtown one. ECP: 139 grids at 1 mile, 295 at 2, 226 at 5, 47 at 10, 3 at 15 (ammo: eyecarepro/grid-radius-calibration). RRH: 31 at 1 mile, 50 at 2, 119 at 5, 54 at 10, 1 at 15 (ammo: rankreallyhigh/scan-radii-market-sized). 3. The two axes: map verdict and product verdict

Each book scores two independent things. The map verdict comes only from grid pins. The product verdict comes only from website checks. The ECP book states no input is shared between the two (ammo: eyecarepro/vocabulary note), which is why a map winner can run a BROKEN site and a perfect site can be invisible. A rep who conflates the axes will be taken apart by the first sharp prospect.

Map verdict vocabularies (they differ per book, learn all three):

  • ECP: NOWHERE (never in the pack, no reach), POCKET_NO_REACH, PACK_WITH_TURF, PACK_NEVER_FIRST (ammo: eyecarepro/verdict-distribution)
  • DB: WINNER (50%+ share), PARTIAL, INVISIBLE (0% share), NOT_MEASURED (ammo: demandboost/segment-split)
  • RRH: NOWHERE, PACK_WITH_TURF, NOT_MEASURED on the hub rows (ammo: rankreallyhigh/hub-rows-146-of-953)

Product verdict bands, same ladder in every book: SOUND, WEAK, FAILING, BROKEN, driven by six checks:

  1. Shared template (is the site one of a mass-produced family)
  2. Majority-recycled copy (are its sentences on other clients' sites)
  3. llms.txt present (the check measures presence of the file, nothing more)
  4. Mobile PageSpeed above the 50 bar
  5. Phone LCP inside Google's 2.5 second bar
  6. LocalBusiness schema present

(Check set and per-check fail counts: ammo: eyecarepro/zero-clean-sites, fail-count-distribution; rate blocks in all three ammo sheets.)

6. Glossary
  • Pin: one simulated search location on a grid; each grid has 121 pins in an 11 by 11 lattice.
  • Grid: the full 121-pin lattice centered on a business, sized by a per-market radius (1 to 15 miles, printed in the book).
  • Map pack: the three named businesses shown with the map at the top of a local search result; positions 1 to 3.
  • Found pins: pins where the business appears at any rank; 0 found pins = invisible on its own map.
  • Turf / rank-1 pins: pins where the business is the #1 result.
  • SOLV / share: percentage of pins where the business is in the top 3; DB's WINNER bar is 50%+.
  • Average rank position: mean map rank across pins where found.
  • Grid leader: the business holding the top spot on a grid; someone always leads.
  • Verdict: the typed judgment per business, one per axis (map, product); vocabularies differ per book.
  • Finding line: one typed, countable problem statement on a hub listing card.
  • Competitor report / scan report: the third-party localrankingtracker.com pages every scanned business links to.
  • LCP: Largest Contentful Paint; Google's phone bar is 2.5 seconds.
  • Mobile PSI: Google PageSpeed Insights mobile performance score, out of 100; under 50 is the book's failing bar.
  • llms.txt: a proposed standard for a file describing a site to AI answer engines; no major engine has committed to reading it. The book's check measures presence or absence of the file, nothing more, and reps must not claim absence makes a site invisible to AI answers.
  • LocalBusiness schema: markup that tells Google a site is a local business; a table-stakes local-SEO item.
  • Template family: a group of sites sharing one design skeleton; membership is a measured fingerprint, not an opinion.
  • The smaller door: RRH-book shorthand for optimizing the lower-demand phrase (ammo: rankreallyhigh/hub-card-smaller-door).
