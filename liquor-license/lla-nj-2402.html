A Type 33 plenary retail consumption licence issued by the City of Jersey City, in Hudson County. Licence only — no premises, no fixtures, no goodwill. What transfers is the privilege: on-premise consumption of beer, wine and distilled spirits, with packaged sales confined to the principal public barroom (N.J.S.A. 33:1-12(1)).

Jersey City is the largest municipality on this book, and the cap still binds it — one consumption licence per 3,000 residents under N.J.S.A. 33:1-12.14. What that buys you here is a licence that can be moved place-to-place across a very large city, from Journal Square to the Heights to Bergen-Lafayette to the waterfront, on a single governing-body approval and without ever leaving the municipality. That mobility is worth considerably more in Jersey City than the same wording is worth in a two-square-mile borough, and it is the strongest argument for this particular licence.

Asked at $299,000, inside a $275,000–$320,000 band set on 10 August 2026. Against the measured statewide picture that sits just above the $255,000 median ask and well under the $440,000 upper quartile — the middle of the New Jersey market, in the densest county in the state. Our own Hudson file runs to 24 closed transfers with a $292,000 median on the consumption class; the table below shows a Jersey City licence that settled at $248,000 and a Hoboken one at $292,000.

Suited to a first location or a second: a bar, a tavern or a restaurant with a full bar, in a city where the rent decides the site and the licence decides whether you can open at all. Jersey City hears transfers on the second and fourth Wednesday, and nothing has been refused in Jersey City since 2020, though two files were adjourned for re-notice — which is the ordinary cost of getting the publication wrong rather than a sign of a hostile council.

Process

What happens next, and how long each part takes

Day counts are medians from our own 168 closed files. They add up to 94 days, which is also the median time from accepted offer to a licence issued in the buyer's name.

  1. Offer accepted, contract signed7 days

    Deposit of 10% into escrow — $29,900 at this asking price. Contract is conditional on municipal approval; if the town refuses, the deposit comes back in full.

  2. Buyer qualification14 days

    Proof of funds and a completed ABC personal history. This is also the point the mutual NDA is signed and the full confidential file is released to you.

  3. Application filed21 days

    ABC-Form-12A to the municipality, with the transfer papers and the tax clearance request. Clearance itself typically takes 21 days and runs in parallel.

  4. Public notice published14 days

    Notice in the newspaper of record under N.J.A.C. 13:2-7.1. This is the window in which objections can be filed.

  5. Municipal hearing30 days

    Jersey City's governing body hears transfers on the second and fourth Wednesday. The next filing deadline is 7 October 2026. Your counsel attends; you usually do too.

  6. Licence issued in your name8 days

    Resolution adopted, state paperwork completed, escrow released to the seller. You can trade.
