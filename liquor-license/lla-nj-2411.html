A Type 33 plenary retail consumption licence issued by the City of Hackensack, in Bergen County. It is offered as licence only: no premises, no fixtures, no goodwill. What transfers is the privilege itself — on-premise consumption of beer, wine and distilled spirits, with packaged sales confined to the principal public barroom (N.J.S.A. 33:1-12(1)).

It moves place-to-place anyplace inside Hackensack with the governing body's approval. It cannot cross the city line unless both municipalities consent and the receiving town sits under its cap. That cap — one consumption licence per 3,000 residents, N.J.S.A. 33:1-12.14 — is the whole reason a Bergen consumption licence trades where it does. About two thirds of New Jersey municipalities are already at it, so no new ones are issued and buyers compete for the licences that exist.

Asked at $440,000, inside a $400,000–$475,000 band set on 14 August 2026 and unchanged since. For context: across the 26 Bergen County licences listed publicly the median ask is $370,000, in a county range of $75,000 to $1,980,000. In our own closed book, 31 Bergen transfers between 2019 and 2026 settled at a $341,000 median — 7.8% below the asking median.

Suited to an operator taking over or opening a bar, tavern or restaurant with a full bar inside Hackensack. The city's governing body hears transfers on the second and fourth Tuesday, and no Hackensack transfer has been refused since 2019.

Process

What happens next, and how long each part takes

Day counts are medians from our own 168 closed files. They add up to 94 days, which is also the median time from accepted offer to a licence issued in the buyer's name.

  1. Offer accepted, contract signed7 days

    Deposit of 10% into escrow — $44,000 at this asking price. Contract is conditional on municipal approval; if the town refuses, the deposit comes back in full.

  2. Buyer qualification14 days

    Proof of funds and a completed ABC personal history. This is also the point the mutual NDA is signed and the full confidential file is released to you.

  3. Application filed21 days

    ABC-Form-12A to the municipality, with the transfer papers and the tax clearance request. Clearance itself typically takes 21 days and runs in parallel.

  4. Public notice published14 days

    Notice in the newspaper of record under N.J.A.C. 13:2-7.1. This is the window in which objections can be filed.

  5. Municipal hearing30 days

    Hackensack's governing body hears transfers on the second and fourth Tuesday. The next filing deadline is 3 October 2026. Your counsel attends; you usually do too.

  6. Licence issued in your name8 days

    Resolution adopted, state paperwork completed, escrow released to the seller. You can trade.
