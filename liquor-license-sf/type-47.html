A Type 47 is an on-sale general licence issued to a bona fide public eating place. It permits beer, wine and distilled spirits to be poured for drinking on the premises — a full bar, a proper cocktail list, wine by the glass, all of it. What makes it a Type 47 rather than a Type 48 is the kitchen: the room has to serve real meals, prepared on site, with the equipment and the seating to back that up.

That obligation is not a formality and it does not lapse quietly. If the food operation stops, or shrinks to a warming cabinet and a bowl of olives, the licence itself is exposed. Buyers who intend to run a bar with token food should be looking at a Type 48 instead, and paying attention to what that costs them in under-21 trade.

There are 18 Type 47 licences on our San Francisco book, asking a median of $232,500 inside a $135,000 to $350,000 range. The spread is not a quality ladder. What moves a Type 47 price here is how clean the file is, whether the seller is under pressure, and whether the licence carries conditions — petition conditions restricting hours, entertainment or off-sale privileges attach to individual licences in San Francisco and travel with them to the buyer. We read the conditions before we list, and you see them the day you qualify.

San Francisco is over the county ceiling for on-sale general licences, so the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control issues no new Type 47 originals here. Every one of these is somebody giving one up. That is the whole reason the class costs six figures in this city and four figures in a county with headroom, and it is why the sensible question is not “can I get the price down” but “is this the right licence for the room I am building”.

What a Type 47 lets you do

Food obligationYes. California ties this licence to a bona fide public eating place: meals prepared and served on the premises, with the kitchen and seating to support them. It is a continuing obligation, not a condition satisfied once at application.
MinorsPermitted on the premises. This is the practical difference from a Type 48, and for a family restaurant or an all-ages room it is the whole argument.

Process

What happens next, and how long each part takes

Day counts are medians from our 64 closed California files and add to 78 days, which is also the median from accepted offer to a licence issued in your name. Thirty of them are a statutory posting period nobody can shorten.

  1. Offer accepted, escrow opened7 days

    A purchase agreement, then escrow with a licensed escrow holder. California requires the full consideration to sit in escrow before the Department will approve the transfer, so this is the point your money moves, not completion day.

  2. Buyer qualification and application10 days

    The person-to-person transfer application, Live Scan fingerprinting for every principal, and proof of funds. This is also when the mutual NDA is signed and the confidential file — licence number, licensee of record, premises address, term, encumbrances, tax position — is released to you.

  3. Public notice posted at the premises30 days

    ABC posts the application at the premises for 30 days, and the notice of intended transfer is published in a newspaper of general circulation. The 30 days are statutory and cannot be shortened, whatever anyone promises you.

  4. Protest period and local agency review14 days

    Any protest filed during the posting is worked through here, and this is where San Francisco can add time: the Police Department’s alcohol liaison reviews on-sale applications, and a Planning conditional use, if your district needs one, runs on its own much longer calendar.

  5. ABC investigation and approval10 days

    The investigator confirms the premises, the qualification of the buyer and the tax position of the seller. Clean files clear here without anyone hearing from anyone.

  6. Escrow closes, licence issued in your name7 days

    Approval issued, escrow released to the seller, licence recorded against you. You can trade.

Released when you qualify

The licence number and the address are not on this page

Not masked, not blurred, not shown in part. They are simply not published, for this class and every other on this site — and neither is any photograph of a licensed premises. A seller is almost always still trading. In a city where a neighbourhood and a licence class narrow a business to a handful of doors, publishing either one tells their staff, their landlord and the operator across the street that they are selling. So the shop window carries everything you need to shortlist, and nothing that identifies the seller.
