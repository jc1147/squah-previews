A Type 20 is an off-sale beer and wine licence. It permits beer and wine to be sold in sealed containers for consumption away from the premises: a corner shop, a bottle shop without spirits, a grocery, a deli with a fridge and a wine rack. No distilled spirits, and nothing opened on site.

Here is the part that matters more than anything else on this page. The Type 20 is not quota-limited. Unlike a Type 21, a Type 47 or a Type 48, the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control will issue a new Type 20 original to a qualified applicant at an approved premises. There is no fixed pool and nothing to bid for. Which means the sensible default in San Francisco is to apply for one rather than buy one.

So why is there one on this book at $15,000, and why would anyone pay it? Two reasons, both about time rather than scarcity. An existing licence is already attached to an approved use at an approved address, so the buyer of a going concern is buying certainty about the location as much as the licence. And an application runs its own 30-day posting and its own local review, which for a shop fitted out and waiting to open is a month of rent against a fixed fee. Beyond that there is no premium here, and $15,000 against a $232,500 Type 47 is the clearest possible illustration of what the quota is actually pricing.

The one thing to check before either route is the address. Parts of San Francisco restrict new off-sale alcohol uses under the Planning Code regardless of class, so a Type 20 at the wrong address can be harder than a Type 21 at the right one. That question is answered by the Planning district, not by ABC, and it is the first thing we will ask you.

The band

What a San Francisco Type 20 costs

There is one Type 20 on the San Francisco book and it asks $15,000. On a class that is not quota-limited there is no scarcity premium to quote, so the price is doing something different from every other price on this site.

What you are buying is time, not supply. A new Type 20 original costs the ABC application fee and roughly two to three months of waiting, including the statutory 30-day posting. For a fitted-out shop paying rent and not yet trading, buying a licence that is already attached to an approved use at an approved address can be cheaper than the wait.

What could make it worth less than the application. If your address is not yet approved for an off-sale alcohol use, buying an existing licence solves nothing — the Planning question follows the address, not the licence. In that situation the application route and the purchase route face the same obstacle, and only one of them costs $15,000.

And the comparison that explains this whole site. $15,000 for a class ABC will issue on application; $232,500 for a Type 47 it will not. Same city, same paperwork, same desk. The difference is the quota, and nothing else.

Process

Transferring a Type 20 in San Francisco

Day counts are medians from our 64 closed California files and add to 78 days, which is also the median from accepted offer to a licence issued in your name. Thirty of them are a statutory posting period nobody can shorten. A person-to-person transfer, where you take the licence at its existing address, is the quickest route; a premises-to-premises transfer adds a fresh posting at the new address and, in many districts, a Planning conditional use on its own calendar.

  1. Offer accepted, escrow opened7 days

    A purchase agreement, then escrow with a licensed escrow holder. California requires the full consideration to sit in escrow before the Department will approve the transfer, so this is the point your money moves, not completion day.

  2. Buyer qualification and application10 days

    The person-to-person transfer application, Live Scan fingerprinting for every principal, and proof of funds. This is also when the mutual NDA is signed and the confidential file — licence number, licensee of record, premises address, term, encumbrances, tax position — is released to you.

  3. Public notice posted at the premises30 days

    ABC posts the application at the premises for 30 days, and the notice of intended transfer is published in a newspaper of general circulation. The 30 days are statutory and cannot be shortened, whatever anyone promises you.

  4. Protest period and local agency review14 days

    Any protest filed during the posting is worked through here, and this is where San Francisco can add time: the Police Department’s alcohol liaison reviews on-sale applications, and a Planning conditional use, if your district needs one, runs on its own much longer calendar.

  5. ABC investigation and approval10 days

    The investigator confirms the premises, the qualification of the buyer and the tax position of the seller. Clean files clear here without anyone hearing from anyone.

  6. Escrow closes, licence issued in your name7 days

    Approval issued, escrow released to the seller, licence recorded against you. You can trade.

Stage day counts are the medians from our own closed California files and add to 7 + 10 + 30 + 14 + 10 + 7 = 78 days, which is also our median from accepted offer to issue. The 30-day posting is a statutory period; the rest is how San Francisco files actually run.

Questions

Type 20 questions we are actually asked

Should I buy a Type 20 or apply for a new one?

Apply, unless one of two things is true: the address you intend to trade from is already approved for an off-sale alcohol use and the licence comes with it, or you are paying rent on a fitted shop and the two-to-three month application timetable costs you more than the licence does. On every other set of facts the application is cheaper and we will tell you so. This is the one class where the honest answer usually costs us the commission.

How long does a new Type 20 application take?

Budget two to three months in a clean case: the application and qualification, then the statutory 30-day posting at the premises, then the investigation and issue. A protest or a Planning question about the address extends it, sometimes considerably. A purchase runs the same 78-day transfer timetable as every other class on this site, so the gap between the two routes is smaller than people expect.

Can I add spirits to a Type 20 later?

No. You would be buying a Type 21 on the open market, at whatever the quota is pricing when you do it — on this book, a $175,000 median. A licence class is not upgraded; it is replaced. If spirits are plausibly part of the plan within a couple of years, work out what the Type 21 costs now before you commit to the cheaper route.

Does my address matter more than the licence?

On this class, usually yes. Parts of San Francisco restrict new off-sale alcohol uses under the Planning Code whatever the licence class, and that question follows the address rather than the licence or the buyer. Send us the address before you decide anything else; it is the first thing we will ask for.

Is a Type 20 enough for a deli or a grocery?

For beer and wine, yes — sealed, taken away, not opened on the premises. It is the right licence for a corner shop, a bottle shop without spirits, a grocery or a deli with a fridge and a wine rack. If you want to sell spirits, or let anyone drink on site, it is not, and the class you need is a Type 21 or an on-sale licence respectively.
