1. Bottom line

  • The process rules are the strongest part of the prompt, and the evidence supports them. The prompt:
    • forbids fabricated quotes;
    • separates event, disclosure and first-tradable times, and checks how much of the move has already happened;
    • keeps three probabilities apart;
    • reprices options at the planned exit;
    • budgets for losing the full premium;
    • refuses to fill a quota;
    • demands forward validation.

    Our research found edges dying in exactly these places: the overnight gap, the spread, the IV crush and hindsight.

  • Most module edge assumptions conflict with post-2010 evidence. In recent data, these signals are close to zero even before costs (for equities: measured after public disclosure, ex-microcap):
    • linked-firm lead-lags;
    • earnings and revision "inflections";
    • 13F and congressional copying;
    • public option volume;
    • fixed on-chain cycle thresholds.

    Short-interest crowding goes further and predicts lower returns. What survives is small, fast, concentrated in small or less-covered names, or on the short side.

  • Buying options starts from a negative base rate, before any skill.
    • Holding an ATM straddle for one day across earnings averaged -7.96%. That figure is for the 50 most liquid optioned names, and the result was negative in every year from 2000 to 2015.
    • Monthly ATM single-stock straddles averaged -17.09% a month at midpoints (1996-2013).
    • Retail option traders lose money on average and pay average spreads of about 12.6%.
    • In our 2026-10-02 Cboe snapshot, a 30-DTE ATM call on a mid or small cap had a 13.7% median spread. It would lose about 20% of its value in 10 flat calendar days, and about 27% over 10 trading days (model).
  • No adequate evidence shows a general chat LLM's judgment beating market prices after costs.
    • Untuned frontier models were worse calibrated than market-implied probabilities, even with expert context. The best came close on Brier score (0.201 against 0.199) but not on calibration error. A system fine-tuned for that one task did beat the market (0.151), so the gap is about general chat models, not about LLMs as such (Jajal et al. 2026).
    • LLM trading-agent backtests collapse after the model's knowledge cutoff.
    • The few positive forward results are short and narrow: nine months of long-only stock picks (Chen & Pu), and a $200, 26-day Kalshi pilot (Gu et al.).
    • Every stated probability should therefore be anchored to its market-implied counterpart.
  • Measurability is the binding constraint. A 30-DTE call held 10 days has a per-trade standard deviation of about 100% of premium.
    • About 400 independent trades are needed just to reach t = 2 at a true +10% net edge per trade, which is a coin flip (about 50% power). About 800 are needed for 80% power. For +5%, the figures are about 1,600 and 3,100.
    • In bootstrap samples, a process with a true -4% edge showed a +10% average after 20 trades 25% of the time.
  • In this setup no options trade can pass the prompt's own gate. We have delayed chains only, no broker or real-time feed, and no configured scheduling or alerts. The framework can run when invoked, as research plus a shadow ledger, and should say so.
  • What to do:
    • Paste the addendum.
    • Run the framework as a paper-only discretionary sleeve, with zero capital until Gate B (section 6).
    • Where a module overlaps a verified systematic unit, let that unit's verdict govern:
      • only that unit's frozen rule may trade (U10, U11, U12, U13, U16, U18, U22, U26), and only after it passes its own pre-registered validation. U12 first needs its 2005-2015 survivorship-free test, and U13 is an entry-timing overlay only;
      • trade nothing where the idea was rejected (U23 as an alpha source, U25).

2. What the framework gets right

2.1 Process discipline and timing

The prompt runs one sequence from discovery to outcome review. It keeps asking "How much of the potential move has already happened?", and it records event time, disclosure time and first tradable opportunity separately. Our research found that this is where most edges disappear.

  • Activist 13Ds: in 2016-18 the whole move came in the overnight gap, which a trader cannot capture (+2.2%, t 4.99). Entering one session late earned +0.13% (t 0.39) (U12).
  • Insider purchases: after 2009 the day +1 abnormal return is +0.38% at price ≥ $5. The original monthly rule earns about zero (U11).
  • Linked stocks:
    • In aggregate, the daily correlation of big-stock returns with next-day small-stock returns was 0.31-0.37 before 2000 and -0.04 to -0.14 since (our computation, Ken French size portfolios; small means below the NYSE median, not microcap).
    • Together with the near-zero link signals (section 4A), this suggests related securities usually reprice quickly. Stock-level diffusion was not measured.
  • Congressional trades: studies measure senators' trades from the trade date. A copier can act only after a disclosure lag of up to 45 days (Belmont et al., NBER w26975).

2.2 Anti-fabrication and data discipline

The prompt forbids invented premiums, IV, borrow costs, positioning, consensus and dates. It also says delayed quotes cannot establish an executable debit. For an LLM this is essential:

  • GPT-4-Turbo with retrieval answered 81% of FinanceBench questions wrongly or refused (2023-era model; abstract level).
  • Prophet Arena lists "inaccurate event recalls" as a bottleneck.
  • LLMs reproduce exact pre-cutoff financial values from memory (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu).

Two specific rules are confirmed by primary sources:

  • Short volume is not short interest. FINRA's own page says its daily short-sale volume files "do not—and are not intended to—equate to" reported short interest.
  • Call volume is not dealer positioning. In GME (January 2021) the SEC staff "did not find evidence of a gamma squeeze". Options volume was mostly puts, and market makers were buying calls.

The rule against claiming monitoring, ledgers or alerts that do not exist also fits this setup, where none are configured (section 5).

2.3 Three separate probabilities

Keeping P(large move), P(direction) and the trade's expected return apart lets each be scored against its own baseline. Those baselines are not 50%:

  • For random S&P 500 entries in 2015-2026, P(up over 10 sessions) was 55% and P(|move| > 10%) was 9.6% (computed; survivorship-biased upward). A 55% bullish hit rate on large caps is the base rate, not skill.
  • Those large-cap figures are illustrations only. The framework's targets include smaller, event-driven and high-volatility names, where P(|move| > 10%) is far higher. Scoring should use base rates computed per name at decision time: P(|move| > threshold) from a lognormal with trailing 63-day realized volatility (a proposed window), and P(up) from the trailing universe up-rate over the same horizon.
  • Frontier LLMs rank outcomes better than they calibrate them (Jajal et al. 2026). Scoring calibration separately catches the most likely LLM error.

2.4 The option-economics gate

This is the most valuable single part of the prompt. Each of its requirements is supported:

  • Reprice at the planned exit, including IV changes.
    • A 30-DTE ATM call bought at 60% IV can lose money on a correct call. If the stock rises 5% in 10 trading days but IV falls to 40%, the call is down 5.6%.
    • With no move it loses about 27% to time decay alone over those 10 trading days, with 16 calendar days left (Black-Scholes, computed). Over 10 calendar days the loss is about 20% (section 4C).
  • Price the event explicitly.
    • A one-day ATM straddle held across the release averaged -7.96% (median -10.24%) in the 50 most liquid optioned names, and was negative in every year from 2000 to 2015 (Dubinsky et al. 2019). Gao, Xing & Zhang's smaller [0,+1] figure (-1.37%) uses announcement dates with no adjustment for before-open or after-close releases, so its window can miss the announcement.
    • Implied event-day volatility averaged 8.22% against 7.42% realized (Dubinsky et al. 2019). In 2011-2015, though, mean implied (6.78%) was below mean realized (7.35%). Only the standardized measure still showed a premium there: realized moves were about 0.91 of implied. Treat the straddle loss as the robust anchor, and any break-even built on the pooled means (section 4D) as period-dependent.
  • A midpoint is not a fill. Customer midpoint orders in two-tick SPXW 0DTE markets filled 50-62% of the time, at about half the cost of a marketable order (Fu, Li, Musto & Pearson 2025, SEC DERA). Wide single-stock markets were not studied.
  • Budget the full premium, because stops do not guarantee exits. We ran 12,000 random 10-day S&P 500 entries with a -7% stop (computed). Of the stop hits, 17% gapped through the stop at the open. Those gap-throughs filled at -9.0% on average, and the worst at -25%.
  • Compare against shares, spreads and no trade. On mid and small caps, the round-trip option spread per dollar of delta was a median 5-9 times the stock's own spread (Cboe snapshot).

2.5 No quotas

These rules are right when the average trade has negative expectancy: "Never fill a quota", "usually no more than 1-3", and PASS and DATA-BLOCKED as normal outputs. Each marginal trade adds spread and premium decay. For a human comparison: of about 360,000 Taiwanese day traders in an average year, about 1,000 (under 1%) predictably earned profits net of fees (Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean). About 13% earned net profits in a typical year, so one profitable year does not separate skill from luck.

The rule that "a rising price or repeated versions of the same signal are not independent confirmation" is also borne out. MVRV rank-correlates 0.93 with BTC's drawdown from its high, and 0.71-0.81 with the Mayer Multiple, price relative to the 200-week MA, and Puell (computed).

2.6 Forward validation and the shadow log

For an LLM-run process, the only valid designs are "never award hindsight credit" and "test proposed improvements prospectively in a shadow log":

  • An LLM's back-dated judgments cannot be separated from memorization (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu).
  • Reported trading-agent returns "evaporate once the model's knowledge window ends" (Profit Mirage).
  • A single-author benchmark, Look-Ahead-Bench, measured more than 15 percentage points of alpha decay between in-window and out-of-window periods.

The prompt is right that "a modest sample is an initial diagnostic". What it lacks are the numbers: baselines, sample sizes, gates and kill rules. The addendum and section 6 supply them.

  • The one link factor that lasted to 2015 is driven by its short side.   • "Inflection" signals are about zero ex-microcap in 2015-24:
    • earnings surprise -0.04%/mo;
    • revenue surprise 0.13%/mo;
    • analyst revisions 0.06%/mo;
    • REV6's top quintile beat the market by 0.16%/mo (t 0.5).

    Post-earnings drift has been absent outside microcaps since 2006 (Martineau 2022).

  • What remains is in small caps, on the short side, and decaying.
    • LLM headline signals fell from a Sharpe of 6.54 (2021Q4) to 1.22 (Jan-May 2024). They were unprofitable at a 20 bp round trip. Their short-only Sharpe was 3.17 in small caps against -0.61 in large caps (Lopez-Lira & Tang).
    • A live, web-searching LLM ranking earned 18.4 bp/day of alpha (t 2.46) on its top-20 Russell 1000 picks over nine months. It showed no skill on losers (Chen & Pu 2026).

Keep

  • The causal chain, and the question "how much has already happened".
  • Separate timestamps and the gap-consumption check.
  • Comparing shares, ETFs, options and no trade.
  • The link map as a risk tool for hidden exposures and correlated theses (feeds J).
  • Labeled inferences, and links taken from primary filings.

Change

  1. Make "already priced" the default for linked securities. A "not yet reflected" claim must show two things:
    • the linked security's move since first tradable time, set against its beta-implied move (from a rolling regression on the source security);
    • why the link is hard to see: a small or less-covered name, an indirect link, or a link disclosed only in a footnote.
  2. Require a dated forcing catalyst, such as the linked firm's own earnings date.
  3. Rank avoid, trim and hedge calls above long "second-order beneficiary" calls in liquid names. For a retail account, the short-side residual means trimming or a sector-hedged put, not shorting hard-to-borrow small caps.
  4. Back an inflection call with text-level or operating evidence the consensus visibly ignores. Score it against a mechanical earnings-surprise baseline.
  5. Build links only from dated filings retrieved at decision time, such as 10-K major-customer notes and 8-K material contracts. Never build them from model memory.
  6. Prefer shares or ETFs for multi-week link theses. Monthly ATM straddles lose 17.09% a month at mid (GXZ).

B. Catalyst Opportunity Watch

Evidence

  • Holding long premium through the event loses. The test: in the 50 most liquid optioned names, 2000-2015, buy an ATM straddle at the close before earnings and sell it at the next close (Dubinsky, Johannes, Kaeck & Seeger 2019 RFS, full text).
    • Mean return -7.96% (median -10.24%, SD 27.5%).
    • Negative in all 16 years.
    • Event-day ATM spreads ran 5.4-7.7% of mid.
  • The pre-event run-up is real but smaller than today's costs (GXZ, 1996-2013, midpoints).
    • Pooled ATM IV rose from 0.467 at day -30 to 0.514 at day -1, then fell to 0.464 at day +1.
    • A straddle held over days [-3,-1] earned +1.37% in liquid names (dollar-OI-weighted, t 3.81). Held to day +1, the return was -0.67%.
    • Today's quoted round trip in liquid names is about 1.5-4.5% at fills of mid ± 25% of the spread (0.5 × the quoted spread). That comes from a single 2026-10-02 post-close snapshot of four event straddles (ORCL, JPM, NKE, FDX). It excludes FDX, which implied 8-17%, and JPM's widest quote implies about 5.5% (U13).
    • No replication after 2013 exists.
  • The stock run-up is a timing tilt, not a trade (U13; survivorship bias inflates it).
    • The earnings-announcement premium in S&P 500 names, 2015-2026, is +0.15% per event against SPY.
    • As a hedged book it nets 2.4-3.3% a year (t about 1).
    • It lost 11.4% in 2018 and 13.3% in 2025.
  • Lockup expiries averaged -1.8% (Field & Hanka 2001, 3,217 lockups) and -1.2% (1,948 IPOs), more for VC-backed firms. No evidence after 2015 was found.
  • The pre-FOMC drift has decayed (U02).

Keep

  • The rolling calendar, the 10/5/2/1-day checkpoints, and the weekend look-ahead.
  • The explicit split between the run-up trade and holding through. The evidence gives the two opposite signs.
  • Separate event-time records.

Change

  1. Default to PASS on holding long options or straddles through a scheduled release. Override only when a stated, sourced expected move exceeds the implied move.
  2. Compute the implied event move for every option that spans an event, in two ways:
    • the ATM straddle mid divided by spot;
    • Dubinsky's term-structure estimate, sigma_j^2 = (IV_T1^2 - IV_T2^2)/(1/T1 - 1/T2).

    Compare both with the stock's last 8 or more realized event moves (EDGAR 8-K Item 2.02 dates).

  3. Option run-up trades are forward-test only, and only where the event straddle's spread is at most about 3% of mid.
  4. Use the stock run-up only to time purchases justified on other grounds. It is never the "why now".
  5. Regulatory, clinical, litigation and M&A events have no verified base rate in our research beyond U18 (merger arbitrage) and section F. Say so, and size accordingly.

C. Pre-Repricing Inflection Radar

Evidence

  • Bought options lose before any skill:
    • Delta-hedged ATM calls returned -0.81% a month on the hedged position (Cao & Han, 1996-2009). At 40% IV we convert that to about -6% of premium a month.
    • High-skew options earn 10-50% a week less (Boyer & Vorkink 2014).
    • OTM single-stock calls had negative average returns in 1996-2005 (Ni).
    • Retail option traders lose on average at 12.6% average spreads (Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya 2023). Much of the loss is the spread itself (Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, abstract).
  • Neither bottom-picking nor confirmation has a tailwind on its own:
    • Past-loser stocks averaged -2.5% a year in excess return over 1927-2013 (Daniel & Moskowitz).
    • From 2006 to Aug 2026, short-term reversal earned -0.06%/mo (t -0.31), long-term reversal -0.08%/mo (t -0.41) and momentum +0.08%/mo (t 0.28) (our computation, French library).
    • Proximity to the 52-week high earned 0.16%/mo (t 0.48) in 2006-24.
    • The big loser rebounds came in panics, when IV is dearest. Losers rose 163% in Mar-May 2009.
  • Costs today (Cboe, 23 underlyings, close of 2026-10-02; value lost is modeled):

Keep

  • "A low price alone does not establish value."
  • "Prioritize remaining opportunity relative to downside and time decay."
  • The EARLY / CONFIRMATION / LATE labels as a journal field. Investors buy more calls after high past returns (Lakonishok, Lee & Poteshman), so the LATE label catches a real bias.
  • The full option-economics gate.

Change

  1. Shares by default for EARLY INFLECTION. Allow an option only with a dated repricing window and a modeled move above the half-life break-even computed from live quotes.
  2. Theta budget: reject the contract if a flat stock through the expected window would cost more than about 25-30% of premium.
  3. No options under 21 DTE or more than 10% OTM in this module.
  4. Spread gate: ATM spread at most about 5% of mid, or 10% for small caps with a mid-price limit.
  5. High IV: above about 1.3 times the name's own one-year median IV, prefer debit spreads or shares.
  6. Puts near tops need a dated deterioration catalyst, and go through D.
  7. Freeze measurable label rules. For example, EARLY = a bottom-quintile 12-1 month return or a price within 10% of the 52-week low, with one-month strength against the sector ETF turning positive. These thresholds are untested proposals.

D. Pre-Event Put Watch

Evidence

  • No study tests this trade directly, and every component points against it.
    • Accruals hedge returns are no longer reliably positive (Green, Hand & Soliman 2011). Our recomputation gives 0.01%/mo for 2006-24.
    • Across 162 anomalies (2006-2020), long-short returns are 0.15%/mo before borrow fees and -0.02% after. The short leg drives them (Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet 2023).
    • Options on high-issuance, high-idiosyncratic-volatility names are the most overpriced (Cao, Han, Tong & Zhan).
    • Lower accounting quality comes with higher pre-announcement IV (SSRN 2509969, abstract).
    • Put prices embed the stock's borrow fee, so a put does not get around short-sale costs.
  • Break-even on one release (our normal model on Dubinsky's inputs: 8.22% implied move, 7.42% realized).
    • At zero drift, an ATM put held over one release returns about -10% gross, and about -15% after a 6% round-trip spread.
    • To break even it needs an expected abnormal event-day move of about -0.6% gross, or -1.0% after costs.
    • These inputs are pooled 2000-2015 means. In 2011-2015 mean implied (6.78%) was below mean realized (7.35%), so the break-even is period-dependent; the one-day straddle loss (section 4B) is the more robust anchor.
  • Slow deterioration signals work too slowly for short options. Text-change signals such as Lazy Prices leak into prices over 6-18 months.
  • Index puts are a fair benchmark. Their traded returns cumulated to about zero from March 2009 to December 2022 (Dew-Becker & Giglio).

Keep

  • "'Expensive company' is insufficient."
  • The requirement for a reason expectations fall within the trade's life.

F. Clinical and Regulatory Event Analysis

Evidence

  • Base rates are well measured, and the market knows them:
    • Phase III to NDA/BLA: 57.8% (oncology 47.5%). 90.6% of submissions are eventually approved (BIO/Informa/QLS, 2011-2020).
    • New molecular entities: 50% (151 of 302) were approved on the first cycle in 2000-2012, and 73.5% eventually (JAMA 2014).
    • Advisory votes: the FDA approved after 97% of positive votes, but declined after only 67% of negative ones (2010-2021).
    • Goal dates: the FDA acted on time for 93-95% of original NME/BLA applications in FY2024. Acting includes issuing a CRL.
  • Prices absorb outcomes on day 0-1. Across 13,807 trial outcomes (2000-2020), abnormal returns on day +2 were not significant (Singh et al. 2022). LLM scoring of trial headlines captured -11.4 bp of drift, which is also not significant.
  • Informed flow arrives before the event. Abnormal option activity before FDA advisory meetings and announcements predicts the event return (JCF 2024, JBFA 2022; abstracts).
  • No peer-reviewed study shows outside analysts beating market-implied probabilities. The one supportive source is an unrefereed 2026 SSRN abstract that gives no numbers.
  • The rare-event math is right under its stated assumptions (our computation).
    • The 95% upper bound after zero events, 1-0.05^(1/n), is close to 3/n for n of about 30 or more.
    • If p varies across trials or populations, zero events is weaker evidence than it looks. With mean p = 1/1000 and n = 1000, the chance of seeing at least one event falls from 0.632 to 0.356.

Keep

  • The rare-event reasoning and its warnings.
  • Comparing the next trial's population, endpoints, exposure duration and size with the prior evidence.
  • Separating statistical, clinical, regulatory and commercial significance.

Change

  1. Start from a reference-class prior, and name its source and definition. BIO and Wong-Siah-Lo differ by about 2x on Phase II.
  2. Extract the market's probability before claiming a gap: p_m = D/(U+D), where U and D are pre-specified success and failure moves. Each point of probability edge is worth about (U+D)/100 of the stock price.
  3. Downgrade the call when unusual option activity runs against the thesis.
  4. Assume no drift after day 1.
  5. Default to PASS on long premium through a binary event, unless the probability gap clears the spread, the IV crush and a full-loss case.
  6. Run F mainly as a Brier-scored forecasting log. In our simulation, an analyst whose errors have SD 0.10 against the market's 0.15 is detected only 19% of the time at 100 events and 39% at 400.

Other row types:

  • paper_fill: the fill rule used, filled or not, price, snapshot hash, fees. The Cboe JSON has one snapshot-level timestamp and no time per quote, its quotes are delayed, and many snapshots are taken after the close. A snapshot fetched after the decision can therefore hold quotes older than the news the decision used. So every fill, exit and control fill uses an eligible snapshot only: its timestamp falls in regular hours (09:45-15:45 ET) and is at least 20 minutes after decided_at_utc. The 20 minutes are the quote delay plus a margin; Cboe's exact delay, and the time zone of its timestamp field, are unverified and must be confirmed before Gate A. Snapshots taken outside regular hours never fill or exit. A decision made outside market hours fills no earlier than the next session's 09:45 ET snapshot. There are two fill rules:
    • Primary: buy at the ask of the first eligible snapshot. Fill only if that ask is at or below the no-chase ceiling and the ask size covers the order.
    • Secondary: fill at mid + 25% of the spread, only if a later eligible snapshot shows an ask at or below that limit.
  • mark: daily bid, ask, mid, IV and underlying price, from the scheduled snapshots (section 5).
  • exit: exit at the bid of the first eligible snapshot after the exit trigger.
  • outcome:
    • net P&L after fees;
    • underlying returns at 1, 3 and 5 sessions and at the intended horizon;
    • whether the large move occurred and whether the direction was right;
    • realized cost against modeled cost.

Fees come from the broker's published schedule. U13 records tastytrade at $1 per contract to open, $0 to close, and $0.10 per contract clearing.

6.3 Scoring, benchmarks and controls

  • Probabilities.
    • Compute Brier and log scores (probabilities clipped to 0.01-0.99). Report each as skill against the market-implied probability and against the base rate.
    • Add a calibration table. About 100 forecasts per bin give ±0.10 precision.
    • Require skill against both baselines. Risk-neutral probabilities carry a variance premium, so "always lower than the market" is not skill.
  • EV. Regress realized net return on predicted EV; the slope should be positive.
  • P&L. Report at natural fills (primary) and limit fills (secondary): per trade, as an equity curve under the frozen sizing, and as hit rate, payoff distribution, loss frequency, maximum drawdown, MFE/MAE and the 1-, 3- and 5-session diagnostics.
  • Benchmarks.
    • T-bills.
    • SPY and QQQ, including a beta-matched SPY position equal to the entry delta-dollars.
    • The sector ETF.
    • For crypto, BTC buy-and-hold and monthly DCA.
  • Random-entry controls. For every trade, a script draws 20 controls at decision time:
    • (a) Random-ticker control: same timestamp; random tickers from the same filtered universe (optionable, price ≥ $5, no wider spread bucket); same structure (type, delta within ±0.05, DTE within ±7); same fill and exit rules.
    • (b) Timing control: the same trade entered 5 sessions later.
    • (c) Index control: the same structure on SPY.

    Control fills and exits follow the same eligible-snapshot rule as the trade (section 6.2).

    The headline statistic is d = trade return minus the mean of controls (a), tested with week-clustered errors or a block bootstrap.

  • Module controls.
    • A/E: a random same-sector, same-size peer, and a mechanical basket of suppliers and customers of firms with top or bottom 5% daily moves.
    • B/D: two random liquid names reporting in the same week, with the same tenor and moneyness.
    • G: three non-flagged names matched on SI decile, fee bucket, size and 20-day return.

6.4 Required sample size

That sample reaches t = 2 only if the realized edge equals the true edge, so a true edge of that size fails about half the time (about 50% power). The 80%-power column uses n = ((1.96 + 0.84) × SD / edge)^2, two-sided 5%, which is about twice as many.

Correlation shrinks the effective sample. k concurrent positions at correlation ρ count as k/(1+(k-1)ρ) bets. At ρ = 0.33, five positions count as 2.2 bets and ten as 2.5.

At a realistic pace, proof takes years. Suppose the framework produces about 100 trades a year (an assumption). Then only an edge near +20% a trade can reach t = 2 within a year, and even then only half the time; 80% power takes about two years. A forward test can catch a clearly broken process: under the kill rule in section 6.6, a process that truly loses 20% of premium a trade is stopped at 50 trades about 75% of the time, and one losing 10% about half the time. Gate B screens less well (section 6.5). The test cannot prove a modest edge quickly.

6.5 Go-live gates

  • Gate A, implementation (at least 3 months and 50 decision rows):
    • every row complete;
    • no mismatch between ledger quotes and snapshot hashes;
    • no contract missing from its snapshot;
    • every decision timestamp earlier than its outcome data;
    • every fill and exit snapshot (trades and controls) later than decided_at_utc plus the quote delay, and inside regular hours;
    • a scorer that reproduces its results exactly.

    Passing A permits only continued paper testing.

  • Gate B, paper economics (at least 100 closed trades and 6 months):
    • mean d ≥ 0;
    • realized cost ≤ 1.5 times modeled;
    • Brier skill for large moves ≥ 0 against the market-implied probability;
    • direction hit rate ≥ base rate;
    • calibration slope between 0.5 and 1.5.

    Passing B permits tiny live trading: at most 0.5% of risk capital in premium per trade, 3% in aggregate, and 10% of the sleeve's intended allocation, with a manual broker quote check on every order.

    Gate B is a sanity screen, not evidence of edge. At a per-trade SD of about 100% of premium, the SE of mean d at 100 trades is about 10%. A process that truly loses 4% of premium per trade then passes the mean-d condition about 35% of the time, and one that loses 10% about 16% of the time. The hit-rate and Brier conditions are similarly noisy at this sample size.

  • Gate C, scale (at least 400 closed trades):
    • d > 0 with clustered t ≥ 2;
    • the result still holds at twice the costs;
    • a Brier-skill confidence interval that excludes zero.

    Passing C permits quarter-Kelly sizing on half the measured edge, still capped at 2% per trade.

  • Multiple comparisons. Results are broken out across about 11 modules, directions, lead types and structures, so some slice will pass t ≥ 2 by chance. Module- and type-level promotions therefore use Holm-Bonferroni across all pre-registered slices (family alpha 0.05). Each rule version adds to the trial count N, and the t-hurdle rises with it: for 12 slices the strictest step needs about t > 2.9 (two-sided).
  • Module-level rules (from the module evaluations). A module that fails its own rule drops to research-only, even if the whole book passes. Each promotion threshold below is subject to the Holm-Bonferroni hurdle above.
    • A/E: after 100 calls, research-only unless the net abnormal return beats the random peer at t > 2 and beats the mechanical-link basket.
    • B and D, long premium through events and pre-event puts:
      • Kill after 50 calls if the mean net return is ≤ -8% or does not beat the controls.
      • Promotion needs 120+ calls, a mean net return above 0 at t ≥ 2 at mid + 50% fills, a selection confidence interval excluding zero, and a Brier score better than the base rate.
    • B, option run-up trades: kill after 50 calls if the mean net return at mid + 25% fills is ≤ 0.
    • C: needs at least 100 calls with the picked-minus-control underlying return at t ≥ 2, and option P&L at least equal to share-equivalent P&L.
    • F: no capital until the analyst's probabilities beat both p_ref and p_m on Brier score over 100+ resolved events, with net paper P&L above 0.
    • G: after 30+ actionable calls across 3+ episodes, keep the long role only if the 10-day net return beats matched controls with a confidence interval excluding zero. Otherwise G becomes an avoid flag.
    • H: promote from context to signal only if 30+ independent multiweek episodes beat BTC buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted net basis while staying calibrated.
    • I: 100 leads per type.
      • To pass, the net lead-minus-control mean must be above 0 at t ≥ 2, positive in both halves and not driven by one name.
      • Kill a type whose net mean is below 0 after 50 leads.

6.6 Kill rules

  • Fabricated data: any fabricated or unverifiable quote or contract in a row marked executable. Stop and audit.
  • Losing trades: after 50 or more closed trades, stop if mean d ≤ -10% of premium (no t requirement), or if the one-sided 90% upper confidence bound of mean d is below 0. Re-check every 25 trades.
    • Why no t requirement: at a per-trade SD of about 100% of premium, t ≤ -2 at 50 trades needs a mean d near -29%. A process truly losing 10% a trade would be stopped only about 10% of the time, and one losing 20% about 27%.
    • Under the new rule, at 50 trades, those figures are about 50% and 75%. The price is that a zero-edge process is stopped about 25% of the time and a true +5% process about 15%, somewhat more with the repeated checks.
  • Probability skill: after 100 or more forecasts, Brier skill below zero against the market, with a confidence interval excluding zero. Stop using the model's probabilities for sizing.
  • Costs: realized costs above twice the modeled costs over 20 or more trades.
  • Drawdown: beyond the pre-set loss budget. The owner sets it before the first trade; 15% of allocated risk capital is one example.
  • Bugs: any look-ahead or data bug. Fix it, increment the trial count and restart Gate A.
  • Rule changes: new rules only as a new version, run in parallel in the shadow log. Never change rules mid-sample.

EVIDENCE PRIORS AND FORWARD-TEST RULES

Starting priors, not forecasts. Each serious candidate states its prior and a sourced reason to beat it. Figures are historical, gross unless marked; "est." = model estimate. The stricter rule wins where this and the framework differ.

BASE-RATE PRIORS

• Bought options lose before skill: delta-hedged ATM single-stock calls ≈−6% of premium monthly at 40% IV (est., Cao & Han 2013); monthly ATM straddles −17.09%/month at mid (Gao, Xing & Zhang 2018). One prior: bought single-name options −5% to −15% of premium per trade before skill (est.); ≈−8% for one-day holds through earnings (Dubinsky et al. 2019); closer to 0% to −5% only for liquid ETF or index contracts held without an event (est.).
• A/E: assume linked securities have already repriced unless shown otherwise. Ex-microcap 2015–2024: customer momentum 0.28%/month (t 0.8); supply-chain and conglomerate lead-lags −0.07% to +0.09%; earnings and revision surprises ≈0 (OSAP, our computation). Geopolitical shocks (11, hand-picked): market higher 20 sessions later in 8; oil held spikes only with verified supply loss (2). New thematic ETFs: ≈−6%/year risk-adjusted for five years (Ben-David et al.).
• B: one-day ATM straddles through earnings averaged −7.96%, negative every year 2000–2015; implied event moves ran ≈10% above realized on average 2000–2015, but not in 2011–2015 means (Dubinsky et al. 2019). Pre-event option run-up +1.37% gross (Gao et al.), below a 1.5–4.5% round trip at mid ± 25% fills (one post-close snapshot of four event straddles, 2026-10-02; FDX 8–17%). Stock run-up ≈+0.15% per event (our computation): timing only.
• C: past losers −2.5%/year excess, 1927–2013 (Daniel & Moskowitz); reversal and momentum ≈0 since 2006 (French data). Mid/small-cap 30-DTE ATM call: 13.7% median spread, ≈20% of value lost in 10 flat calendar days, ≈27% over 10 trading days (Cboe 2026-10-02; est.).
• D: an ATM put over one release breaks even at an expected abnormal move of ≈−0.6% gross, −1.0% net of a 6% round trip (est.; built on pooled 2000–2015 means, so period-dependent). Anomaly short legs: 0.15%/month before borrow fees, −0.02% after (Muravyev et al. 2023). High-issuance, high-volatility names have the most overpriced options (Cao et al.).
• F: Phase III to NDA/BLA 57.8%, oncology 47.5% (BIO 2021); 50% of new molecular entities approved first cycle (JAMA 2014); ≈1 in 3 approved after a negative advisory vote; no tradable drift after day 1 (Singh et al. 2022). Market-implied probability ≈D/(U+D) for pre-set up (U) and down (D) moves.
• G: crowding predicts lower returns: top short-interest quintile CAPM alpha −0.89%/month EW (2010–2024, our computation); top fee and utilization −2.67%/month (Schultz 2023). At 90%+ utilization, lender-recall squeezes occur about every 11 days (Schultz 2023); price squeezes subside in about 7 trading days on average (2012 working paper, abstract; a different definition). Start at −1% to −3%/month.
• H: ≈4 completed bottoms since 2011; 4-for-4 fits a true hit rate as low as 47%. Positive 1-year BTC returns followed 71% of days since 2012, 53% since 2021. MVRV, Mayer, 200-week MA, Puell and drawdown are one factor (rank correlations 0.71–0.93): one vote. First "bottom-zone" entries came 1.4–2.5× above the final low (Coin Metrics, our computation).
• I: after disclosure, ≈0: 13F clones (GURU −0.13% to −0.16%/month, t≈−1, two independent regressions), congressional copying (NANC/GOP ≈−0.05%/month), analyst changes, public options volume. • Model probabilities: untuned frontier LLMs scored worse than calibrated market prices (Brier 0.201–0.259 vs 0.199; Jajal et al. 2026); only a system fine-tuned for that one task beat the market (0.151), and a chat model following this prompt is not that. Risk-neutral tail probabilities overstate physical ones, so for P(large move) also report a trailing-realized-vol lognormal base rate.

SCORING
