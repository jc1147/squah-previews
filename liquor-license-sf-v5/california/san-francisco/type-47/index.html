18 licenses on the book, asking $135,000 to $350,000, median $232,500. That is the asking range across every Type 47 license on our San Francisco book — license only, no premises, no fixtures, no goodwill. Every one of them has a page of its own.

A Type 47 is the license a San Francisco restaurant needs to pour spirits as well as beer and wine. Formally it is an On-Sale General Eating Place license, and the “eating place” half is the part that catches people out: the kitchen has to stay open and serving whenever alcohol is served, and food has to be a real part of the business rather than a bowl of crisps at the bar. Get that wrong and the license is at risk, which is why a Type 47 and a Type 48 are not interchangeable even though both allow spirits.

It is also the deepest part of the San Francisco market. There are 18 Type 47 licenses on our San Francisco book, asking a median of $232,500 inside a $135,000 to $350,000 range, and the spread inside that band is mostly the condition of the file and the conditions on the license rather than anything about the class. Every one of them is listed below, each with its asking price, its days on the market and a page of its own. The license number, the licensee and the premises address are released to a qualified buyer under NDA, not published.

That obligation to keep feeding people is not a formality and it does not lapse quietly. If the food operation stops, or shrinks to a warming cabinet, the license itself is exposed. Buyers who intend to run a bar with token food should be looking at a Type 48 instead, and paying attention to what the under-21 rule costs them in daytime trade.

San Francisco is over the county ceiling for on-sale general licenses, so the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control issues no new Type 47 originals here. Every one of these is somebody giving one up. That is the whole reason the class costs six figures in this city and four figures in a county with headroom, and it is why the sensible question is not “can I get the price down” but “is this the right license for the room I am building”.

The band

What a San Francisco Type 47 costs

Type 47 licenses on our San Francisco book run from $135,000 to $350,000, with the middle of the book at $232,500. Eighteen licenses is enough spread to see what actually moves a price here, and it is not what buyers expect.

The condition of the file moves it most. A clean register entry with no discipline history, a current tax clearance and an unencumbered position sells at the top of the band. A file with an open tax question or a lien behind it sells at the bottom, and the discount is usually larger than the cost of clearing the problem.

Petition conditions move it next. Hours cut back to midnight, no live entertainment, no outdoor service: these attach to the individual license in San Francisco and travel with it to you. A conditioned license at $175,000 can be a worse buy than a clean one at $240,000 if the conditions collide with the room you are building.

Process

Transferring a Type 47 in San Francisco

Thirty of them are a statutory posting period nobody can shorten.

A person-to-person transfer, where you take the license at its existing address, is the quickest route; a premises-to-premises transfer adds a fresh posting at the new address and, in many districts, a Planning conditional use on its own calendar.

  1. 1

    Offer accepted, escrow opened

    7 days

    A purchase agreement, then escrow with a licensed escrow holder. California requires the full consideration to sit in escrow before the Department will approve the transfer, so this is the point your money moves, not completion day.

  2. 2

    Buyer qualification and application

    10 days

    The person-to-person transfer application, Live Scan fingerprinting for every principal, and proof of funds. This is also when the mutual NDA is signed and the confidential file — license number, licensee of record, premises address, term, encumbrances, tax position — is released to you.

  3. 3

    Public notice posted at the premises

    30 days

    ABC posts the application at the premises for 30 days, and the notice of intended transfer is published in a newspaper of general circulation. The 30 days are statutory and cannot be shortened, whatever anyone promises you.

  4. 4

    Protest period and local agency review

    14 days

    Any protest filed during the posting is worked through here, and this is where San Francisco can add time: the Police Department’s alcohol liaison reviews on-sale applications, and a Planning conditional use, if your district needs one, runs on its own much longer calendar.

  5. 5

    ABC investigation and approval

    10 days

    The investigator confirms the premises, the qualification of the buyer and the tax position of the seller. Clean files clear here without anyone hearing from anyone.

  6. 6

    Escrow closes, license issued in your name

    7 days

    Approval issued, escrow released to the seller, license recorded against you. You can trade.

Questions

Type 47 questions we are actually asked

Can I run a Type 47 room as a bar after the kitchen closes?

No. The bona fide eating place obligation is continuing: meals have to be prepared and served on the premises whenever the license is being exercised, with a kitchen and seating that can support them. A Type 47 room that drifts into trading as a bar with a warming cabinet is a room with an exposed license. If the concept is drinks-led after nine, look at a Type 48 and price in what the under-21 rule costs you in daytime trade.

Can I move a San Francisco Type 47 to another address?

Inside San Francisco County, usually yes. A premises-to-premises transfer needs ABC approval and a fresh 30-day posting at the new address, and that address has to clear San Francisco Planning on its own account — in many Neighborhood Commercial Districts that means a Conditional Use Authorization. Moving a quota license out of the county is generally not available, because the quota is counted county by county.

What happens to the license if the restaurant closes first?

The license survives the room, but not indefinitely and not without care. It has to be kept current with ABC, renewed on time, and either transferred or placed in safekeeping within the period the Department allows. Most of the distressed Type 47s we see are not distressed because the restaurant failed; they are distressed because nobody looked after the license for the eighteen months afterwards.

Why is a Type 47 more expensive than a Type 41?

Spirits and scarcity, in that order. A Type 47 adds distilled spirits, and San Francisco is over the county ceiling for on-sale general licenses, so the only supply is somebody else giving one up — which is what puts these 18 in six figures. A Type 41 covers beer and wine in a bona fide eating place, and there is no Type 41 resale stock on our San Francisco book, so that route is an application to ABC rather than a purchase. We run both routes, so the answer is not “go and do it yourself”: tell us what you are opening and we will tell you which class fits the room and handle whichever route it turns out to be. Does the price include the restaurant?

No. Every figure on this page is for the license alone: no premises, no lease, no fixtures, no kitchen, no goodwill, no stock. If you are buying a going concern, the license is one line of that deal and it is the line that sets the ABC timetable for all of it.
