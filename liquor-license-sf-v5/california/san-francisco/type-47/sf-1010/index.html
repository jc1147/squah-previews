A Type 47 is the license a San Francisco restaurant needs to pour spirits as well as beer and wine. Formally it is an On-Sale General Eating Place license, and the “eating place” half is the binding part: the kitchen has to be open and serving whenever alcohol is served, and food has to be a real part of the business rather than a token gesture at the bar. Drinking is on the premises only, and under-21s may be on the premises. If you are building a bar with token food, a Type 48 is the honest answer instead.

San Francisco sits over the county ceiling for on-sale general licenses, so the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control issues no new Type 47 originals here. Every one on our book, this one included, is an existing license its holder is giving up. That fixed supply, not the fit-out and not the address, is what puts the class into six figures in this city.
