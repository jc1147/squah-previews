The silent complication

Retinopathy does its damage quietly

Diabetic retinopathy is the eye complication of diabetes, and it can work on your retina for years before anything looks or feels different. Your vision holds steady, so it is easy to put the yearly dilated check off, or to never be told you need one at all, until the day your sight finally changes and the damage is already done.

That is the whole reason the screen exists: to find what is happening now, while you still cannot feel it. Eye Trends is here for Clear Lake and Bay Area families and adults who want thorough, personal eye care from a doctor who takes the time, not a screening they get rushed through.

What this exam does

Three things this exam gives you

Not one more box ticked. A true look at the retina, care that is joined up, and one doctor who holds this year against last.

01

A true look at the retina

With your pupils dilated, Dr. Hyder examines the retina and its blood vessels directly, so early bleeding, microaneurysms, and swelling are caught while they are still manageable, not months after they start to touch your vision.

02

Care that is joined up

You leave with a written report that Dr. Hyder sends to the doctor managing your diabetes, so your eye findings and your medical care stay on the same page.

03

One doctor, year over year

This is unhurried medical care, not a fifteen-minute look-and-go. Because the same doctor manages your eye health over time, he can hold this year's retina against last year's and notice the slow change that matters.

Inside the visit

What actually happens at your visit

Your visit here is a proper dilated workup, not a quick glance. Here is what it involves:

  • Dilating drops to open the pupil so the back of the eye can be seen fully.
  • A careful retinal examination of the retina and its blood vessels for microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and macular swelling.
  • A check of your overall eye health, because diabetes rarely travels alone.
  • A documented report shared with your primary-care doctor or endocrinologist.

When the findings point to active disease, Dr. Hyder co-manages your care and refers you to a retina specialist. The screening and the year-to-year monitoring happen here; the specialist handles treatment procedures such as injections or laser. You are never left to figure out the next step on your own.

Common questions

Straight answers before you book

Is a diabetic eye exam covered by insurance?

A diabetic eye exam is often billable to your medical plan rather than your vision plan. We accept most major vision and medical plans, VSP, EyeMed, and Superior, plus medical plans like Medicare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, Cigna, and Tricare, and we are glad to help you check your coverage before you come in. We do not quote prices online; call (281) 488-0066 and we will walk through it with you.

I see fine. Do I still need one?

Yes. Early retinopathy is usually silent, which is exactly why it is screened for every year. Seeing clearly today does not mean the retina is untouched, it means there is still time to catch a change early.

Do you treat retinopathy here?

Screening and monitoring happen here. When treatment is needed, Dr. Hyder co-manages your care and refers you to a retina specialist for procedures such as injections or laser, then keeps watching alongside them.
