Off-the-shelf lenses

"Contacts just aren't for me" is usually the wrong conclusion

Here is how it tends to go. You have astigmatism, or you have hit the age where reading up close has changed, or your prescription is simply demanding, and you get handed a standard soft lens. By mid-afternoon it has dried out. It blurs. It never quite sits where it should. After a round or two of that, most people give up and decide contacts are not for their eyes.

The lens was the problem, not your eyes. A soft lens off the shelf is built for the average eye, and a harder-to-fit eye is not average. That is the kind of patient we built this practice around: Clear Lake and Bay Area families and adults who want thorough, personal eye care from a doctor who takes the time, rather than a rushed big-box fix. We will not throw a made-up statistic at you about how common this is, we will just take the time to find the lens that fits.

What changes

Three things the right specialty lens gives you

Not a stock lens off a shelf. A lens matched to your eye, comfort that holds all day, and a doctor who keeps refining the fit until it is right.

01

A lens matched to your actual eye

We fit the lens to the real shape and movement of your eye, not to an average off a chart. That is the whole difference between a lens that fights you all day and one you forget you are wearing.

02

Comfortable, stable vision through the whole day

The goal is not "clear for the first hour." It is clear and comfortable at 9 in the morning and still clear and comfortable at 6 at night, so you stop planning your day around when the lenses give out.

03

A doctor who adjusts the fit until it is right

This is unhurried care, not a fifteen-minute counter visit. Dr. Hyder takes the time to measure and fit, and then refines the lens over calm follow-ups instead of settling for "close enough." We talk, we visit, and we keep at it until the fit is right on your eye.

What we fit

The three specialty lenses we fit, and how the fitting works

  • Toric lenses for astigmatism, soft lenses built to hold their orientation so astigmatism does not blur back in through the day.
  • Gas-permeable (GP) rigid lenses for demanding prescriptions, custom-ordered rigid lenses that keep their shape on the eye and correct a strong or difficult prescription more precisely than a soft lens usually manages.
  • Multifocal lenses for presbyopia, one lens that carries both near and far, so you can read a menu and see across the room without reaching for readers.

Every one of these starts the same way: careful measurement of the curvature and surface of your cornea, a trial lens on the eye so we can watch how it centers and moves each time you blink, and follow-up visits to confirm the comfort and the clarity both hold.

We fit toric, gas-permeable, and multifocal lenses, and those three fittings cover the large majority of the difficult prescriptions we see.

Some eyes, an irregular or surgically-changed cornea, for instance, need a specialty lens that falls outside those three fittings. That kind of lens is outside our scope, and if your eyes need it we will tell you honestly and point you to the right specialist rather than guess at it.

Common questions

Honest answers before you book

I have been told my eyes are too hard to fit. Can you help?

Often, yes. A lens built to your eye often succeeds where a standard soft lens cannot. We will tell you honestly at the fitting whether a specialty fitting is right for your eyes, and if your eyes need a lens that falls outside the three we fit, we will say so plainly and refer you rather than guess.

Does it take more than one visit?

Sometimes. A hard-to-fit lens can need a trial and a follow-up or two to get right, because we are refining the fit on your actual eye rather than pulling a stock lens off a shelf. That patience is the point, it is how the lens ends up comfortable instead of merely close.

Is it covered by insurance?

A contact-lens fitting is often billable. We accept most major vision and medical plans, VSP, EyeMed, and Superior, plus medical plans like Medicare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, Cigna, and Tricare, and we are glad to help you check your coverage before you come in. There is no surgery performed at our office; the specialty fittings we do here are the three lens types described above. We do not quote prices online, so call (281) 488-0066 and we will walk through it with you.
