The drops cycle

Months of drops, and your eyes still burn

If you have been buying a new bottle of drops every few weeks, you already know the pattern. The relief lasts an hour, maybe an afternoon, and then the burning, grittiness, or watering comes back, usually right in the middle of a workday at the screen, a long drive home, or an evening you wanted to wear your contacts. Off-the-shelf drops treat the feeling for a little while. They do not tell you why your eyes are dry in the first place, so the cycle keeps repeating.

Most of the people we see for dry eye in Clear Lake are not looking for one more quick fix. They are families and working adults who want someone to take the time, look closely, and sort out what is actually going on, so it can be treated once instead of managed blindly for years.

What changes

Three things a real dry-eye workup gives you

Not one more bottle. A diagnosis, a plan built around it, and follow-up that keeps working as your eyes change.

01

Which kind of dry eye you have

Dry eye comes in more than one form. Your eyes might not be making enough tears, or they might be making tears that evaporate too fast because the oil glands in your lids are blocked. The treatment for one is not the treatment for the other. The first thing we do is find out which kind you have, so the plan is aimed at the right problem.

02

A plan built for your eyes

Once we know the type, you get a plan matched to it rather than a one-size bottle. That may be a specific drop, warm compresses, a change to how you work or use screens, or prescription treatment when it is warranted. The point is that every step has a reason behind it.

03

Follow-up that adjusts

Dry eye changes with the seasons, your health, and your habits, so a plan that worked in March may need a tweak by August. We take the time to check whether your treatment is actually working and change course when it is not. This is medical treatment, which means we manage the condition over time, not just hand you a prescription and send you off.

Inside the exam

What we look at, and what we do about it

A dry-eye visit is a proper medical workup, not a squint-and-guess. We evaluate the surface of your eye, assess your tears and how well they coat and protect the eye, and check your eyelids and the oil (meibomian) glands along their edges, since blocked glands are one of the most common reasons eyes feel dry. Then you leave with a written plan you can actually follow: drops chosen for your type, warm compresses if your glands need them, practical adjustments to screens and environment, and prescription treatment when it is indicated.

Because Dr. Hyder is a licensed therapeutic optometrist, dry eye is part of the medical eye care we provide here, alongside conditions like glaucoma and diabetic eye disease. If it turns out to stem from something that needs a specialist's procedure, we will tell you plainly and help coordinate that next step, rather than sell you something we do not do.

Common questions

Straight answers before you book

Is dry eye treatment covered by insurance?

Often, yes. Dry eye is a medical condition, so a dry-eye visit is frequently billable to your medical insurance rather than a vision plan, though it depends on your plan and your symptoms. We accept most major medical and vision plans, including Medicare, Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Cigna, and Tricare. Call us at (281) 488-0066 and we will help you understand what applies to you. We do not post prices, since so much depends on your coverage, so call for pricing and we will give you a straight answer.

Won't over-the-counter drops just fix it?

They can ease the symptoms for a while, but they do not address why your eyes are dry, so the relief usually does not last. A real diagnosis tells us which type of dry eye you have, and that is what makes the treatment stick instead of masking the feeling one bottle at a time.

How long until I feel relief?

It depends on the type and how severe your dry eye is. Some people feel a real difference soon after starting the right plan; others have a more stubborn case that we manage over time. We set honest expectations at your exam rather than promise a number we cannot back up, and we adjust as we learn how your eyes respond.
