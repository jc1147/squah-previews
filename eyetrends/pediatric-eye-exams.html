The problem hides

A young child can't tell you what they can't see

Blurry is normal to a child who has never seen any other way, so a vision problem rarely shows up as "I can't see." It shows up as squinting, sitting too close to the screen, headaches after homework, losing their place when they read, or getting restless and "acting up" in class. A quick school line-screening, or a rushed check at a big-box counter, is easy to pass and can still miss all of it.

We built this practice for Clear Lake and Bay Area families who want thorough, personal care rather than a rushed screening. So we take the time to look at the whole picture, not just the top line of the chart.

Inside the exam

What a pediatric exam covers here

A pediatric exam at Eye Trends is a real, careful look, sized to your child's age. For pre-readers, we use picture and matching charts instead of letters. Dr. Hyder checks how well the eyes focus, team together, and track a moving target, along with depth and color vision, and he takes a refraction to measure a glasses prescription if one is needed. He also looks at your child's overall eye health.

You also walk out with a school-vision screening you can use for school and sports forms. And for a child who is becoming nearsighted, we offer myopia management using atropine or multifocal lenses to help slow the change while their eyes are still growing. Anything that calls for care we don't provide here, we refer to a trusted specialist rather than over-promise.

Common questions

Straight answers before you book

How old should my child be for a first exam?

We see children from about age 4 to 5. If you have a question about a younger child, call us at (281) 488-0066 and we'll help you decide when the time is right.

Isn't the school screening enough?

A school screening is a fast distance check, and it's a helpful early flag - but it flags less than a full exam. It can miss focusing problems, eye-teaming issues, and eye-health concerns. A full pediatric exam looks at all of it.

Is it covered by insurance?

Many vision plans include a child's exam. We accept most vision and medical plans, including VSP, EyeMed, Superior, Aetna, BCBS, Medicare, Cigna, and Tricare. Coverage varies by plan, so call (281) 488-0066 and we'll help you check yours before the visit.

What if my child needs more than glasses?

If they're becoming nearsighted, we offer myopia management with atropine or multifocal lenses. We don't run therapy programs or eye-exercise programs here, and for anything outside what we do, we'll point you to the right specialist or co-manage the care with them.
