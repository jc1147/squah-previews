The yearly jump

Every year a stronger prescription, and no one mentions the alternative

If your child is nearsighted, you already know the pattern. The eye exam ends the same way each time: the numbers have climbed again, the lenses get a little thicker, and you walk out with a stronger prescription and a plan to come back and do it all over next year.

What most families are never told is that a child's nearsightedness can often be slowed down while their eyes are still growing. A rushed visit leaves no room for that conversation, so families just accept the yearly jump, and the higher long-term risk that rides along with it. If you would rather have thorough, personal care than a quick fix, that gap is exactly what a myopia-management plan is built to close.

What changes

What a myopia plan is working toward

We care for children here from around age 4 or 5, and a myopia plan is built around your child and you, the parent, not squeezed into a rushed appointment. Here is what the plan is working toward.

01

Slower progression

Instead of watching the prescription jump every year, the plan works to slow how fast it changes, so your child grows up toward a lower final prescription than their eyes might have reached on their own.

02

Fewer disruptive jumps

Smaller changes between visits mean fewer of those sudden blurry stretches where your child cannot read the board until a new pair of glasses catches up. Their vision stays steadier from one check to the next.

03

A lower lifetime risk

Very strong nearsightedness carries a higher lifetime risk of eye problems later in life. By keeping the final prescription lower, the plan aims to lower that long-term risk, not just make this year's lenses thinner. It slows how fast the nearsightedness worsens; it is not a cure.

How it works

How the program works

Dr. Hyder offers two proven methods for slowing a child's myopia, and chooses between them based on your child's age, eyes, and daily routine:

  • Low-dose atropine eye drops. A small nightly drop that helps slow how quickly the myopia progresses, without changing what your child has to do during the day.
  • Multifocal soft contact lenses. Soft daytime lenses that correct your child's vision and work to slow progression at the same time.

Every plan starts with a careful baseline measurement of your child's current prescription, so there is a clear starting point on the record. From there we bring your child back for periodic re-checks to see whether the plan is working, and to adjust it over time as their eyes keep growing.

Common questions

Questions parents ask

Can this cure or reverse my child's myopia?

No, and we will always be straight with you about that. Myopia management slows how fast the nearsightedness gets worse; it does not undo the nearsightedness your child already has. The aim is a lower final prescription and healthier eyes for the long run, not a cure.

Are low-dose atropine drops safe for kids?

Low-dose atropine has a long track record in eye care, and the concentration used for myopia control is very small. Most children tolerate it well. We start carefully, watch how your child responds at each re-check, and adjust or switch to multifocal soft lenses if the drops do not suit them.

What if my child needs something you don't offer?

We keep our scope honest. For myopia management we offer low-dose atropine drops and multifocal soft contact lenses, and if your child ever needs care beyond what we provide, we will tell you plainly and refer you, or co-manage with the right specialist, rather than stretch past what we actually do here.

Is it covered by insurance?

It depends on your plan, and this kind of care can fall under medical or vision benefits differently from a routine exam. We accept most vision and medical plans, including VSP, EyeMed, Superior, Aetna, BCBS, Medicare, Cigna, and Tricare. Because coverage varies from family to family, we don't post prices online. Call (281) 488-0066 and we will help you check your benefits before the visit.
