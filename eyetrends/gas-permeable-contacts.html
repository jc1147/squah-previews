The wrong answer

"Contacts won't work for your eyes" is often the wrong answer

If your prescription is strong or highly astigmatic, a soft lens can blur, or rotate off-axis so the world drifts in and out of focus. Or you go to a value counter, get told your eyes are too complicated for contacts, and give up - back to glasses full-time, sure that lenses just aren't for you.

A gas-permeable lens is often exactly the answer. It's a rigid lens, custom-ordered to the shape of your eye, and it holds focus where a soft lens gives it up. But it only works with a careful fitting - it isn't pulled off a shelf the same day, and it isn't guessed at between other patients. That's the kind of care we built this practice around: Clear Lake and Bay Area families and adults who want thorough, personal eye care from a doctor who gives it real time, not a rushed off-the-shelf hand-off.

What it gives you

Three things a rigid GP lens, fitted right, gives you

Not a same-day guess off a shelf. Sharper vision, a lens that lasts, and a fit refined over calm follow-ups until it sits the way it should.

01

Sharper, steadier vision

A GP lens keeps its shape on the eye instead of draping over it, so it can correct a demanding or astigmatic prescription more precisely than a soft lens usually manages. For a lot of people, it's the clearest they've seen in contacts.

02

A lens that lasts

Cared for properly, a GP lens holds up for years - it isn't a monthly disposable you're forever reordering. You clean it, you store it, and it stays your lens.

03

A fit that gets refined until it's right

This is unhurried care, not a fifteen-minute conveyor belt. Dr. Hyder gives you real thirty-minute visits where we talk, we visit, and we get the fit right - adjusting the lens over calm follow-ups until it sits and moves the way it should on your eye.

Inside the fitting

What happens when you're fitted for GP lenses

  1. Careful measurements of the curvature and surface of your eye, so the lens is shaped to you and not to an average.
  2. A trial rigid lens on the eye, watched for how it centers and how it moves each time you blink.
  3. An over-refraction - checking your vision through the trial lens to land on the sharpest prescription.
  4. Hands-on coaching for a first-time rigid wearer: how to get the lens in and out, and how to clean, store, and care for it.
  5. Follow-up visits to fine-tune the lens on your eye until the fit and the vision both hold.

We fit gas-permeable lenses for demanding, standard prescriptions - strong astigmatism and hard-to-fit eyes that soft lenses can't hold, along with rigid options for reading-age vision. A small number of specialty rigid fittings, for eyes with an irregular or surgically-changed cornea, fall outside what we do here.

If your eyes need that kind of specialty lens, we'll tell you honestly and point you to the right specialist rather than guess at it.

Honest answers

Straight answers before you book

Do gas-permeable lenses hurt at first?

There's an adaptation period. A rigid lens feels present for the first few days - you know it's there - and then it settles as your eyes get used to it. We coach you through that stretch, and if it turns out a rigid lens isn't right for you, we'll tell you straight rather than push it.

Can you fit me if my eyes are complicated - surgery history or an unusually shaped cornea?

We fit rigid GP lenses for demanding, standard prescriptions. Eyes with an irregular cornea sometimes need a specialty rigid lens that falls outside our scope - in that case we'll tell you honestly and refer you. If you've had LASIK or cataract surgery, we handle your contacts as co-management, working alongside your surgeon.

Is the fitting covered by insurance?

A contact-lens fitting is often billable. We accept most major vision and medical plans - VSP, EyeMed, and Superior, plus medical plans like Medicare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, Cigna, and Tricare - and we're glad to help you check your coverage before you come in. We don't quote prices online; call (281) 488-0066 and we'll walk through it with you.
