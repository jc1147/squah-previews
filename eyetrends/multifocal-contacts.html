Readers in every room

Somewhere in your forties, the menu goes fuzzy

It starts small. The menu is blurry in low light, texts move to arm's length, and the label you could read last year now wants a brighter lamp and a longer reach. A lot of people are told this is where contacts end, and that from here on it is a pair of readers in every room and a set of drugstore cheaters perched over their lenses. It does not have to go that way.

Most of the people we fit for multifocal contacts are not looking for a workaround. They are Clear Lake and Bay Area families and adults who want thorough, personal eye care from a doctor who takes the time, rather than a rushed hand-off at a big-box counter. Presbyopia is a normal change, and there is usually a way to stay in contacts through it. We will not pretend every eye adapts the same way, but we will find out what yours can do before we promise anything.

Inside the fitting

What a multifocal fitting actually covers

A multifocal or bifocal contact fitting works through a few clear steps, each one explained as we go:

  • Near and distance measured. We measure your near and distance prescription so the correction is built around how your eyes actually focus.
  • Your dominant eye. We check which eye is your dominant one, since that guides how the design is balanced between the two.
  • The approach that suits you, trialed. For most people that is a soft multifocal lens, which carries both near and distance zones in the same lens; for some, a monovision setup fits better, where one eye is tuned for distance and the other for near.
  • A follow-up to fine-tune. We bring you back to fine-tune the lens while it is on your eye, because how a lens settles in real life is what decides whether it works.

Multifocal and bifocal fittings are part of the contact-lens exams we perform here, alongside hard-to-fit toric lenses for astigmatism and gas permeable lenses for demanding prescriptions. Some eyes need a specialized medical lens that we do not fit in this office; when that is the case, we will tell you plainly and point you to the right specialist, rather than sell you something we do not do.

What a chain counter can't do with a multifocal

At a rushed big-box or chain optical counter, a multifocal tends to end the same place: you are handed a trial box and sent on your way, and if the lenses feel off, there is no one who remembers your eyes to adjust them. That is a transaction, and multifocals are one of the fittings a transaction handles poorly, because they take a follow-up or two to settle in. Eye Trends is a private, one-doctor practice, not an impersonal retail counter. Common questions

Straight answers before you book

Will multifocal contacts actually work for me?

For a lot of people, yes, but not every eye adapts identically, and we would rather be straight with you than oversell. That is why we trial the lenses first and watch how your eyes respond before you commit to anything. If multifocals turn out not to be right for you, we will tell you, and we will look at other ways to get you seeing clearly at both distances.

Can I get multifocal contacts if I have astigmatism?

Often, yes. There are toric multifocal lenses made to correct astigmatism and presbyopia at the same time, and we will check whether the fit holds and stays put on your eye. Astigmatism on its own rarely rules out staying in contacts.

Is the fitting covered by insurance?

A contact-lens fitting is often billable, though it depends on your plan, and a multifocal is often handled a little differently from a standard fit. We accept most major vision and medical plans, including VSP, EyeMed, Superior Vision, Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Cigna, Medicare, and Tricare. We do not post prices, since so much depends on your coverage, so call (281) 488-0066 for pricing and we will give you a straight answer. There are also specialized lenses we do not fit in this office; if your eyes need one, we will say so and help you find the right care.
