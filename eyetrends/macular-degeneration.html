The silent stage

In its early stages, macular degeneration is silent

No pain, no blur, no warning

Age-related macular degeneration usually gives you no warning at the start. There is no pain, no sudden blur, and in the early and dry stages your vision can feel completely normal while the macula slowly thins underneath. That is what makes it easy to drift for years, until the day reading gets harder, driving feels different, and faces start to lose their center.

That silence is the whole reason to look now, while there is still nothing to feel. Eye Trends is here for Clear Lake and Bay Area families and adults who want thorough, personal eye care from a doctor who takes the time, not a rushed big-box look-and-go.

Inside the evaluation

What actually happens when we check for macular degeneration

  • A dilated examination of the macula, looking for drusen and pigment changes, the earliest signs the macula is under strain.
  • Telling dry from wet. Most macular degeneration takes the slower dry form; the wet form moves faster and needs prompt attention, so knowing which one you have shapes everything that follows.
  • A simple at-home Amsler grid, so you can watch your own central vision between visits and call us the moment something looks different.
  • Plain guidance on the steps tied to slower progression, the nutrition, the smoking, and the everyday choices that research links to a steadier course.
  • A written monitoring schedule, so you and Dr. Hyder both know when you are due back.

The next step, handled here

If the picture ever points to wet macular degeneration, Dr. Hyder refers you promptly to a retina specialist for the treatment itself. The diagnosis and the year-to-year monitoring happen here; the specialist handles procedures such as injections. This is co-management: Dr. Hyder does not perform injections or surgery on site, and you are never left to sort out the next step alone.

Common questions

Straight answers before you book

Can macular degeneration be cured?

No one can reverse macular degeneration, and we will never tell you otherwise. What we can do is diagnose it, monitor it closely over time, guide the nutrition and lifestyle steps tied to a slower course, and coordinate promptly with a retina specialist if it turns wet. Caught and watched early, there is a lot we can do to protect the vision you have.

Do you give the eye injections?

No. Injections for wet macular degeneration are done by a retina specialist. Dr. Hyder handles the diagnosis, the ongoing monitoring, and the coordination, and refers you promptly when treatment is needed, then keeps watching alongside the specialist.

Is a macular degeneration check covered by insurance?

A macular degeneration evaluation is often billable to your medical plan rather than your vision plan. We accept most major vision and medical plans, including VSP, EyeMed, and Superior, plus medical plans like Medicare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, Cigna, and Tricare, and we are glad to help you check your coverage. We do not quote prices online; call (281) 488-0066 and we will walk through it with you.

How often should I be seen?

It depends on your stage and your risk. Some patients are watched once a year, others more often. We set the schedule with you, so you always know when you are due back.
