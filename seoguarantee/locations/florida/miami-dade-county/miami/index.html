Why would a Miami business need Spanish pages for SEO?

Because many of your customers search in Spanish, and Google answers a Spanish search mostly with Spanish pages. If your team serves customers in Spanish, pages in Spanish let those searches find you. We write them alongside your English pages and keep both accurate.

Is guaranteed marketing in Miami a promise of more sales?

No. The guarantee in guaranteed marketing is our first page guarantee: it covers where you rank, never how much you sell. Of all digital marketing, SEO is the piece that meets people as they search, and around it we look after your Business Profile, your reviews and, through our Full Suite, design and conversion on your website.

Do you work with businesses across Miami-Dade, not just in the city?

Yes. Pages go up for the neighborhoods and cities your customers come from, inside the city limits or outside them, and your Business Profile’s service area is drawn to cover the same ground. The county page goes city by city.

My website is old. Can we still start?

Yes. Work begins on the site you already run, starting with what customers trip over first: slow pages, a booking link that fails on a phone, opening hours that contradict your profile. If the site itself is holding you back, the assessment will say so plainly.

How do you decide which Miami neighborhoods get their own page?

We start with the neighborhoods that already bring you work and the ones where few competitors answer the search well. A page for Wynwood or Coconut Grove only goes up if your team really serves it and has something true to say about it.

Is SEO still worth paying for in a city this competitive?

For most local businesses, yes, and the competition is the reason. Ads stop the day the budget stops, while a page that ranks keeps working for you. In a crowded market the business with the clearest pages tends to win the customer, and those pages are what we build.

Where our figures come from

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau: Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (July 1, 2025); American Community Survey, 2020-2024 5-year estimates; 2022 Economic Census.

How the figures marked † were derived

  • People who moved to Miami in a typical year (at least) † is derived: residents one year and over who lived in another county, another state or abroad a year earlier, added together; a move from elsewhere in Miami-Dade County is not counted, so it is a floor; a typical year of the 2020-2024 surveys.

  • Share of workers in their own business † is derived: employed residents who work in their own business, incorporated or not, divided by all employed residents.

  • Share of Miami-Dade County living in the city † is derived: the county's July 2025 residents inside the Miami city limits divided by all its residents.

  • Share who speak Spanish at home † is derived: residents 5 and over who speak Spanish at home divided by all residents 5 and over.
