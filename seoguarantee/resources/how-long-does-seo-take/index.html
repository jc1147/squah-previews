How Long Does SEO Take to Work?

What Google and the ranking studies say about SEO timing, what can move in the first weeks, what takes months, and how 90, 180 and 365-day first page milestones line up.

How long SEO takes to work depends on the phrase and on who already holds page one: Google says some changes take effect within a few hours and others take several months, and it suggests waiting a few weeks before judging your work [2]. Competitive phrases take longest, because the pages already ranking for them tend to be old [5] and well linked [17].

The short answer

Direct answer

SEO shows its first effects in weeks and its bigger results over months. Google says some changes take effect within hours and others take several months, so judge after a few weeks [2]. Work you control, like your Business Profile and your pages, can be done early; reviews, links and reputation build over months. Plan and measure in 90, 180 and 365-day steps.

Three things set the pace:

  • The phrase. Ahrefs found that high-volume keywords take longer to rank for than low-volume ones [5].
  • The place. The map results and the organic results lean on different signals. Whitespark's 2026 survey of local search experts weights the map pack toward Business Profile signals (32%) and reviews (20%), and local organic results toward on-page factors (33%) and links (24%) [9].
  • The competition. The pages you have to pass set the bar, and in Ahrefs' data most of them have been around for years [5].

What Google says about timing

Direct answer

Google's current SEO Starter Guide says some changes take effect in a few hours and others could take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks to judge [2]. Until June 2026, Google's "Do you need an SEO?" page also said results typically take four months to a year [1]. The revision dated June 5, 2026 removed that sentence [3], so treat it as history, not current guidance.

Google's current wording is worth reading in full: "Every change you make will take some time to be reflected on Google's end. Some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months. In general, you likely want to wait a few weeks to assess whether your work had beneficial effects in Google Search results." [2]

In versions of "Do you need an SEO?" dated from September 2022 to December 2025, Google wrote: "Remember that it will take time for you to see results: typically from four months to a year from the time you begin making changes until you start to see the benefits." [1] The version dated June 5, 2026 no longer contains it [3].

Some effects wait on Google itself. After site-wide quality work, Google says "it could take several months for our systems to learn and confirm" the change, and adds: "If it's been a few months and you still haven't seen any effect, that could mean waiting until the next core update." [4] Google confirmed core updates on March 13, June 30 and December 11, 2025, and on March 27 and May 21, 2026 [6]. It also says positions in its results are not fixed in place [4].

What the ranking studies show

Direct answer

One timing study is Ahrefs' 2025 analysis of new pages. Of one million random URLs its crawler found in September 2023, only 1.74% reached Google's top 10 within a year; in a stricter sample of English pages, 6.11% did [5]. Of the pages that made it, 40.82% got there within a month, and high-volume keywords took longer [5].

Stat

1.74% of the random new pages Ahrefs sampled reached the top 10 within a year [5]

Read that figure carefully. These were random pages with no targeted work behind them, so the study shows that page one is not automatic; it does not predict what a planned campaign will do. It also shows how old the competition is: 72.9% of top-10 pages were more than three years old, and the page in the first organic result was five years old on average [5]. Ahrefs' practical advice is to update a page that is still outside the top 10 after about six months [5].

What moves first: the Business Profile and map results

Direct answer

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity, which its help page explains under the heading "Prominence" [7]. Relevance is the part you can fix first: the primary category, services and complete, accurate details. Distance you cannot change. Prominence, which Google ties to links and reviews, builds over months, so reviews need a steady flow.

Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking [7]. It also says the categories you choose affect local ranking, and that changing a category can prompt Google to ask you to verify the business again [8]. Settle categories early. In Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 experts, the primary Business Profile category ranked as the top map-pack factor [9].

Reviews are a flow, not a stock. Sterling Sky's study of 8,186 businesses in 200 US cities found that the number of reviews a business gained that month mattered more than its total, a correlation rather than proof [10]. Whitespark's experts also rate a steady flow of reviews over time, rather than bursts, as a ranking factor [9]. And Google said in April 2026 that a sudden spike in reviews can lead it to pause new reviews on the profile and display a notification banner [11]. You cannot safely rush this part. Our local SEO guide covers the full profile work.

What takes longer: competitive organic phrases

Direct answer

Competitive organic phrases take longest because the pages already on page one tend to be old [5] and well linked [17]. Ahrefs' rule of thumb for the top 10 is to acquire as many backlinks as the pages already there, if not more [17]. Links and reputation are earned over months, so the order in which you go after phrases decides how early results appear.

Most keywords are long-tail: in Ahrefs' US database, almost 93% of keywords get fewer than 10 searches a month [12]. In our method, the plan starts with the service, modifier and place variations whose page-one holders measure weakest, and works up to the head terms. That order comes from measurement, phrase by phrase, which is what keyword difficulty and SEO competitor analysis are for. The organic SEO guide covers the page and link work itself.

Google's AI answers follow the same foundations. Google says its generative AI features are "rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems" [13], and that AI Overviews often don't appear at all [15]. How often they cite page-one results varies by study: Ahrefs found 76% of AI Overview citations ranked in the top 10 in July 2025, and about 38% in March 2026 [14].

A 90, 180 and 365-day view

Direct answer

A realistic SEO timeline measures in three windows. The first 90 days go to measurement, the Business Profile, page fixes and the phrases with the smallest gaps. By 180 days, content, links and review flow compound across more phrases. The rest of the first year goes to the harder phrases, where links and reputation take longest. This is how we plan the work, not a measured average.

No public data give the odds for a schedule like ours, so we don't quote any. What we can show is the measurement behind the first page dates, in how an SEO guarantee works.

Frequently asked questions

How long does SEO take for a new website?

Longer than for an established site, because a new site competes with old pages: in Ahrefs' 2025 study, 72.9% of top-10 pages were more than three years old [5]. Start with your Business Profile and with the long-tail variations whose page-one holders are weakest, then work up to competitive phrases. Judge single changes after a few weeks [2], and the whole plan against dated milestones.

How long does local SEO take?

Profile fixes such as categories and complete details can be made in the first weeks, and Google says complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show [7]. Distance never changes. Prominence, which rests partly on reviews and links, builds month by month [7], and recent reviews appear to carry weight: Sterling Sky found that reviews gained that month mattered more than the total [10]. Plan in months, not days.

Why is my SEO not working after three months?

Check three things. Are the phrases held by strong, well-linked pages? Have the links and reviews actually been built? Are you checking from your customers' market, since Google's results depend on the searcher's location [16]? After site-wide quality work, Google says an effect can take several months and sometimes waits for the next core update [4]. Compare against your baseline before changing course.

Does SEO take longer in competitive markets?

Yes. Ahrefs found that high-volume keywords take longer to rank for than low-volume ones [5], and its rule of thumb for the top 10 is to match the backlinks of the pages already there, if not more [17]. Competitive markets have stronger page-one holders, so the gap to close is larger. Measuring that gap first tells you how long the plan needs.

When should I expect first page results?

It depends on the gap between you and the pages already there, so measure that first. Under our first page guarantee, the written milestones are at least 20% of your agreed phrases on the first page by day 90, 40% by day 180 and 50%+ by the end of the first year, measured separately in each of the three places (in AI answers, over the phrases for which Google shows one when checked). It is offered after an assessment, and only if you qualify.

Can SEO results disappear?

Yes. Google says positions in its results are not fixed in place, and it makes broad core updates several times a year [4]. In local results, recent reviews appear to carry weight [10], so a profile that stops earning them can slip. SEO is maintained, not finished: the work that got you to page one is the work that keeps you there.
