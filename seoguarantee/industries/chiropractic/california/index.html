Advertising law

Chiropractic advertising in California: the rules we follow for you

We put your license ahead of any ranking, so every page follows the state's chiropractic advertising rules. California bars false, misleading or deceptive claims and images, anything likely to raise false hopes of good results, superiority claims you cannot back with objective evidence, and scientific claims without published, peer-reviewed support. The chiropractic board's own rules add a ban on distorted, sensational or fabulous statements.

Paying people known as cappers or steerers to bring you patients is unprofessional conduct, which is why we win patients through search and never through paid referrals of accident victims. If you advertise a routine service at a discount, any extra charge that visit needs the patient's agreement first. An ad may state a certification from a board or agency, so we show yours exactly as granted. Start Step 1 and your current pages get a rules check from us.

What exactly is guaranteed when you market a California chiropractic clinic?

Just your rankings. Our first page guarantee is about the spot your clinic holds on Google and says nothing about bookings. Alongside it we keep your Business Profile complete, ask happy patients for reviews and write pages about the care you give; a new website is an option through our Full Suite.

How do you bring in injury patients without chasing accident victims?

Injured people search for help themselves, so the work is making your exam, records and billing pages easy to find and easy to trust. Paid steering of patients is off the table for us, as the board's rules require.

Can you market a chiropractic practice built around one kind of care in California?

Yes. Sports, rehab, prenatal, pediatric, injury, decompression, upper cervical and family practices each get pages written around their care and their patients' words. Any certification you mention is stated as granted, naming the board or body that granted it, and no page promises a cure or claims you outperform other chiropractors.

Can my pages mention decompression tables and FDA status?

Yes, accurately. Powered traction equipment is a cleared device class, and clearance differs from an FDA approval, so your pages describe the table in the terms of its classification and make no promise of relief.

Do you work with clinics outside Los Angeles and San Diego?

Yes. Sacramento, Fresno, the Inland Empire and the Central Coast all have patients searching for a chiropractor, often with fewer clinics answering well. We plan your pages around the cities your patients actually come from.

Should a multi-location clinic have separate pages and profiles?

Yes. Google treats every location as a separate map entry, so each one needs its own page and its own profile, while your clinic name and standards stay consistent.

Where our figures come from

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau: Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (July 1, 2025); American Community Survey, 2020-2024 5-year estimates; 2022 Economic Census; Census Geocoder (to place each practice address on the map).

  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS): NPPES NPI Registry, September 26, 2026.

How the figures marked † were derived

  • People who moved to California in a typical year † is derived: residents one year and over who lived in another state or abroad a year earlier, added together; a typical year of the 2020-2024 surveys.

  • Chiropractors in California by main field † is derived: the California chiropractors counted by the main field each lists (self-reported, not a license or a certification).

  • Chiropractors by county † is derived: the same California chiropractor records, counted by the county each practice address falls in.

  • Chiropractors with a practice address in California (at least) † is derived: every active chiropractor in the NPPES registry whose practice address places inside California; a floor that also counts faculty and residents.

  • chiropractors with a practice address in the county † is derived: the California chiropractor records whose practice address places inside Orange County; a floor.

  • Average receipts per chiropractic office † is derived: California chiropractic offices' 2022 receipts divided by the number of California chiropractic offices.

  • Residents per chiropractic office † is derived: California's July 2022 population estimate divided by the 2022 count of chiropractic offices in the state.

  • Share who speak Spanish at home † is derived: residents 5 and over who speak Spanish at home divided by all residents 5 and over.

Rules cited on this page

  • California Code of Regulations, title 16, division 4 (Board of Chiropractic Examiners), in the board's Rules and Regulations (revised November 2021): 16 CCR 311, 16 CCR 317, 16 CCR 319 (version dated 2022-01-11).

  • Code of Federal Regulations, title 16, part 465 (FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials): 16 CFR 465.2.

  • Code of Federal Regulations, title 21, part 890 (FDA: Physical Medicine Devices): 21 CFR 890.5900.

  • California Business and Professions Code, section 651: BPC 651.
