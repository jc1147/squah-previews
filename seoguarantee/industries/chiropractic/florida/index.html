Florida's chiropractic ad rules

Florida's chiropractic advertising rules, and how our pages keep to them

Protecting your license outweighs any ranking, so our pages follow the rules of Florida’s Board of Chiropractic Medicine. False, deceptive or misleading advertising is grounds for discipline, every ad must identify the doctor or clinic as chiropractic, and soliciting patients in person or through an agent is barred except where board rules allow it.

The board also bars promises of cures or unjustified expectations, claims about the quality of care, and any suggestion that you are better than other chiropractors. You may state Diplomate status in a specialty the board recognizes; a credential from any other body must name that body and carry the board’s private-organization statement. A free or discounted offer states your usual fee and carries the statutory patient notice. Start with Step 1 and we will read your current pages against these rules.

Can a Florida chiropractor advertise as a specialist?

Only in the way the board allows. You may state Diplomate status in a specialty the Board of Chiropractic Medicine recognizes; any other certification must name the body that granted it and carry the board’s statement that it is a private organization. We write your credentials exactly that way.

What does chiropractic marketing cover, and what does your first page guarantee promise a Florida office?

Chiropractic marketing reaches past search into your Business Profile, your reviews, the referrals you get and a website that takes bookings. Our first page guarantee applies to the search part: it concerns your ranking, never a number of new patients, and everything around it exists to turn a search into a first visit.

Can you market my office to people hurt in car accidents?

We can make sure people searching for auto-injury care find clear pages about how you treat them. We never contact or solicit accident victims, in person or through anyone else; the patient finds you, never the other way round.

Does it matter where in Florida my office is?

It changes the work, not whether we can do it. Crowded coastal markets need neighborhood-level pages and a steady stream of reviews, while inland and Panhandle towns often have fewer offices answering searches well, so fewer pages go further.

Can my pages mention decompression tables or other equipment?

Yes, accurately. We describe each device the way its FDA status allows, so a cleared traction table is never called FDA approved, and no page promises a result.

Will you write Spanish pages for my office?

Where our figures come from

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau: Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (July 1, 2025); American Community Survey, 2020-2024 5-year estimates; 2022 Economic Census; Census Geocoder (to place each practice address on the map).

  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS): NPPES NPI Registry, September 26, 2026.

How the figures marked † were derived

  • People who moved to Florida in a typical year † is derived: residents one year and over who lived in another state or abroad a year earlier, added together; a typical year of the 2020-2024 surveys.

  • Chiropractors in Florida by main field † is derived: the Florida chiropractors counted by the main field each lists (self-reported, not a license or board certification).

  • Chiropractors by county † is derived: the same Florida chiropractor records, counted by the county each practice address falls in.

  • Chiropractors with a practice address in Florida (at least) † is derived: every active chiropractor in the NPPES registry whose practice address is in Florida, counted once; it includes teaching faculty and residents, not only chiropractors in private practice.

  • Average receipts per chiropractic office † is derived: Florida chiropractic offices' 2022 receipts divided by the number of those offices.

  • Residents per chiropractic office † is derived: Florida's July 2022 population estimate divided by the 2022 count of chiropractic offices with paid staff.

  • Share who speak Spanish at home † is derived: residents 5 and over who speak Spanish at home divided by all residents 5 and over.
