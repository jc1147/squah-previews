How clients choose

How people choose a therapist, and where they stop

Your next client may search late at night, after weeks of putting it off. They look for someone who works with their concern, who takes their insurance or explains the fee, and who has an opening soon.

Small things end the search: no mention of the concern they came with, a contact form that asks for too much, silence on fees and insurance, hours on your profile that disagree with your site. We fix those first, then build the pages that are missing.

Many are looking on behalf of someone else, a child, a partner or a parent, so we write pages that help them take the first step for another person. Tell us in Step 1 who you most want to help.

Where people stop

  1. No page for their concernThey assume you do not work with it and move on.
  2. A long intake formToo many questions before any contact feels like a barrier.
  3. Unclear insurance and feesSilence on cost sends people to a practice that answers it.

  • Couples and marriage counseling SEO

    Couples often search together, or one partner searches first and shares the page. We build a page that explains how you work with couples, whether you see them online, and what happens when only one partner is ready to start.

  • Child and teen therapy SEO

    Parents searching for a child's therapist want to know you understand young people and how you involve the family. We write for the parent reading at night, and keep your services for younger children and for teens on pages of their own.

  • Trauma and PTSD therapy SEO

Ethics and privacy

Marketing a therapy practice inside the rules

Your license comes first, and so does your clients' privacy. Federal rules forbid reviews that are fake, bought or written for someone, and any story of a result shown on your pages is read as typical unless the page says what clients generally experience. So we build your pages around your approach and your training rather than client stories, and keep every page free of details that could identify a client.

Licensing boards for counselors, psychologists and social workers set advertising rules of their own, including how you may describe your training, and we write inside the rules of your state. Send us Step 1, and we will go through the wording on your current pages with those rules in mind.

Which services come with mental health marketing, and what does the first page guarantee cover?

It covers every step from a search to a first appointment: your profiles, a page for each concern you treat, your website and how easy it is to reach you. The first page guarantee concerns rankings, never a promise of new clients; everything else we do is about turning a search into a first session.

Can you market my practice without client testimonials?

Yes, and for most therapists that is the better way. Your pages can build trust through your approach, your training, clear information on fees and insurance, and a calm first contact, with no client stories needed.

Do you help with online therapy across a whole state?

Will my pages mention insurance and fees?

They should, because cost is one of the first questions people have. We write what you tell us about the plans you accept and how fees work, and nothing you have not confirmed.
