How does local SEO work for a Houston dental office?

Local SEO covers the map and every search that names a place, such as a dentist in the Heights or near the Galleria. Google picks who appears there using signals it can check: a complete Business Profile, recent reviews, one page per office and matching practice details across the web. We put those in order first.

Is dental marketing in Houston the same thing as your first page guarantee?

No. Dental marketing is the whole path to your chair, from referrals and reviews to your website and your Business Profile. Our first page guarantee covers one part of it: your practice's placement on Google's first page. It never promises new patients. We build the rest around it, with website design and conversion in our Full Suite.

Do you build pages for orthodontists, endodontists and other specialists in Houston?

Yes. Orthodontic, endodontic, periodontic, pediatric, oral surgery and prosthodontic offices each get pages about their own procedures, in the words patients type. If referrals matter to you, we add a page for the general dentists who send you cases.

Can a Houston general dentist say the practice offers braces or implants?

Yes. Texas lets a general dentist advertise services in the specialty areas as long as the ad clearly discloses that the dentist is a general dentist. So your pages say General Dentist beside the dentist's name wherever braces or implants appear, and never suggest a specialty you do not hold.

Our patients come from outside the city limits too. Is that covered?

Yes. Patients cross from Bellaire, West University Place, Pasadena and the unincorporated suburbs without a second thought, so your pages follow where your patients actually live.

Should our pages list the insurance plans we accept?

Yes. Many patients search with their plan in mind and leave a page that never mentions it. We list the plans you take on your treatment pages and your Business Profile, and keep the list current when your contracts change.

Where our figures come from

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau: Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (July 1, 2025); American Community Survey, 2020-2024 5-year estimates; 2022 Economic Census; 2025 TIGER/Line city boundaries; Census Geocoder (to place each practice address on the map).

  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS): NPPES NPI Registry, September 26, 2026.

How the figures marked † were derived

  • Net migration into Harris County † is derived: the Census Bureau's yearly net migration estimates for Harris County (moved in minus moved out), added up from April 2020 to July 2025.

  • Dentists with a practice address in the city (at least) † is derived: every active dentist in the NPPES registry with a practice address in Houston's ZIP codes was placed on the map; the count keeps those inside the 2025 city boundary, so it is a floor, and it includes dental school faculty and residents (about 108 at one university address), not only dentists in private practice.

  • Dentists with a Houston address outside the city † is derived: the same records whose practice address says Houston but falls outside the city boundary, counted by the Texas city or census place each falls in.

  • Dentists in the city by main field † is derived: the dentists inside the city limits, counted by the main field each lists in NPPES (self-reported, not a license count).

  • Average receipts per dental office † is derived: all 2022 receipts of Houston's dental offices divided by the number of those offices.

  • Residents per dental office † is derived: the city's July 2022 population estimate divided by the 2022 count of dental offices in the city.

  • Share who speak Spanish at home † is derived: residents 5 and over who speak Spanish at home divided by all residents 5 and over.

Rules cited on this page

  • Code of Federal Regulations, title 16, part 465 (FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials): 16 CFR 465.2.

  • Texas Administrative Code, title 22, part 5, chapter 108 (State Board of Dental Examiners: Professional Conduct): 22 TAC §108.52.

  • Texas Administrative Code, title 22, part 5, chapter 108 (State Board of Dental Examiners: Professional Conduct): 22 TAC §108.55.
