Florida's dental ad rules

Florida's dental advertising law, and how we work inside it

A ranking is never worth a complaint to the Board of Dentistry, so our pages for your practice are written to Florida’s dental advertising law from the first draft. The law bars laudatory statements about a dentist, claims likely to create unjustified expectations of results, comparisons of your care with other practices and ads that play on patients’ fears, and misleading advertising is grounds for discipline.

A dentist may hold out as a specialist only with the ADA-approved specialty training and board standing the statute sets; otherwise a practice emphasis may be announced only with a clearly set-off notice that the area is not a recognized specialty. Every ad names the Florida-licensed dentist responsible for it, a fee comes with a minimum-fee notice, and a free or discounted offer carries the statutory patient notice and its dates. A general dentist who announces a service says a general dentist provides it, and your website and its SEO must stay truthful. Answer Step 1 and your current pages get checked against every one of these rules.

Can a Florida general dentist advertise implants or aligners?

Yes, your practice may say it offers them. Florida’s restriction is on presenting yourself as a specialist without the credentials the statute sets, so your pages name the service as part of general dentistry or, for a practice emphasis, add the notice the law requires.

Is dental marketing in Florida more than getting found on Google?

Yes. Dental marketing takes in search, plus your Business Profile, reviews, referral sources and a site patients can book on. Our first page guarantee covers the search part and concerns where you rank, never how many new patients arrive; the rest of the work turns that search into a visit.

Do you cover practices outside South Florida?

Yes. Pages follow the towns your patients come from, whether that is the Panhandle, Central Florida or the Gulf Coast, and smaller towns often have fewer practices answering those searches well.

How do Florida’s rules affect before-and-after photos and reviews?

They shape what a page may claim. We use only your own consented cases, avoid laudatory wording and promises of results, keep any testimonial to what patients typically experience, and keep review replies factual and silent about patient details.

Can my practice’s pages be written in Spanish?

Yes. If your dentists and front desk care for patients in Spanish, we write Spanish pages with the same care and legal wording as your English pages, and a Spanish speaker on your staff approves each one before it is published.

I hold a specialty credential. How will my pages show it?

Accurately. Where you meet Florida’s specialist standard, your pages can say so; where you do not, we describe your focus as a practice emphasis with the required notice, or simply as a service you provide.

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau: Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (July 1, 2025); American Community Survey, 2020-2024 5-year estimates; 2022 Economic Census; Census Geocoder (to place each practice address on the map).

  • U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics: Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, 2025 annual averages.

  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS): NPPES NPI Registry, September 26, 2026.

How the figures marked † were derived

  • dentists with a practice address in the county † is derived: the Florida dentist records whose practice address places inside Miami-Dade County; a floor.

  • People who moved to Florida in a typical year † is derived: residents one year and over who lived in another state or abroad a year earlier, added together; a typical year of the 2020-2024 surveys.

  • Dentists by county † is derived: the same Florida dentist records, counted by the county each practice address falls in.

  • Dentists with a practice address in Florida (at least) † is derived: every active dentist in the NPPES registry whose practice address places inside Florida; a floor that also counts faculty and residents, not only dentists in private practice.

  • Dentists in Florida by main field † is derived: the Florida dentists counted by the main field each lists (self-reported, not a license count).

  • Average receipts per dental office † is derived: Florida dental offices' 2022 receipts divided by the number of Florida dental offices.

  • Residents per dental office † is derived: Florida's July 2022 population estimate divided by the 2022 count of Florida dental offices.

  • Share who speak Spanish at home † is derived: residents 5 and over who speak Spanish at home divided by all residents 5 and over.
