Before they book

What Miami patients check before booking a dentist

Few people type just the word dentist. They look for a Saturday opening, help with a chipped tooth today, braces for a teenager, a practice that takes their plan and speaks their language, and they decide from reviews, photos and hours before anyone at your front desk hears from them.

Any one of those checks can lose you the booking: a page that loads slowly, hours that differ from your profile, silence on which plans you accept, a Spanish speaker who finds only English. New arrivals move on fastest, because nobody has vouched for you yet.

Tourists and seasonal visitors add a third group worth planning for: a lost crown on vacation sends them looking for any practice that can see them this week, and they judge you entirely by what your profile and pages say.

We repair what patients meet first before we build anything new: the broken booking link, the missing holiday hours, the plan list nobody updated. Fill in Step 1 and point us to the patients you would most like to see.

  • Periodontist SEO

    Someone replacing old dentures or planning a full-mouth restoration may research for months. Each option gets a page that follows the whole path from consultation to fitting, so your answers are already there when they decide.

  • Dental implant SEO

    Implant shoppers compare one tooth against a full arch and ask about price early. Every implant page we write states whether a general dentist or a specialist places them at your practice, and shows your own consented cases.

  • Cosmetic dentist SEO

    Veneers, whitening and bonding sell on photographs, and stock smiles convince nobody. Your cosmetic pages are built on cases you actually treated, shared with consent, and worded without the praise and promises Florida bars in dental ads.

  • Emergency dentist SEO

    Pain does not wait for Monday, and people in it book the first practice that shows it is open. We build your emergency page, keep weekend and holiday hours exact on every listing, and make the way to reach you obvious on a phone.

  • General and family dentist SEO

    Most families want a single dentist near home who sees every age, speaks their language and can fit a new patient in soon. Each service you offer gets a page, and when a service overlaps a specialty, the page makes clear that a general dentist performs it.

Where does SEO end and dental marketing begin for a Miami practice?

Where our figures come from

Data sources

  • U.S. Census Bureau: Population Estimates, Vintage 2025 (July 1, 2025); American Community Survey, 2020-2024 5-year estimates; 2022 Economic Census; 2025 TIGER/Line city boundaries; Census Geocoder (to place each practice address on the map).

  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS): NPPES NPI Registry, September 26, 2026.

How the figures marked † were derived

  • People who moved to Miami in a typical year (at least) † is derived: residents one year and over who lived in another county, another state or abroad a year earlier, added together; a move from elsewhere in Miami-Dade County is not counted, so it is a floor; a typical year of the 2020-2024 surveys.

  • Dentists with a practice address in the city (at least) † is derived: every active dentist in the NPPES registry with a practice address in the Miami ZIP codes was placed on the map; the count keeps those inside the 2025 city boundary, so it is a floor, and it includes university faculty and residents, not only dentists in private practice.

  • Dentists with a Miami address outside the city † is derived: the same records whose practice address says Miami but falls outside the city boundary, counted by the Florida city or census place each falls in.

  • Dentists in the city by main field † is derived: the dentists inside the city limits, counted by the main field each lists in NPPES (self-reported, not a license count).

  • Average receipts per dental office † is derived: Miami dental offices' 2022 receipts divided by the number of those offices.

  • Residents per dental office † is derived: Miami's July 2022 population estimate divided by the 2022 count of dental offices with paid staff.

  • Share who speak Spanish at home † is derived: residents 5 and over who speak Spanish at home divided by all residents 5 and over.

Rules cited on this page

  • ADA Principles of Ethics and Code of Professional Conduct (2026): section 5.I (version dated 2026-02-18).

  • Florida Administrative Code, rule chapter 64B5-4 (Board of Dentistry: Advertising): FAC 64B5-4.002.

  • Florida Statutes, chapter 466 (Dentistry, Dental Hygiene, and Dental Laboratories): FS 466.019, FS 466.0282.
