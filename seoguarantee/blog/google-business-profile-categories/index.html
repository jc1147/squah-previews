Google Business Profile Categories: How to Choose the Right Ones

The primary category is the strongest map signal local experts can name. How to choose it, when to add more, where the category list lives, and the mistakes that cost rankings or trigger suspensions.

Choose one primary category that says what your business is, add only the few additional categories that describe your core business, and never use a category as a keyword. Google says the categories you select affect your local ranking [1], and the local search experts Whitespark surveyed for 2026 scored the primary category higher than any other local pack factor [2].

The short answer

Direct answer

A Google Business Profile has one primary category and room for up to nine more [3]. Google says the categories you select affect your local ranking [1], and in Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts, the primary category scored 227, the highest of any local pack factor [2]. Pick the most specific category that says what you are, then add as few as you need.

Stat

In Whitespark's 2026 local pack ranking, the primary category scored 227, just ahead of proximity to the searcher (225) and keywords in the business title (223) [2].

That third factor is not an invitation. Adding keywords to your business name breaks Google's rules, as the mistakes section below explains. The survey also measures expert opinion rather than Google's code, since none of the experts has special access to Google's local algorithm [2]. But it agrees with Google's own statement, and it tells you where to start: with the category, before photos, posts or anything else. Our guide to local SEO covers the rest of the map results.

Why the primary category matters most

Direct answer

The primary category is your main statement to Google of what the business is. Google tells you to select the primary category that best describes your business, and you can't create your own [1]. Whitespark's experts rank it above proximity and every other local pack factor, and they rank an incorrect primary category the second most damaging negative factor, behind only a profile marked permanently closed [2].

Google defines relevance as how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for [4]. The primary category is the plainest statement of that match. In the editor, whichever category sits in the first field is your primary category [3].

Categories mostly move the map results. Whitespark's 2026 weights put Business Profile signals at 32% of local pack rankings but only 7% of local organic rankings, where on-page factors (33%) and links (24%) lead [2]. The website still matters for categories, though: Google says it can detect category information from your website and from mentions of your business across the web [5]. A page for each core service, named the way your category is named, keeps the two telling the same story. Whitespark's experts rank a dedicated page for each service first for local organic results and second for AI search visibility [2].

Additional categories: when to add them

Direct answer

Add an additional category only when it names a core part of what the business is. Google's test is that the category should complete the statement "This business IS a", not "this business HAS a" [5]. Google also says to choose the fewest categories it takes to describe your core business [5]. Services, amenities and features belong in their own fields, not in categories.

Whitespark's experts rank additional categories eighth among local pack factors [2], so a true second category is worth adding. A dental practice IS a dentist; it HAS a teeth-whitening service. The first is a category; the second belongs in the services list.

Google also saves you redundant picks. Its guidelines explain that a specific category implies more general ones: select "Golf Resort", and Google implicitly includes "Resort Hotel", "Hotel" and "Golf Course", so you can skip any category that is redundant with a more specific one you chose [5].

Services deserve the care people put into categories. Google suggests services tailored to your business type, and a listed service may be highlighted on your profile when people search for it [6]. Whitespark's experts ranked the presence of Google's predefined services 22nd among local pack factors [2].

How to find the right category

Direct answer

You choose from Google's own list inside the profile editor, and you can't invent a category [1]. Google doesn't publish that list as a public page, but third-party lists mirror it; PlePer's full US list ran to 4,044 entries in September 2026 [7]. If you can't find a category that fits, Google says to choose one that's more general [5].

Here is what exists for five common practice types, from PlePer's US list [7]:

Then test your choice against real searches. A verified profile's Performance report lists the search terms people used to find your business, updated at the start of each month [8]. If those terms keep naming a service your categories don't cover, you have found either a missing category or a service to add. Dentists will find the details in our guide to Google Business Profile for dentists, and other practices on our industry pages.

How to check competitors' categories

Direct answer

For each phrase you want to win, list the businesses Google shows in the map results from your own market and record their primary and additional categories. The category they share suggests what Google treats as relevant for that search. If the businesses already showing use a more specific category than yours, that is a gap worth closing first.

Search from your own market, not a national setting. Google measures distance from the searcher [4], and national checks mislead: in one national capture for "near me seo", Google's local pack included a French agency [9]. A customer searching from your city would likely see a different pack.

Record each business's categories against the service the phrase names. In our competitor measurements, categories are the first map input we record for each business, followed by distance from the search point, review count, rating, recent reviews and profile completeness. The method is laid out in our guide to SEO competitor analysis.

Mistakes that cost rankings or cause suspensions

Direct answer

Five mistakes do the most damage: a wrong primary category, which Whitespark's experts rank the second-worst negative factor [2]; categories used as keywords, which Google's guidelines forbid [5]; categories for things you merely offer; keywords stuffed into the business name, which can get a profile suspended [5]; and frequent category edits, which can trigger re-verification [1].

  • Keyword-stuffed names. Sterling Sky found that adding a keyword to a business name "dramatically improved ranking", and removing it undid the gain [10]. Google says unnecessary information in a business name isn't permitted and could result in suspension [5], and Whitespark's experts list keyword stuffing in the name eighth among suspension risks [2]. Use your real-world name.
  • Two profiles in one category at one address. Whitespark's experts rank a same-category profile at the same address third among negative factors [2]. Google's rule is one profile per business [5], with separate profiles allowed for individual practitioners such as dentists and doctors [5].
  • Categories chosen for a season. Editing a category can prompt Google to ask you to verify the business again [1]. Settle the list once, and change it only when the business changes.

Frequently asked questions

How many categories can a Google Business Profile have?

One primary category plus up to nine additional ones, ten in all [3]. Google's guidelines add that you should choose the fewest categories it takes to describe your overall core business [5], so most businesses need far fewer than ten. Whitespark's experts value relevant additional categories, ranking them eighth among local pack factors [2], but a category that fails Google's "IS a" test doesn't belong.

Can I change my primary category?

Yes. Edit your profile, and whichever category sits in the first field becomes the primary [3]. Google warns that adding or editing a category can prompt you to verify the business again [1], and because categories affect local ranking, your map rankings can move after the change [1]. Make the change once, deliberately, and record your map rankings before and after.

Do categories affect rankings?

Yes. Google says the categories you select affect your local ranking [1]. In Whitespark's 2026 survey, the primary category scored highest of all local pack factors and additional categories ranked eighth [2]. Categories matter far less for organic results: the same survey weights Business Profile signals at 32% of local pack rankings but 7% of local organic rankings [2].

Where is the full list of categories?

Google doesn't publish its category list as a public page; you choose from it inside the profile editor, and you can't create your own [1]. Third-party lists mirror it: PlePer's full US list had 4,044 entries in September 2026 [7]. Use a list to shortlist candidates, then confirm each one in your own profile before relying on it.

What if my service has no matching category?

Google says that if you can't find a category for your business, you should choose one that's more general [5]. Then describe the specific service in your profile's services section, where a listed service may be highlighted to searchers [6], and give it a dedicated page on your website. Google says it can also detect category information from your website and from mentions across the web [5].

Categories are the first thing we measure

Categories are the first map input we record for every business already on page one for a client's phrases.
