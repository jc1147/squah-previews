How Patients Choose a Dentist, and How to Get More Dental Patients

How patients find and choose a dentist, from named 2024-2026 surveys and ADA Health Policy Institute data, and the order of work that gets a practice more new patients.

Many patients start with a Google search, compare a short list on reviews, star ratings, distance and cost, and then check the website before they book. In Tebra's 2025 survey of 3,964 US adults, 56% started their research on Google and 79% read reviews before choosing a provider [1]. Getting more dental patients means winning each of those steps, in the order patients take them.

The short answer

Direct answer

Patients find a dentist through Google search, the map results and, increasingly, AI answers. They compare reviews, star ratings, distance, insurance and cost, then check the website before they book. The surveys agree on the pattern: in Tebra's 2025 survey, 79% of adults read reviews before choosing a provider and 53% won't consider one with fewer than four stars [1].

Where patients start: search, maps and AI answers

Direct answer

Most start with Google. In Tebra's 2025 survey, 56% of US adults began their research for a provider on Google [1]. Google says its local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and it applies the searcher's location even when no place is typed [5][6]. AI tools are a growing starting point: 45% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey had used one for local recommendations [7].

Popularity is where a practice has the most control. Google says: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." It also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking [5]. Distance you can't change; relevance comes from your categories, services and pages.

AI answers add a new front door. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, ChatGPT was the tool most used for business recommendations (31%), followed by Google's AI Mode (23%), and 88% of AI users said they check the reviews or sources an AI tool cites [7]. Google says its AI responses can include information about local businesses, and that Business Profiles "can help your products and services to be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results" [8].

Directories feed those answers too. In a small BrightLocal test of 20 searches across 10 industries and four AI tools, ChatGPT "exclusively sourced information from ten different dental directories" [9]. Keep your listings on the major dental directories as accurate as your Business Profile; our guide to the Google Business Profile for dentists covers the profile itself.

What patients compare: reviews, stars and replies

Direct answer

Reviews come first. In Tebra's 2025 survey, 79% of adults read reviews before choosing a provider and 53% won't consider one with fewer than four stars [1]. In Press Ganey's survey of 1,000 healthcare consumers, people read 4.7 reviews on average and most treated four stars as the lowest acceptable rating [3]. Recent reviews count most, and patients notice replies.

Stat

62% of the 803 patients in Tebra's September 2025 survey had avoided booking with a provider because of negative reviews [2].

Old reviews lose force fast. In Press Ganey's survey, 64.8% of consumers said reviews over a year old are irrelevant [3], and 74% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey look for reviews written in the last three months [4]. A steady flow of new reviews tells these readers more than a long list of old ones.

Google reviews carry the most weight: in Tebra's later survey they ranked highest in influence, at 83% [2]. Even referred patients check: 83.5% of Press Ganey's respondents at least occasionally turn to online reviews to validate a referral [3].

Replies change minds. 70% of Tebra's 2025 respondents said a provider's reply to a negative review improved their opinion [1], and in its later report 71% of patients said they trust providers more when they respond, while 45% of providers said they never respond to negative reviews [2]. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 80% of consumers said they're likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews [4].

Reply in general terms. HHS's Office for Civil Rights has penalized dental practices over patient details disclosed in review replies; New Vision Dental paid $23,000, and OCR's director called such disclosures "a clear NO." [10] Our guide to HIPAA marketing rules shows the wording the ADA recommends, and whether Google reviews help SEO covers the ranking side.

Cost, insurance and competition

Direct answer

Cost stops more people from getting dental care than any other kind of care. The ADA Health Policy Institute found that 13% of people reported cost barriers to dental care in 2023, against 4-5% for other health services, and that only 45% of the US population visited a dentist in 2022 [11]. Show prices or ranges, the plans you accept and payment options where patients can find them.

Insurance matters too: 62% of adults had private dental insurance in 2022, and only 40% of adults aged 19 to 64 saw a dentist that year [11]. Google said in 2021 that people searching for a specific provider can check which insurance networks it may accept [12]. We found no Google source confirming that dental carriers appear there, so publish your accepted plans on your own site.

Patients have choices, and more in some states than others. There were 59.5 dentists per 100,000 people in 2024, from 40.2 in Arkansas to 103.2 in the District of Columbia, and 16.1% of dentists were affiliated with a dental support organization in 2024, up from 7.2% in 2015 [13].

Capacity varies. In the ADA Health Policy Institute's panel for the second quarter of 2026 (589 of 2,432 invited dentists responded), about a quarter of dentists said they were not busy enough, new patients waited an average of 13.9 days for an appointment, and consumer spending on dental care was up only 1% over 12 months [14]. If you can see new patients this week, say so on your booking page.

The website and the first booking

Direct answer

The website is where a patient confirms the choice. In Tebra's 2025 survey, 44% of US adults said a practice's website influences their decision [1]. Give each treatment its own page with the details patients compare: who does the treatment, the price or accepted plans, and a way to book online. Our list of dental website must-haves covers the rest.

Booking pages need care. HHS guidance says tracking tools on pages where people schedule appointments without logging in may have access to protected health information in certain circumstances [15]. Keep ad pixels off those pages until your counsel has reviewed the setup.

Name the dentists. Google's guidelines treat dentists as individual practitioners, whose Business Profiles may include a title such as Dr. [16]. On the site, show each dentist's photo, training and the treatments they provide.

Keeping patients: experience and reviews

Direct answer

No survey in our research measures why dental patients stay or leave, so start with what the review data show: patients read recent reviews and notice replies. Ask every patient for an honest review, reply to every review in general terms that never confirm who is a patient, and follow Google's and the FTC's rules on how you ask.

Google bans incentives for any review, selective requests to happy customers only, and pressure to review while the customer is on your premises [17]. The FTC's rule on consumer reviews, in force since October 21, 2024, bans incentives conditioned on a positive or negative review, and FTC staff say asking only happy customers "could violate the FTC Act" [18]. The ADA advises that staff not post reviews and that practices not pay for them, and notes some jurisdictions may prohibit soliciting reviews from patients [19].

There is room to grow. With only 45% of the US population visiting a dentist in 2022 [11], more than half of Americans did not see one that year. Recall and reactivation messages reach your own patients, so run any that promote a service past HIPAA's marketing rule with counsel.

What to change first

Direct answer

Start where patients start. Fix the Google Business Profile, then build a steady flow of recent reviews with replies, then give every treatment its own page with prices or plans and online booking. Measure each step against the practices already in the map results for your main treatments: their review count, rating and recent pace, their categories and their pages.

Frequently asked questions

How do most patients find a new dentist?

Most start on Google. In Tebra's 2025 survey of 3,964 US adults, 56% began their research for a provider on Google [1]. The map results weigh relevance, distance and popularity, including reviews [5]. AI tools are growing: 45% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey had used AI for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier [7]. Even referred patients check: 83.5% of Press Ganey's respondents at least occasionally use reviews to validate a referral [3].

Do patients read reviews before choosing a dentist?

Yes. In Tebra's 2025 survey, 79% of adults read reviews before choosing a provider [1], and Press Ganey found people read 4.7 reviews on average before deciding [3]. Neither survey is dental-only, but Tebra's later survey of 803 patients, which included dental patients, found 62% had avoided booking with a provider because of negative reviews [2].

How many stars does a dental practice need?

Four is the line in every survey we found. 53% of Tebra's 2025 respondents won't consider a provider with fewer than four stars [1], most Press Ganey respondents treat four as the lowest acceptable rating [3], and 68% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey only use businesses rated four stars or higher [4]. Recency matters too: 74% look for reviews from the last three months [4].

Does replying to reviews help win patients?

Patients say it does. 70% of Tebra's 2025 respondents said a provider's reply to a negative review improved their opinion [1], and 80% of BrightLocal's 2026 respondents said they're likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews [4]. Keep replies general: OCR has penalized dental practices over patient details in review replies, including a $23,000 settlement with New Vision Dental [10].

How can a practice get more new patients?

Win the steps patients take: a complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of recent reviews with replies, a page for each treatment with prices or plans, and online booking. Then measure the practices already in the map results for your main treatments and close the gaps in reviews, categories and pages. Our dental local SEO guide covers the order of work.
