Near Me SEO: How "Near Me" Searches Really Work

Near me searches run on the searcher's location, not on having the words near me on your page. What Google does with them, what actually helps you appear, and how to measure it properly.

Near me searches run on location, not on the words "near me" on your page. Google already knows roughly where the searcher is and uses that to show nearby results [1]. So near me SEO is local SEO done properly: a precise Business Profile, a steady flow of recent reviews, being open when people search, and a real presence close to your customers.

The short answer

Direct answer

To show up in near me searches, you don't need the words "near me" on your pages. Google uses the searcher's location to show nearby results, whether or not they type "near me" or a place. What helps is what ranks the map pack: the right primary category, a complete and accurate profile, recent reviews with text, real hours and a real location close to the searcher [1][2][3].

Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity" [2]. The words "near me" tell Google the searcher wants something close, but Google measures the distance itself. Your job is to be the most relevant, best-known match nearby. For the full playbook behind that, see our local SEO guide.

How Google handles "near me"

Direct answer

Google uses your location to show nearby results even when a search names no place, and it treats a plain search for a type of business as a search for one near you. If the searcher hasn't shared a location, Google estimates it. Distance from the searcher then becomes one of the three factors behind the map pack, alongside relevance and popularity [1][2].

Google's location help page makes the point with an example: "if you search for coffee shops, you're likely searching for coffee shops near you" [1]. It applies your location "even if you didn't include a location in your search" [1]. That is why the words "near me" add little Google doesn't already know: it applies location either way.

The habit has been building for years. In 2018 Think with Google reported that smartphone users "were dropping location qualifiers like ZIP codes and neighborhoods from local searches", and that near me searches were shifting toward a specific thing, in a specific area, in a specific period of time [4]. Its figures compared US mobile searches from July to December 2015 with the same months of 2017:

Stat

Mobile searches for "___ near me today/tonight" grew more than 900%, and for "___ near me now" more than 150%, between July-December 2015 and July-December 2017 (Google data, US, published 2018) [4]

Searches pairing "can I buy" or "to buy" with near me grew more than 500% over the same two years [4]. These are old figures, and our research found no newer ones from Google. They still show where near me searches were heading: toward something specific, somewhere close, soon.

Do you need "near me" on your pages?

Direct answer

Usually not. Google applies the searcher's location itself, so writing "near me" on a page doesn't move you closer to anyone. Name your services and the places you really serve in your titles and headings, as Google advises for any page. In one agency's reported case, adding "near you" wording helped organic rankings but did nothing for the map pack [1][5][6].

Google's general advice is to "Use words that people would use to look for your content, and place those words in prominent locations on the page, such as the title and main heading of a page" [5]. For a local business, those words are your services and your places.

Three things to avoid:

  1. "Near me" in your business name. Google says unnecessary information in the name isn't permitted and can get the profile suspended [7].
  2. A near me page for every town. Google's spam policies treat "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse [8].
  3. Stuffing the phrase. In Sterling Sky's 2025 near-me report, "near you" wording on one business's site helped its organic rankings, but it "didn't move the needle at all for local pack rankings" [6]. It is one case, but it matches how Google describes distance.

Your own data shows which near me phrases already bring people to you. The Performance report in your Business Profile lists the search terms people used to find you, updated at the start of each month [9], and Google says autocomplete predictions reflect real searches and vary with the location a search comes from [10]. Our guide to local SEO keyword research shows how to turn them into a phrase list.

What helps you show up: profile, reviews, proximity

Direct answer

Near me searches are local searches, so the map-pack levers decide who appears. The strongest are a precise primary category, proximity, a real address in the city being searched, being open at the time of the search, good ratings and a steady flow of recent reviews with text. Sterling Sky found this month's reviews mattered more than total reviews [3][6].

  • Category and completeness. Google says the categories you select affect your local ranking, and that complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show up [11][2]. In Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts, the primary category is the highest-scored map-pack factor (227) [3].
  • Proximity and address. Proximity to the searcher scored 225 and a physical address in the city being searched 213 [3]. You can't move, but you can make sure your map pin sits exactly on your entrance; pin placement scored 165 [3].
  • Open now. Being open at the time of the search scored 189 [3]. Sterling Sky first reported that effect in December 2023, and Google hasn't confirmed it [12]. Never list hours you don't keep; Google asks for your real customer-facing hours [7].
  • Recent reviews with text. Sterling Sky and Places Scout studied 8,186 businesses across 200 US cities and five service searches, in a correlational study of near me rankings. The number of reviews gained that month mattered more than the total, and reviews with text had a stronger effect [6]. The same report describes a business whose rankings "fell off a cliff" after 18 days without new reviews: an anecdote, not a rule [6].

Your ratings can also reach searchers directly. When we captured Google's results for "near me seo", searched US-wide on September 27, 2026, the AI Overview named two of the three businesses in the map pack along with their star ratings and review counts, and linked neither [13]. One observation proves nothing general, but it shows profile data traveling into AI answers. For step-by-step work on the profile, see our guides to Google Business Profile optimization and how to rank higher on Google Maps.

Service-area businesses and near me

Direct answer

If you travel to customers, you can still appear for near me searches. Google says service-area businesses should hide their address and list up to 20 service areas, within about two hours' drive, never as a radius. The evidence on hidden addresses is mixed: one large study found a negative correlation with ranking, but Google's rule still decides [7][14][6].

Sterling Sky's near-me study found "a negative correlation between hiding your address and ranking well in the local pack" [6]. In a separate case, one of its clients lost rankings when its address was hidden and regained them when it was shown again [15], while Whitespark's Darren Shaw thinks the effect is a bug rather than a ranking factor, a view he says he appears to hold alone [3]. Correlation isn't cause, and showing an address customers can't visit goes against Google's guidelines.

Two more rules: one profile per service-area business [14], and service areas that "shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time" [7], a suspension risk on Whitespark's list [3]. Service-area businesses compete on everything else above: category, recent reviews, accurate hours, and a website page for each service and each area they really serve.

Measuring near me visibility from the right spot

Direct answer

A near me ranking only exists from a location, so measure from several points where your customers actually are, record the date and device, and read the map pack, the organic results and any AI answer separately. A check from your office or a national tool will mislead you: in our US-wide capture for "near me seo", the map pack included an agency based in France [13].

Google says its results depend on signals including "your location and settings" [16], and Search Console's help notes that your position for a specific search can differ from the average because of your search history and location [17]. So:

  1. Pick the phrases: every service, with and without "near me", "open now" and your place names.
  2. Pick the points: your door, the edges of your trade area, and the neighborhoods you most want to win.
  3. Record each place separately: the map pack, the organic results and any AI answer.
  4. Repeat monthly: same points, same device.

Frequently asked questions

Should I put "near me" in my page titles?

Usually not. Google applies the searcher's location to local searches whether or not they type "near me" [1], so the phrase on your page doesn't bring you closer to anyone. Put your service and the places you serve in your titles and headings instead, as Google recommends for any page [5]. Never add "near me" to your Business Profile name; extra words there can get it suspended [7].

Why do I show up near my office but not across town?

Because distance is measured from each searcher. Google ranks local results mainly on relevance, distance and popularity [2], so someone across town sees businesses closer to them. Proximity to the searcher is the second-highest-scored map-pack factor in Whitespark's 2026 survey [3]. To reach farther, strengthen what you control: your category, your reviews, and the pages and links that build your prominence.

Can a service-area business rank for near me searches?

Yes. Google lets businesses that travel to customers hide their address and set up to 20 service areas within about two hours' drive [7][14]. Sterling Sky's study found a negative correlation between hiding an address and ranking well, but that's a correlation, and Google's rule still applies [6]. Compete on category, recent reviews, accurate hours and a website page for each service and area.

How far does "near me" reach?

Google's ranking page gives no radius. It defines distance simply as how far each business is from the person searching [2], so in practice your reach depends on how many relevant, well-known businesses sit closer to that searcher. The two-hour limit you may have read about applies to the service areas on your profile, not to how far a search reaches [7]. Measure from several points to find your real edge.

Does "near me" change what AI answers show?

It can. OpenAI says ChatGPT may use an approximate location based on your IP address to give local results [19], and Google says its AI answers can include information about local businesses [20]. In our capture for "near me seo", Google's AI Overview named two of the three map-pack businesses with their ratings and review counts [13]. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of consumers had used AI tools for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier [21].
