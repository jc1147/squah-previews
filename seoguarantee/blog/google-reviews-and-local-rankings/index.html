Do Google Reviews Help SEO and Local Rankings?

Reviews move the map results far more than organic rankings. What Google and the 2026 surveys say about count, rating, recency and replies, and the Google and FTC rules on collecting them.

Yes, above all in the map results. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking [1], and the local search experts Whitespark surveyed for 2026 weight reviews at 20% of map-pack rankings, against 6% for ordinary local web results [2]. How you collect reviews matters as much as how many you have, because Google's review policy and a 2024 FTC rule both rule out the shortcuts.

The short answer, in Google's own words

Direct answer

Google's help page on local ranking says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and that popularity draws on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. In Google's words: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." The same page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking [1].

That is about the map results. For organic results, reviews count for much less:

Stat

Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts weights reviews at 20% of local pack (map) rankings, 6% of local organic rankings and 16% of AI search visibility [2].

The survey measures expert opinion, not Google's code; none of the experts has special access to Google's local algorithm [2]. Read with Google's statement, the answer is clear: reviews are a major lever on Google Maps, a minor one for organic rankings and a notable one for AI answers. The rest of the map picture is in our guide to local SEO.

Count, rating, recency and velocity

Direct answer

The count is one of four things that matter. In Whitespark's 2026 survey, high Google star ratings ranked sixth among local pack factors, the number of Google reviews with text ninth and review recency eleventh, and a sustained flow of reviews over time, rather than bursts, also made its leading list [2]. Count, rating, recency and a steady pace work together.

Recent reviews may matter more than the total. Sterling Sky and Places Scout analyzed 8,186 businesses in 200 US cities and found that the reviews a business gained in the current month mattered more than its total, and that reviews with text had a stronger effect than bare star ratings [4]. The study is correlational: it shows what ranking businesses have in common, not how Google's code works.

Small tests point the same way. In one Sterling Sky test, businesses that went from 9 to 10 reviews saw a small but noticeable Maps lift for their main keyword, while going from 10 to 11 did nothing; each test used three businesses [5]. Read results like that as a sign that thin profiles have the most to gain, not as a Google rule.

Velocity cuts both ways. Whitespark's experts rank receiving too many Google reviews too fast 12th among suspension risks [2], and since April 2026 Google says a spike in reviews can lead it to pause new reviews on the profile, alert the owner and display a notification banner [6]. A steady flow from real customers is the goal. Our guide on how to get Google reviews covers the asking.

What reviewers write: services and places

Direct answer

What customers write seems to matter, but you can't script it. Sterling Sky's near-me study found reviews with text had a stronger ranking effect than star-only ratings [4]. Yet when Sterling Sky planted keywords in reviews for one business, its map ranking did not improve [7], and Google's policy bars businesses from asking customers to include specific content [8].

Google tightened that part of its policy this year. Internet Archive captures show two clauses first appearing between February 15 and February 23, 2026 [9]: businesses may not ask staff to collect a set number of reviews, and may not ask for reviews with specific content, including content that names a staff member [8]. The policy also bars pressuring customers to leave a rating or review while they are on your premises [8].

That keyword test also used reviews from people who weren't customers, which Google's policy forbids anyway [7]. The practical answer: ask every customer for an honest review and let them describe the visit in their own words. A review that names the service someone received tells the next customer more than a star does. To tell Google what you do, start with the right Google Business Profile categories.

Replying to reviews

Direct answer

Reply to every review, briefly and in your own words. Google's review tips say to be conversational, not promotional [10]. Google checks replies before they post, which usually takes up to 10 minutes and sometimes up to 30 days, and customers can still change their review after reading your reply [11]. Whitespark's experts rate keywords in owner responses a ranking myth, so write for people [2].

Customers notice replies. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 89% of consumers said they expect business owners to respond to reviews, generic or templated replies put off 50%, and 19% expected a reply on the day they posted, up from 6% a year earlier [3].

Negative reviews deserve your calmest reply. Google's tips note that a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy [10]. Thank the reviewer, don't argue details in public, and offer a direct way to talk. Never threaten a reviewer: the FTC's rule bans suppressing reviews through unfounded legal threats, physical threats or intimidation [12].

Healthcare practices face one more rule. HHS's Office for Civil Rights has taken action over patient details disclosed in replies to online reviews. New Vision Dental paid $23,000, and OCR's director said providers cannot disclose patients' protected health information when responding to negative reviews: "This is a clear NO." [13] The ADA's model reply stays in broad "all patient" terms and never references a specific person or incident [14]. Our article on HIPAA marketing rules covers the rest.

What is not allowed: fake, bought or gated reviews

Direct answer

Google bans paid reviews, incentives of any kind (payment, discounts, free goods or services), review gating (discouraging negative reviews or asking only happy customers), pressure to review on your premises and requests for specific content [8]. The FTC's rule, in force since October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews, buying fake reviews and incentives conditioned on a positive or negative review [12][15].

The FTC announced the rule on August 14, 2024 [12], and FTC staff confirm it went into effect on October 21, 2024 [15]. The rule makes it an unfair or deceptive practice for a business to write, create or sell a review that misrepresents whether the reviewer exists or used the business, or to buy a review it knew or should have known misrepresents those things [16]. It also bans compensation or incentives conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment, positive or negative [12].

FTC staff draw two lines that Google draws tighter. The FTC's Q&A says the rule does not ban an incentive with no sentiment condition, and has no specific ban on asking only happy customers, though that practice could violate the FTC Act [15]. Google bans both outright [8].

Google enforces its side at scale:

Stat

Google blocked or removed more than 292 million policy-violating reviews in 2025 and removed more than 13 million fake Business Profiles [6], up from more than 240 million policy-violating reviews in 2024 [17].

Offers still circulate: 11% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey said they had been offered an incentive to write a positive review [3]. If an SEO vendor offers to "boost" your reviews, treat it as one of the clearest SEO red flags there is.

Reviews in AI answers

Direct answer

Reviews now feed AI answers as well. Whitespark's 2026 survey weights reviews at 16% of AI search visibility [2], and Google says Ask Maps, its conversational Maps feature, draws on reviews from more than 500 million contributors [18]. In one captured search, Google's AI Overview quoted two businesses' star ratings and review counts [19]. Genuine reviews can shape what AI tools say about you.

Customers use these tools. BrightLocal's 2026 AI survey found 45% of consumers had used AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier; ChatGPT was the tool most used for business recommendations (31%), followed by Google's AI Mode (23%) [20]. Most also check what the AI cites: 88% of AI users said they check whether a review is legitimate or where it came from [20].

Two cautions. That AI Overview was a single observation from one national search, not a pattern [19]. And SOCi's study of more than 350,000 locations, all from multi-location brands, found the locations ChatGPT recommended averaged 4.3-star ratings [21]. Frequently asked questions

How many Google reviews do I need to rank?

Google publishes no number. The useful benchmark is the businesses already in the map results for your main phrases: their review count, their rating and how many reviews they gained in the last 90 days. Close that gap at a steady pace. Customers apply their own floor as well: in BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 47% said they won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews [3].

Do review replies help rankings?

Google lists responding to reviews among its tips for local ranking, saying helpful replies can help your business stand out, but it doesn't call replies a ranking factor [1]. Whitespark's experts rate keywords in owner responses a myth [2]. Replies matter to the people reading them: 89% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey expect owners to respond [3]. Reply to every review for your future customers, and let the reviews themselves carry the ranking weight.

Can I offer a discount for reviews?

Not for Google reviews. Google's policy bans incentives such as payment, discounts, or free goods or services in exchange for a review [8]. Separately, the FTC's rule bans incentives conditioned on a review being positive or negative [12]. FTC staff say the rule does not reach incentives with no sentiment condition [15], but Google's ban applies to every review posted on Google.

Do negative reviews hurt local SEO?

Ratings matter. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking [1], and high ratings ranked sixth among Whitespark's 2026 local pack factors [2]. A few negatives among many positives are normal, and Google notes that a mix often feels more trustworthy [10]. Reply calmly, fix the cause, and never try to suppress a review with threats, which the FTC's rule bans [12].

Do keywords in reviews matter?

Reviews with text appear to carry more weight than bare star ratings in Sterling Sky's near-me study [4], but planting keywords did not help in its separate test [7]. Google's policy bars asking customers to include specific content [8]. Ask every customer for an honest review, and let them name the service they received in their own words.
