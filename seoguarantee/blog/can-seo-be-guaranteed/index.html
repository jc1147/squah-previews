Can SEO Be Guaranteed? The Honest Answer

Rankings can't be guaranteed, and Google says so. A first page share of an agreed phrase list, checked on set dates under written terms, can be. Here is the difference, and how to read the terms.

SEO rankings can't be guaranteed, but a first page result defined in written terms can be. No one outside Google controls where a page ranks, and Google says so; what an SEO provider can promise is a measured share of an agreed phrase list on the first page of Google by set dates, checked from your market.

That difference, between promising a position and promising a measured share, is the whole answer.

The short answer

Direct answer

No one can guarantee a ranking position, traffic or sales from SEO; anyone who does is promising something they don't control. A first page result can be promised honestly when it is defined as a share of an agreed phrase list, measured on set dates from your market, with its limits written down before work starts.

What Google says about ranking promises

Direct answer

Google's advice on hiring an SEO says no one can promise the top ranking, and warns about SEOs who promise rankings or claim a special tie to Google. It also tells owners to ask what results to expect, in what timeframe, and how success will be measured. A first page promise that answers those questions in writing is a different thing.

Google's line, word for word:

A first page promise should never suggest otherwise. The rest of that warning covers SEOs that claim to guarantee rankings, claim a special tie to Google or sell a paid fast lane into it [1]. Google goes on: "An SEO audit should be about giving you realistic estimates of improvement, and an estimate of the work involved." [1] It tells owners to walk away from anyone who promises the top result, and gives them questions to ask: "What kind of results do you expect to see, and in what timeframe? How do you measure your success?" [1]

Money does not buy position either: Google says it "never accepts money to include or rank sites in our search results", and there is no way to pay for a better local ranking [1] [2]. Even a page that does everything right has no assured spot on the first page: "Indexing and serving isn't guaranteed." [3]

Google's John Mueller, in a Google podcast transcribed by Search Engine Journal, called ranking or traffic promises "usually a red flag", because no one can reach into Google's systems and change the rankings [4]. The warnings have a history behind them. In 2018 the Federal Trade Commission charged a Florida-based scheme with falsely claiming to represent Google and falsely promising small businesses first-page placement in Google search results [5]. Our guide to SEO scams lists the other warning signs.

What cannot be promised: positions, traffic, sales

Direct answer

A position can't be promised because Google's systems decide it and change it several times a year. Traffic and sales can't be promised because they depend on how many people search, what else sits on the results page, and your offer, prices and reviews. Honest terms list these as exclusions instead of leaving them to the sales pitch.

Rankings move. Google says its core updates come "Several times a year", and that "positions in Google Search results aren't static or fixed in place" [6].

Clicks move too. In Pew's March 2025 study of 900 US adults, people clicked a traditional search result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% when none did [7]. Google disputes the size of such declines, saying total organic clicks have been "relatively stable" year over year [8].

Sales depend on things no SEO controls: your prices, your reviews, how quickly you answer and what customers find when they arrive. That is why our written terms exclude "more traffic, calls, leads, customers or sales", and any particular position on the page.

What can be promised: a measured share, dates and written terms

Direct answer

A provider can promise a defined, checkable result: an agreed list of phrases, a share of them on the first page of Google, set dates, a stated way of checking from your market, and written terms covering what happens if a date is missed. Ours sets milestones at day 90, day 180 and the end of the first year, measured in each of the three places separately.

Here is our promise, as we register and publish it: "We guarantee up to 50% of the key phrases you could rank for will rank on the first page of Google." The list is built from every variation your customers could type to find a business like yours, and agreed with you in writing at the start; searches for your own business name don't count. The schedule is at least 20% of the list on page one by day 90, 40% by day 180 and 50% by the end of the first year.

The three places are your Google Business Profile in the local map results, the first ten organic results, and Google's AI answer when it names or links your business. The share is measured in each of the three places separately, and each has to reach the milestone. Google shows an AI answer on only some searches; in our check of 12 commercial searches made from Las Vegas on September 27, 2026, it showed one for 2 [9]. So the written terms work out the AI share only over the phrases for which Google shows an AI answer when we check; if it shows none at a check, that milestone is judged in the map and organic results. No AI answer is promised for every phrase.

The first page guarantee is offered after an assessment, and only if you qualify. That order matters: the competition is measured before any promise is made, and the share is set from what the measurements support. Our guide to how an SEO guarantee works shows that first page math step by step.

Even skeptics leave room for a first page promise written this way. Ahrefs argues that "what cannot be controlled, cannot be guaranteed", yet says a guarantee that isn't a scam "clearly and transparently communicates what is and what isn't covered" [10]: exactly the job of written terms.

Don't confuse a first page promise with Google's own ads badge. Google replaced its Google Guaranteed badge for Local Services Ads with a single Google Verified badge in October 2025 [11] [12]; it is a trust badge on ads, not a ranking promise. Our article on Google Guaranteed explains the first page difference.

How to read an SEO guarantee's written terms

Direct answer

Read the terms, not the headline. Check which places count and how each is measured, who picks the phrases and whether your own name is excluded, how the share is calculated, where and when checks are made, what is excluded, what happens on a missed date, and how either side can end the agreement. If a point isn't in writing, it isn't promised.

Checklist: reading the written terms of an SEO guarantee

In any first page guarantee, watch the phrase list most closely. Ahrefs warns that some guarantees rest on keywords unrelated to your business, on "Keywords with very little to no search volume", or on informational searches that won't bring leads, and advises: "Don't sign a long-term contract on a short-term guarantee." [10]

Regulators agree written terms matter. The FTC's Guides for the Advertising of Warranties and Guarantees (16 CFR Part 239, published in 1985) say most of their sections apply to advertising any warranty or guarantee [13]. For a first page promise, two sections stand out. Section 239.3(b) says an ad that mentions a satisfaction guarantee or similar promise should disclose its material limitations or conditions clearly and prominently, which is the job of written terms. Section 239.5 says a seller should advertise a guarantee only if it performs its obligations under it promptly and fully [13]. Whether the Guides reach a particular business-to-business SEO contract is a question for a lawyer; as a yardstick for good written terms, they are useful. Ours are public: read our written guarantee terms.

Is SEO worth it without a promise of results?

Direct answer

For most businesses that depend on being found on Google, yes, because page one is where nearly all the clicks are. But judge a plan by what it will measure and by when, not by a promise alone. SEO takes weeks to months, and Google itself says a small local business can do much of the work without an agency.

Page two gets almost nothing. In Backlinko's click-through data (revised 2022), only 0.63% of searchers clicked a result on Google's second page, and SISTRIX measured well under 1% for every position there [14] [15].

Results take time. Google says some changes take effect in hours and others "could take several months" [16]. Until June 2026, its "Do you need an SEO?" page said results typically take four months to a year; that line has since been removed [17]. And Google is candid that "If you run a small local business, you can probably do much of the work yourself." [1] If you hire help, hire it for measurement and follow-through. Frequently asked questions

Can an SEO company guarantee first page rankings?

It can't guarantee positions, and it shouldn't claim to. It can guarantee a defined share of an agreed phrase list on the first page of Google by set dates, under written terms. Ours is at least 20% by day 90, 40% by day 180 and 50% by the end of the first year, measured in each of the three places separately, and offered after an assessment, only if you qualify.

Why does Google warn about ranking promises?

Because no one outside Google can set rankings, and position promises have been used to sell SEO that doesn't deliver. Google tells owners to walk away from anyone promising the top result [1], and in 2018 the FTC charged a scheme that falsely promised first-page placement while claiming to represent Google [5]. A promise defined in writing, with no position in it, is the answer to that warning.

What should written terms for an SEO guarantee include?

The phrase list and who agrees it, the places that count and how each is measured, how the share is calculated, the dates, where checks are made from, the exclusions, what happens if a milestone is missed, and how to end the agreement. It should also say plainly what is not promised: positions, traffic and sales.

What happens if a first page milestone is missed?

That depends entirely on the written terms, which is why you should read them before you sign. Ours set out what happens after a missed milestone, the exclusions and how either side can end the agreement, on our written guarantee terms page. If a provider can't show you this in writing, treat the guarantee as a sales line.

Can AI answers be part of an SEO guarantee?

Google's own AI answer can, when the written terms say how it is measured, because it appears on Google's results page and can name or link your business. ChatGPT, Perplexity and other assistants are separate products with their own sources, and our first page promise does not count them. Google shows an AI answer on only some searches, so no honest terms promise one for every phrase.
