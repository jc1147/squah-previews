Local SEO vs Organic SEO: What's the Difference?

The map pack ranks Business Profiles and the organic results rank web pages. How each one is ranked, where the signals overlap, and which to invest in first.

Local SEO wins the map pack, where Google ranks Business Profiles mainly on relevance, distance and popularity. Organic SEO wins the list of web pages, where Google weighs hundreds of factors [1][2]. The two overlap more than most owners expect, and a business that serves customers in a particular place usually needs both.

The short answer

Direct answer

Local SEO is about your Google Business Profile appearing in the map pack and Google Maps for searches near you. Organic SEO is about your web pages appearing in the ranked list of results, which is also what national SEO competes for. Local results rest mainly on relevance, distance and popularity; organic results on the relevance and quality of each page. Your website and your links feed both [1][3][4].

Two kinds of results on one page

Direct answer

A single local search can show a map pack of Business Profiles, a list of organic web pages and sometimes an AI answer. In our capture of 12 commercial searches made from Las Vegas, 11 showed a map pack, all 12 showed organic results and 2 showed an AI Overview. Each part ranks on its own signals, so each needs its own work [7].

Because the map pack is made of Business Profiles, a business can sit in it without ranking organically, and the reverse. Our capture covered searches from "dentist" to "garage door repair" on September 27, 2026: one date and one device, so an example rather than a rate [7].

Informational searches look different. When we ran 36 informational searches US-wide the same day, every one showed an AI Overview [8]. SISTRIX puts the general rule well: "The search intention of a keyword defines the SERP layout" [9]. In our captures, the buying searches brought up map packs and the learning searches brought up AI answers.

How local results rank: relevance, distance, popularity

Direct answer

Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". Relevance is how well your Business Profile matches the search; distance is how far you are from the searcher; popularity, explained under the heading "Prominence", is how well known you are, including links and reviews. Complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and no one can pay to rank higher [1].

Google rewrote that page in mid-2025. The old version credited information "from across the web, like links, articles, and directories" and added that "Your position in web results is also a factor"; both lines are gone [10]. Google's current page no longer ties your map ranking to your web ranking, although, as the overlap section shows, your website still matters.

In Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts, the highest-scored map-pack factors are the primary category, proximity to the searcher and keywords in the business name [4], though keywords in a name break Google's rules and risk suspension [11]. For the full picture, read our local SEO guide and our guide to Google Business Profile optimization.

How organic results rank

Direct answer

Google ranks web pages with automated systems that weigh "hundreds of factors", including the searcher's location, language and device, mostly page by page. Its starter guide says useful content will likely matter more than any other suggestion in it, and Google names words in titles and headings, links and Core Web Vitals among the signals. Nobody can pay Google for organic placement [2][3][12][6].

In Google's own words:

  • "Relevancy is determined by hundreds of factors, which could include information such as the user's location, language, and device (desktop or phone)." [2]
  • "Creating content that people find compelling and useful will likely influence your website's presence in search results more than any of the other suggestions in this guide." [12]
  • "Use words that people would use to look for your content, and place those words in prominent locations on the page, such as the title and main heading of a page" [13].
  • "Google uses links as a signal when determining the relevancy of pages and to find new pages to crawl." [14]
  • "Core Web Vitals are used by our ranking systems." [15]

Where you land on page one matters. In Advanced Web Ranking's July 2026 data for US desktop, the first organic result averaged a 20.02% click-through rate, the second 10.36% and the third 3.89% [16]. Map-pack click data is weaker: we found no current independent study, so we don't quote one. Our organic SEO guide covers the organic side in depth.

Where the signals overlap

Direct answer

Your website, your links and your reviews count in both, with different weights. Whitespark's 2026 experts give on-page signals 15% of the map-pack weighting and 33% for local organic results, and links 8% and 24%. A dedicated page for each service is their strongest local organic factor and still sits 24th for the map pack. Google also reads category signals from your website [4][11].

Some work pays off everywhere. A dedicated page for each service sits 24th for the map pack, first for local organic results and second for AI visibility; content relevant to your city or neighborhood sits 18th, second and sixth [4]. Google says it can detect category information "from your website and from mentions about your business throughout the web" [11], and it names links under prominence [1]. An older Local SEO Guide study linked organic and map-pack rankings, but it warns that "correlation does not equal causation" [17].

The measuring logic is the same on both sides. For organic results, Ahrefs' rule of thumb is to earn as many links as the pages already in the top 10, "if not more" [18]. For the map pack, the same logic applies to categories, reviews and profile completeness: measure what the businesses already there have, then match and exceed it.

Key takeaway

Build your website and links once and they feed both lists; the Business Profile and reviews are the extra layer that only local results reward.

Which matters more for your business

Direct answer

If customers come to you, or you go to them within an area, start with local SEO: buying searches near you often bring up a map pack, and your profile is eligible for it. If you sell nationally or only online, with no face-to-face contact, you can't have a Business Profile, so organic SEO, sometimes called national SEO, and AI answers are your field. Most local businesses need both [5][7].

  • Practices and storefronts (dentists, law firms, clinics, shops): the map pack showed for 11 of the 12 commercial searches in our Las Vegas capture [7], so the profile comes first, with service pages that feed both lists.
  • Service-area businesses (plumbers, cleaners, mobile services): eligible for the map pack with the address hidden and up to 20 service areas [11][19], with website pages for each service and area you really cover.
  • National and online-only businesses with no face-to-face contact: Google says only businesses that make face-to-face contact with customers are eligible for a Business Profile [5], so the map pack is closed to you. Local SEO vs national SEO is then an easy choice: national SEO is organic SEO without the location advantage, competing with every site that targets the same phrase.

Paid placements compete too: Sterling Sky's 2026 report found ads in the local pack on almost 22% of its reports, up from 1% at the start of 2025 [20]. For searches with "near me" in them, see our piece on near me SEO.

How the three places add up on one results page

Direct answer

The map pack, the organic results and an AI answer are three separate chances to be seen for the same search, and they don't always appear together. Measure each one on its own. Our first page guarantee counts all three places and measures the share separately in each of them at every milestone, never as one blended number [7].

How often each appears varies. In our Las Vegas capture, the map pack appeared on 11 of 12 commercial searches and an AI Overview on 2 [7]. Outside estimates of AI Overview frequency vary: about 18% of searches in Pew's March 2025 data [21] and 43% of sampled desktop results pages in Advanced Web Ranking's July 2026 data [16], and Google itself says AI Overviews "often don't trigger" [22].

Frequently asked questions

Is local SEO part of organic SEO?

They're closer to siblings than parent and child. The map pack ranks Business Profiles on relevance, distance and popularity, while organic results rank web pages [1]. But local SEO also covers the organic results for local searches, where Whitespark's experts weight on-page content (33%) and links (24%) most heavily [4]. Your website serves both, so plan them together.

Do I need both local and organic SEO?

If you serve customers in person, almost certainly. Buying searches near you often bring up a map pack as well as organic results: 11 of 12 did in our Las Vegas capture [7]. The profile wins the map; your pages win the organic list and feed the profile's relevance and prominence. A business that only sells online can't have a Business Profile, so organic SEO is its whole game [5].

Can a business without an office rank locally?

Yes, if it meets customers face to face at their location. Google lets service-area businesses hide their address and list up to 20 service areas, within about two hours' drive [11][19]. What doesn't qualify is a virtual office or a P.O. box used as an address [11]. A business with no face-to-face contact at all isn't eligible for a Business Profile [5].

Which is faster, local or organic SEO?

No study in our research compares the two directly. Google says some changes take effect in a few hours and others take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging a change [12]. Brand-new pages can be slow to rank organically: in Ahrefs' study of random new pages, only 1.74% reached the top 10 within a year [23].

Does my website affect map rankings?

Yes, indirectly. Google names links to your business under prominence [1] and reads category information from your website [11]. Whitespark's experts give on-page signals 15% of the map-pack weighting, and a site whose name, address and phone match the profile scores as a map-pack factor in their survey [4]. What Google no longer says is that your web ranking itself is a factor; that line left its help page in 2025 [10].

Where to start

Look at what Google shows for your most valuable searches from where your customers are: if a map pack appears, put the profile first and build the service pages that feed both lists.
