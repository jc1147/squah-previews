Google Business Profile Optimization: The Complete Guide

Every field of a Google Business Profile with Google's current rules on names, categories and reviews, a dated record of the features Google has changed since 2024, and a checklist that keeps you inside the rules.

Google Business Profile optimization means making your profile the most complete, accurate and relevant match for what your customers search, without breaking any of Google's rules. Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", that complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and that the categories you select affect your local ranking [1][2].

This guide goes field by field: who qualifies, verification, the name and address rules, categories, services, hours, photos, reviews and posts, then what gets profiles suspended. It also gives a dated record of features Google has added, changed or retired since 2024, because much of the advice still in circulation describes a product that no longer exists.

What a Google Business Profile does for local rankings

Direct answer

Your Business Profile is what Google ranks in the map pack and shows in Google Maps. Google ranks those results on relevance, distance and popularity, and says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. In Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts, profile signals carry 32% of the map-pack weighting, more than any other group [1][3].

Google's help page, rewritten in mid-2025, now defines the three factors like this [1][4]:

  • Relevance is "how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for".
  • Distance is "how far each business is from the customer who's searching".
  • The third factor, popularity, is explained under the heading "Prominence": "how well-known a business is", including "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have". Google adds that "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking."

The same page says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results, and that "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." [1] The old version's lines about directories, and about your web ranking being a factor, were removed in the rewrite between June 14 and July 10, 2025 [4]. Treat any guide that still quotes them as out of date.

The profile's reach goes beyond the map pack. Google Maps has more than 2 billion users a month (Google, February 2025) [5]. The Gemini app uses public information from Google Maps to answer questions about places, including addresses, websites, ratings and opening hours [6], and Google's guidance for site owners lists an up-to-date Business Profile among the basics for its AI features [7].

Stat

In Whitespark's 2026 survey, the primary Business Profile category is the highest-scored single map-pack factor (227), just ahead of proximity to the searcher (225) [3]

For how the profile fits with your website, reviews and links, see our local SEO guide.

Who qualifies, and how to set up and verify the profile

Direct answer

A Business Profile is only for businesses that make face-to-face contact with customers, at your location or at theirs. Virtual offices and P.O. boxes don't qualify, and each business gets one profile. Google chooses your verification method, which you can't change, and recommends video verification when it's offered. Editing categories later can trigger another verification [8][9][10][2].

Before creating anything, search Google Maps for your business. If a profile already exists, claim it rather than starting a duplicate: Google says "There should only be one profile per business" [9].

The eligibility rules [8][9]:

  • Face-to-face contact. "Only businesses that make face-to-face contact with customers are eligible for a Business Profile."
  • A real place. A virtual office isn't eligible, and "P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable."
  • Practitioners. Doctors, dentists, lawyers and similar professionals can have practitioner profiles, and Google allows a title or degree, such as Dr., MD, JD, Esq. or CFA. Practices have extra considerations, covered in Google Business Profile for dentists.

Verification is on Google's terms: "Verification methods are automatically determined by Google and can't be changed," and Google recommends video verification if your business is eligible for it [10]. Expect to verify more than once. Google warns that if you add or edit a category, "you might be asked to verify your business again" [2].

Keep the profile under a Google account the business controls. Whitespark's experts rate a link to a suspended Google account as the second-biggest suspension risk [3], so don't let a profile hang off someone else's account.

Name, address and service area: the rules

Direct answer

Use your real-world business name exactly as it appears on your sign, website and stationery. Adding keywords, locations or taglines isn't permitted and can get the profile suspended. If customers visit you, show your address and place the pin precisely. If you travel to customers, hide the address and list up to 20 service areas within about two hours' drive [9][11].

The name

Google's rule is that your profile should represent your business the way it is consistently represented and recognized in the real world, on signage, stationery and other branding [9]. Anything extra, such as a city or a service tacked onto the name, breaks it. Google says including unnecessary information in the name "isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile" [9].

It's worth being honest about why people break this rule: it works, for a while. Sterling Sky found that adding a keyword to a business name "dramatically improved ranking", and removing it sent rankings back to where they started [12]. Whitespark's experts score keywords in the business name as the third-strongest map-pack factor (223) [3]. But the same experts list keyword stuffing in the name among the top suspension risks [3], and Sterling Sky believes Google's 2021 Vicinity update may have reduced the effect [13]. Use your real name. If competitors stuff theirs, the risk is theirs.

The address

If customers come to you, your address shows on the profile. Whitespark's survey scores an address showing on the profile 176 and proper placement of the map pin 165 [3], so check that your pin sits on your actual entrance. A virtual office isn't eligible [9], and Whitespark's experts rank a P.O. box, UPS store or virtual office address as the biggest suspension risk of all [3].

Service-area businesses

If you serve customers at their location, Google says "you should hide your business address from customers" [9]. The service-area rules [9][11]:

  • Up to 20 service areas, set by city, postal code or other areas you serve.
  • No radius: "You can't set your service area as a radius distance around your business."
  • Keep the area within about two hours of driving time from where you're based.
  • One profile per service-area business.

The evidence on hiding an address is mixed. Sterling Sky's study of 8,186 businesses found a negative correlation between hiding the address and ranking well in the map pack [14], and one of its clients lost rankings when the address was hidden and regained them when it was restored [15]. Whitespark's Darren Shaw, who runs the survey, thinks the effect is a bug rather than a ranking factor, a view he says he appears to hold alone [3]. None of it changes Google's rule: if customers don't come to you, hide the address.

Categories: primary and additional

Direct answer

Choose one primary category that best describes your business, then add only the extra categories that describe what your business is, up to nine more. Google says your categories affect your local ranking, to use the fewest that describe your core business, and never to use categories as keywords. Whitespark's experts score the primary category as the strongest single map-pack factor [2][16][9][3].

Google's category rules, in its own words:

  • "Select a primary category that best describes your business." [2]
  • "You can select up to 9 more categories", and "the category in the first field will be your primary category" [16].
  • "You can't create your own category." [2]
  • "Choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business." [9]
  • Use the "IS a" test: choose categories that complete "This business IS a" rather than "this business HAS a" [9].
  • "Do not use categories solely as keywords or to describe attributes of your business." [9]

Two details are easy to miss. Google "implicitly includes more general categories", so you don't need to add a broad parent category yourself, and it can detect category information "from your website and from mentions about your business throughout the web" [9]. Your website and listings should describe the same business your categories do. And because changing a category can trigger re-verification [2], settle your categories before other changes.

How much it matters: the primary category scored 227 in Whitespark's 2026 survey, the highest of any map-pack factor, and additional categories scored 173. An incorrect primary category is the second most damaging negative factor, after being marked permanently closed [3]. The third is what the experts call "The Filter": a second profile in the same category at the same address [3].

To choose well, search your most valuable phrase from your market and note the primary category of every business in the map pack. That comparison, not a guess, is where the choice starts. Our guide to Google Business Profile categories goes through the research step by step.

Services, products, hours and attributes

Direct answer

List the services you really offer, choosing from the options Google suggests for your business type; Google says a listed service may be highlighted when people search for it. In-store product listings are for retail businesses in four countries. Hours must be your real customer-facing hours, with special hours for short closures, and a business that works only by appointment shouldn't list hours [17][9][18].

Services

Google says that when local customers search for an offering you provide, "that service may be highlighted on your profile", and that you can "choose from suggested options tailored to your business type" [17]. The ranking evidence has shifted: an earlier Sterling Sky test found no effect from services, but its 2022 retest found that they did affect rankings, with the ranking impact taking 24 to 72 hours to show [19]. Whitespark's experts score the presence of Google's predefined services 22nd for the map pack [3]. List only what you do, in the words Google offers, and give each service a matching page on your website.

Products

Google's guidelines describe in-store product listings for retail businesses in the US, Canada, the UK and Ireland [9]. For a service business, the services list does that job.

Hours

Google asks you to "Provide your regular customer-facing hours of operation", and its guidelines single out businesses that operate only by appointment as ones that shouldn't list hours [9]. Use special hours for short changes, such as closing for up to six days in a row [18].

Hours affect rankings as well as customers. Being open at the time of the search scored 189 in Whitespark's survey, fifth for the map pack, and "No Hours of Operation" is the eighth most damaging negative [3]. Sterling Sky first saw openness acting as a map-pack factor in November 2023 and reported it that December; it is an observation, and Google hasn't confirmed it [20]. Whitespark's report notes that you start to drop about 60 minutes before closing, and it warns any business customers can visit never to list hours other than its real ones [3].

Attributes and description

Fill in the attributes Google offers for your category where they're true, because Google ties complete information to showing up [1]. Write the description for customers. Google's rule on links is blunt, "No links of any type are allowed", and the description shouldn't focus on promotions [9]. Keywords in the description don't help: Whitespark's experts rate them a myth [3].

Photos and videos

Direct answer

Add real, recent photos of your premises, your team and your work, and keep adding them. Google says business photos should have no significant alterations or excessive use of filters or AI, and should represent reality. Geotagging your photos doesn't help: Whitespark's experts put geotagged photos at the head of their list of local search myths [21][3].

Google's standard fits in five words: "the image should represent reality" [21]. Shoot what a first-time customer needs to recognize: the outside of your building as people approach it, the entrance, the inside, your team, and your work where your profession allows it. Short videos of the same things help people too; hold them to the same standard of honesty.

Photos also matter beyond the profile. Google's advice for its AI features is to "support your textual content with high-quality images and videos on your pages, and ensure that your Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date." [22]

Reviews and replies

Direct answer

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. Ask every customer for an honest review with a Google link or QR code, never offer incentives or ask only happy customers, never set staff review quotas, and reply to every review in a conversational, non-promotional voice. Since October 21, 2024, a federal rule also bans incentives tied to a review's sentiment [1][23][24][25].

What customers expect, from BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults [26]:

  • 97% read reviews for local businesses.
  • 68% will only use a business with four or more stars, up from 55% in 2025.
  • 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
  • 89% expect a response, and templated replies put off 50%.
  • Incentive offers persist: 11% had been offered one for a positive review.

Stat

74% of consumers look for reviews written in the last three months (BrightLocal, 2026) [26]

What the ranking studies show:

  • Whitespark's experts score high ratings 181, the number of native reviews with text 170, recency 164, and a sustained influx of reviews rather than bursts 154 [3].
  • Sterling Sky's near-me study found reviews gained in the current month mattered more than total reviews, and reviews with text had a stronger effect; it is a correlation, not proof of cause [14].
  • Two small Sterling Sky tests are worth knowing: a small Maps lift from the tenth review but not the eleventh, and no gain from keywords planted in reviews. Each involved very few businesses, so treat them as observations [27][28].

The rules

Google's Maps content policy [24] bans:

  • reviews "paid for, directly or in kind";
  • incentives such as payment, discounts, or free goods or services, in exchange for a review or for changing or removing a negative one;
  • discouraging negative reviews, or selectively asking happy customers (review gating);
  • posting content on a competitor's profile to undermine it;
  • asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that include specific content, such as a staff member's name, in clauses that first appear in Internet Archive captures of the policy between February 15 and 23, 2026 [29].

What you can do: Google says you "can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code" [23].

The law behind it: the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule was announced on August 14, 2024 and took effect on October 21, 2024 [30][25]. It applies to businesses. It bars them from offering compensation or other incentives conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment, positive or negative, and from suppressing reviews with unfounded legal threats, physical threats or intimidation [30]. FTC staff say review gating, though not specifically named in the rule, "could violate the FTC Act" [25]. Google's policy is broader than the rule: it bans review incentives of any kind [24].

Enforcement is real. Google says it blocked or removed more than 292 million policy-violating reviews and over 13 million fake Business Profiles in 2025 [31], up from more than 240 million reviews in 2024 [32]. In April 2026 it said a sudden spike in reviews can now lead it to pause new reviews on the profile, alert the owner and show a notification banner [31]. Whitespark's experts also list receiving too many reviews too fast as a suspension risk [3]. The safe pattern is a steady ask to every customer, not a campaign.

Replies

Google's advice is to be "conversational, not promotional", and it notes that a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy [23]. Replies are checked before they appear, usually within 10 minutes but sometimes up to 30 days, and the customer can still change their review after reading your reply [33]. Keywords in replies don't help rankings, according to Whitespark's experts [3]. BrightLocal found 19% of consumers now expect a reply the same day, up from 6% a year earlier [26].

For the evidence in depth, see do Google reviews help SEO; for the asking process, see how to get Google reviews.

Posts, Q&A, messaging and the features Google has retired

Direct answer

Posts still exist in three types, Updates, Offers and Events, and archive after six months unless you set a date range; experts rate posting volume as a myth. Google turned off Business Profile websites in March 2024, removed chat and call history on July 31, 2024, shut the Q&A API on November 3, 2025, and is moving customer questions to AI answers in Maps [34][3][35][36][37][38].

Posts

Google offers three post types: Updates, Offers and Events, and "Posts older than 6 months are archived unless a date range is set." [34] Whitespark's experts list the quantity of Google Posts among their myths [3], so post when you have something real to say, such as a new service, an event or changed hours, rather than to a quota.

Q&A and the Ask button

Customer questions are moving to AI. Google discontinued the Business Profile Q&A API on November 3, 2025, having announced it on September 15, 2025 [37]. In December 2025, Search Engine Roundtable reported that Google Maps was replacing the Q&A section with an AI-powered "Ask" button that answers from the business's own answers and relevant reviews; a Google Product Specialist said owners can still answer existing questions in the profile [38]. The rollout is uneven: in January 2026, Whitespark's Miriam Ellis saw the new feature on many profiles in the US but not in Canada [39]. The experts already rated the number of Q&A questions a myth [3], so seeding questions isn't worth your time. What matters now is that your answers, services and reviews give the AI accurate material to work from.

Messaging and calls

Google removed chat and call history from Business Profiles on July 31, 2024 [36]. Verified profiles can instead add a WhatsApp or text messaging option to their contact information, in select regions [40]. Google may also call you itself: it says it might contact your business through an automated phone call to complete a customer's request [41]. Since July 16, 2025, Google Search can call businesses for pricing and availability on a searcher's behalf, with businesses "always in control via their Business Profile settings" [42], and in May 2026 Google said people can ask it to call businesses in select categories such as home repair, beauty or pet care [43]. Keep what your staff say on the phone consistent with your profile.

Measure what the profile does

Direct answer

A verified profile's Performance report lists the search terms people used to find you, updated at the start of each month, alongside views and actions. It mixes in Google Ads activity, so allow for paid activity when you read it. Check your map ranking from several points across your market, because distance is measured from each searcher [45][1].

Google defines the Searches metric as "The search terms people used to find your business" and says it "is updated at the start of each month" [45]. The report's views, searches and actions include both organic results and Google Ads [45], so a month with heavy ad spend can flatter it.

Calls are a weak proxy for visibility. Sterling Sky's look at 179 law-firm profiles across 34 US firms found clicks-to-call declining over two years [46], so a fall in calls doesn't by itself mean you've lost ground in the map pack.

To measure against competitors (our method, not a measured result), search each phrase on your list from the points that matter and record, for every business in the map pack: its primary and additional categories, its distance from the search point, its review count, rating and reviews in the last 90 days, how complete its profile is, whether it has a real address or a service-area profile, whether its name follows Google's rules, and whether it's open at the time. The weakest business holding a place sets the bar, and your gap to it, field by field, is the work list. Google says third-party tools "don't have access to Google's internal ranking data" [47], so treat every such measurement as an estimate; Google decides. For turning the gaps into a plan, see how to rank higher on Google Maps.

Suspensions: what triggers them and how to recover

Direct answer

Whitespark's 2026 expert panel puts an ineligible address at the top of its suspension risks, and also lists a keyword-stuffed name, fake or gated reviews, a burst of reviews and oversized service areas. If suspended, fix the cause and appeal through Google's process; don't create a new profile for the same business while the appeal is under review. Google says decisions can take up to five business days [3][48][49].

The suspension risks Whitespark's experts ranked highest in 2026 [3]:

  • a P.O. box, UPS store or virtual office address (first);
  • a link to a suspended Google account (second);
  • reports of review gating (sixth) and of fake reviews (seventh);
  • keyword stuffing in the business name (eighth);
  • receiving too many Google reviews too fast (12th);
  • service areas beyond two hours' drive (17th).

Each maps to a Google rule covered above: eligible addresses and honest names [9], genuine, ungated reviews [24], and service areas within about two hours' drive [9]. Google's scale of enforcement is large: it removed over 13 million fake Business Profiles in 2025 [31].

If it happens [48][49]:

  1. Fix the cause first: the name, the address, the categories, the service areas or the review practice.
  2. Appeal through Google's process for Business Profile restrictions.
  3. Don't start over. Google's instruction is "Do not create a new Business Profile for the same business" while your appeal is under review.
  4. Allow time. Google says "Appeal reviews and decisions can take up to 5 business days."

Checklist: Google Business Profile optimization

Frequently asked questions

How do I optimize my Google Business Profile?

Make every field complete, accurate and specific. Use your real business name, pick the most specific primary category and only the extra categories that describe what you are, choose services from Google's suggestions, list real hours, add genuine photos, and ask every customer for an honest review. Google says complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show up in local results, and that no one can pay for a better local ranking [1].

How does Google Business Profile verification work?

Google decides how you verify. Its help page says "Verification methods are automatically determined by Google and can't be changed," and recommends video verification when your business is eligible for it [10]. You may need to verify again later: Google warns that adding or editing a category can trigger another verification [2]. Your business must also be eligible, which means making face-to-face contact with customers [8].

Can I add keywords to my business name?

No. Google says including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted and could get the profile suspended [9]. Sterling Sky's testing shows keywords in a name can lift rankings [12], and Whitespark's experts score it highly, but the same experts list name stuffing among the top suspension risks [3]. Use the name on your sign and website; practitioners may include a title such as Dr. or MD [9].

How many categories should I choose?

As few as describe your core business. You choose one primary category and can add up to nine more [16], but Google says to "Choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business" and to pick categories that complete "This business IS a", not "HAS a" [9]. Google also adds broader parent categories on its own, so you don't need to list them.

Do Google Business Profile posts help rankings?

Not by volume. Whitespark's 2026 survey lists the quantity of Google Posts among its myths [3]. Posts are still useful to customers: there are three types, Updates, Offers and Events, and posts older than six months are archived unless you set a date range [34]. Post when you have real news, such as a new service, an event or changed hours, rather than to a schedule.

Why was my Business Profile suspended?

In local search experts' view, the usual causes are an ineligible address such as a virtual office or P.O. box, extra words in the business name, service areas set too wide, or fake or gated reviews [3], each of which breaks Google's rules [9][24]. Fix the cause, then appeal. Don't create a new profile for the same business while the appeal is under review; Google says decisions can take up to five business days [48][49].
