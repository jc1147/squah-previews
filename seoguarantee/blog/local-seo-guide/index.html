Local SEO is the work of making a business that serves customers in person show up when people nearby search for what it offers: in Google's map pack, in the organic results for local searches and in the AI answers Google writes for some searches. Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity" [1], so the job comes down to three things: be the clearest match for the search, make the most of where you are, and be the best-known business of your kind nearby.

This guide uses Google's current wording (its local ranking page was rewritten in mid-2025), industry studies named with their methods, and the rules that get profiles suspended, and ends with a checklist.

What local SEO is, and what the map pack is

Direct answer

Local SEO is the part of search optimization aimed at searches with local intent, such as "dentist" or "plumber near me". Its main prize is the map pack, also called the local pack or 3-pack: the block of Google Business Profile listings that Google shows with a map. Those listings are profiles, not web pages, so they rank on different signals from the organic results beneath them.

One results page can hold three kinds of result: the map pack (Business Profiles), the organic results (web pages) and, for some searches, an AI answer, which Google says can include information about local businesses [2]. Each ranks on its own signals, so a business can appear in one and not another; our article on local SEO vs organic SEO compares them.

Which searches bring up the map? On September 27, 2026 we captured Google's first page for 12 commercial searches made from Las Vegas, from "dentist" and "emergency dentist" to "garage door repair". Eleven of the 12 showed a map pack, all 12 showed organic results, and only 2 showed an AI Overview [3]. That is one date, one device and one city, so read it as an example rather than a rate.

Stat

Google Maps has more than 2 billion users a month, and Google counted 250 million businesses and places on the map (Google, February 2025) [4]

Local SEO in the map-pack sense has an entry rule. Google says "Only businesses that make face-to-face contact with customers are eligible for a Business Profile." [5] That includes storefronts and businesses that travel to their customers, such as plumbers or cleaners [6]. A business that serves customers only online competes in the organic results and AI answers instead.

How Google ranks local results: relevance, distance and popularity

Direct answer

Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity". Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the person searching. The third factor, which Google explains under the heading "Prominence", is how well known your business is, including links to your website and your reviews. No one can pay for a better local ranking [1].

Google's page defines each factor [1]:

  • Relevance: "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for." Your categories, services and description are how you tell Google what you are, and Google says separately that "The categories you select affect your local ranking on Google." [7]
  • Distance: "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who's searching." When a searcher hasn't shared a location, Google says it uses what it knows about where they are.
  • Prominence: Google describes prominence as "how well-known a business is", based partly on "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have". It adds: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking."

The same page says complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and that "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." [1] Whitespark's expert panel also rates taking part in Google Ads or other paid Google products as a ranking myth [8].

What changed in 2025

Google rewrote this help page between its Internet Archive captures of June 14 and July 10, 2025 [9]. The old version said local results were "based primarily on relevance, distance, and prominence", credited information "from across the web, like links, articles, and directories", and added that "Your position in web results is also a factor". None of those lines is on the current page.

Plenty of advice still repeats the old text. That doesn't prove those signals stopped mattering, but it means you should weigh them on evidence, not on Google's word. The research below is that evidence, with its limits stated.

Key takeaway

Build on Google's current page: relevance, distance and popularity, with links and reviews named under prominence, and no way to pay your way up.

What the experts' survey adds

Google publishes no weights. The industry's long-running estimate is Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors survey: for 2026, a panel of 47 local search experts was invited to score 187 factors in a two-hour survey, and Whitespark says none of them has special access to Google's local algorithm [8]. It is informed opinion, not a measurement of Google.

The highest-scored individual map-pack factors were the primary Business Profile category (227), the distance from the searcher to the business address (225), keywords in the business name (223) and a physical address in the city being searched (213). Being open at the time of the search came fifth (189), and high Google ratings sixth (181) [8]. Keywords in the name, though, break Google's rules, as the next section explains.

Your Google Business Profile: the foundation

Direct answer

Your Google Business Profile is the listing the map pack ranks, so it carries most of the local work. Use your real business name, choose the most specific primary category, list your real services and hours, add genuine photos, and keep every field accurate. Google says complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and that the categories you choose affect your local ranking [1][7].

Our Google Business Profile optimization guide goes field by field. The essentials:

  1. Name. Use the name on your sign, website and stationery, and keep one profile per business. Adding unnecessary information to the name "isn't permitted, and could result in the suspension of your Business Profile" [6]. Keywords in a name lifted rankings in Sterling Sky's testing [10], but Whitespark's experts list name stuffing among the top suspension risks [8], and the rule is Google's.
  2. Primary category. Pick the one category that best describes your business. You can add up to nine more, and you can't invent your own [7][11]. Google's test for each extra category is whether it completes the statement "This business IS a" rather than "this business HAS a" [6]. An incorrect primary category is the second most damaging negative factor in the Whitespark survey [8]. Our guide to Google Business Profile categories shows how to research the categories your competitors use.
  3. Services. Choose from the services Google suggests for your business type. Google says a listed service may be highlighted on your profile when local customers search for it [12].
  4. Hours. List your regular customer-facing hours [6]. Being open at the time of the search came fifth in the survey, an effect Sterling Sky first reported in December 2023 and Google hasn't confirmed [8][13]. Never list hours you don't keep.
  5. Photos. Google asks for images with "no significant alterations or excessive use of filters or AI" [14]. Show your real premises, team and work.
  6. Address or service area. If customers come to you, show your address. If you go to them, Google says to hide it and set up to 20 service areas within about two hours' drive. Virtual offices and P.O. boxes aren't eligible at all [6][15].

Stat

Whitespark's 2026 panel of 47 local search experts gives Business Profile signals 32% of the map-pack weighting, the largest single group [8]

Reviews: count, rating, recency and replies

Direct answer

Reviews work twice: Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking, and customers read them before they choose. What counts is a steady flow of recent, genuine reviews with text, a strong average rating and a reply to each one. Incentives, gating and staff quotas break Google's rules, and a federal rule bans incentives tied to a positive or negative review [1][16].

From BrightLocal's 2026 survey of a representative panel of 1,002 US adults [17]:

Stat

97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, while Google's share as a review source slipped from 83% in 2025 to 71% (BrightLocal, 2026) [17]

  • 68% will only use a business rated four stars or higher, up from 55% in 2025.
  • 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
  • 74% look for reviews written in the last three months.
  • 89% expect owners to respond, and generic or templated replies put off 50%.
  • 19% expect a reply on the same day they post, up from 6% the year before [17].

What the ranking evidence says:

  • Whitespark's experts score high ratings (181), the number of native Google reviews with text (170) and review recency (164) among the stronger map-pack factors, and add a "sustained influx of reviews over time (rather than bursts)" (154) [8].
  • Sterling Sky and Places Scout analyzed 8,186 businesses in 200 US cities across five service searches, in a study of what drives near me rankings. Reviews gained in the current month mattered more than total reviews, and reviews with text had a stronger effect [18]. The study is correlational.

The rules for asking:

  • You can ask customers for honest reviews, for example with a Google link or a QR code [19].
  • Google's policy bans reviews "paid for, directly or in kind", incentives such as discounts or free services, discouraging negative reviews, and asking only happy customers, a practice known as review gating [20].
  • Clauses that first appear in Internet Archive captures of the policy between February 15 and 23, 2026 also bar merchants from asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that include specific content, such as a staff member's name [20][21].
  • The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, announced on August 14, 2024 and in force since October 21, 2024, bars businesses from offering incentives conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment, positive or negative [16][22]. FTC staff add that review gating, though not named in the rule, "could violate the FTC Act" [22]. Google's own policy goes further and bans review incentives of any kind [20].
  • Google says it blocked or removed more than 292 million policy-violating reviews and over 13 million fake Business Profiles in 2025 [23], up from more than 240 million reviews in 2024 [24]. In April 2026 it said a sudden spike can now pause new reviews, alert the owner and put a banner on the profile [23].

Replies matter too. Google's advice is to be "conversational, not promotional" [19]; replies are moderated, usually within 10 minutes but sometimes up to 30 days, and a customer can still change their review afterwards [25]. Keywords in replies are a myth, according to Whitespark's experts [8], so write for the next customer. For the full evidence, see do Google reviews help SEO.

Key takeaway

Ask every customer the same honest way; a steady flow of recent, genuine reviews with replies beats a burst, and a burst can trip Google's filters.

Proximity: why the map changes across town

Direct answer

Distance is measured from the person searching, so the map pack can change from one neighborhood to the next. Google applies the searcher's location even when the search names no place. You can't move your business, but you control relevance and prominence, and you can make sure Google places you exactly where you are and knows when you're open [26][1].

Google's location help page says your location helps show you nearby results "even if you didn't include a location in your search", and gives the example that "if you search for coffee shops, you're likely searching for coffee shops near you" [26].

In the Whitespark survey, the distance between the searcher and the business address is the second-highest-scored map-pack factor (225), a physical address in the city being searched is fourth (213), and the address's distance from the city's center scored 157 [8]. In a post quoted by BrightLocal, Google confirmed a local update that ran from November 30 to December 8, 2021, calling it "a rebalancing of various factors"; the industry named it Vicinity [27]. Sterling Sky read it as giving proximity more weight [28], an agency's interpretation rather than Google's statement.

What you can do about distance:

  • Put the pin on your door. Proper placement of the map pin scored 165 in the survey [8].
  • Be open when people search. Whitespark's report notes that you start to drop about 60 minutes before you close [8].
  • Set service areas honestly. Google says service areas shouldn't extend farther than about two hours of driving from where you're based and can't be set as a radius [6][15]. Whitespark's experts list service areas beyond two hours' drive as a suspension risk [8].
  • Follow Google's address rule. Google says service-area businesses should hide their address [6]. Sterling Sky's near-me study found a negative correlation between hiding the address and ranking well [18], and one of its clients lost rankings when the address was hidden [29], though Whitespark's Darren Shaw, who runs the survey, thinks the effect is a bug rather than a ranking factor, a view he says he appears to hold alone [8]. The evidence is disputed; Google's rule is not.

For searches that include the words themselves, see near me SEO; for a practical playbook, see how to rank higher on Google Maps.

Your website's local signals: service pages, NAP and schema

Direct answer

Your website feeds local rankings through relevance and prominence. Give each service its own page, write about the places you really serve, show the same name, address and phone number (NAP) as your profile in plain text, and earn links. In Whitespark's survey, a dedicated page for each service is the strongest factor for local organic results and scores high for AI visibility too [8].

The survey's four highest-scored local organic factors are a dedicated page for each service (210), how well your content matches your city or neighborhood (190), the quality and authority of links to your domain (187), and keywords in the title tag of the page your profile links to (179) [8]. The page-per-service factor sits 24th for the map pack, first for local organic results and second for AI visibility; geographic relevance sits 18th, second and sixth [8]. Work that pays off in all three places is the best place to start.

Google's Search Essentials apply here too: "Use words that people would use to look for your content, and place those words in prominent locations on the page, such as the title and main heading of a page" [30]. Google also says it can detect category information "from your website and from mentions about your business throughout the web" [6], so your service pages should describe the same business your categories do.

Three website rules that matter locally:

  1. NAP in crawlable text. In the survey, HTML NAP that matches your profile is the 15th map-pack factor (153), a site with no crawlable NAP is the 10th most damaging negative (121), and mismatched NAP or tracking phone numbers across the web is the 24th (90) [8].
  2. Real pages for real places. Google's spam policies name "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse, and ban scaled content made mainly to manipulate rankings [31]. One strong page per genuine location or service area beats a stack of near-identical city pages.
  3. Structured data that matches the page. Google uses structured data to understand a page's content, but doesn't promise the markup will show in results [32][33], and it asks that structured data match the visible text. There is no special schema for its AI features either [34].

Relevance also beats polish: "Google Search always seeks to show the most relevant content, even if the page experience is sub-par." [35] To build the list of services and places your pages should cover, see local SEO keyword research.

Citations and links that build prominence

Direct answer

Citations are listings of your business name, address and phone number on other sites, such as directories and map apps. Whitespark's experts now rate them a small map-pack factor (6%) but a larger one for AI visibility (13%). Links carry more weight: Google names the number of websites linking to your business under prominence, and the same experts weight links at 24% for local organic results [8][1].

Citations have been sliding for years. The strongest citation factor in the 2026 survey, consistent listings on Google Maps, Bing Maps and Apple Maps, sits only 28th for the map pack (124), and the sheer quantity of directory citations sits 110th (78) [8]. Google's old ranking page named directories as a source of prominence; the 2025 rewrite removed them [9].

AI answers are changing that. Whitespark says "3 of the top 5 AI Search visibility factors are citation factors", and that in AI SEO, mentions "are the new link" [8]. Two studies point the same way:

  • In a small BrightLocal test of 20 searches across 10 niches and 4 AI tools, Yelp was a source in 33% of searches, and in its dentistry searches ChatGPT sourced information exclusively from ten different dental directories [36].
  • Yext analyzed 6.8 million citations from over 1.6 million AI responses across Gemini, OpenAI and Perplexity: websites supplied 44% and listings 42%, healthcare leaned on listings (52.6%), and Reddit supplied just 2% [37]. Yext sells listings management, so weigh its framing accordingly.

The survey's top AI-visibility factors are mostly off your site: expert-curated "best of" lists (179), a dedicated page for each service (170), prominence on key industry websites (167), and unstructured citations such as newspaper articles and industry associations (160) [8].

Google names links under prominence [1], and its spam policies list "Buying or selling links for ranking purposes" and "Low-quality directory or bookmark site links" as link spam [31]. The useful links for a local business are earned: local press, associations, sponsors and suppliers. For organic results, Ahrefs' rule of thumb is to earn as many links as the pages already in the top 10, "if not more" [38]. That is our approach too: measure what the businesses already winning have, match it, then exceed it. Our piece on local citations covers which listings still matter.

Local results and AI answers

Direct answer

Google says there are no extra requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and lists an up-to-date Business Profile among the basics. Its AI answers can include local businesses, and Google says Business Profiles can help. AI answers appear on only some searches, so the same local work (profile, reviews, service pages, mentions) is what can earn a place in them [34][2].

The dates that matter:

DateWhat happenedSource
May 14, 2024AI Overviews began rolling out to everyone in the US[39]
May 20, 2025AI Mode opened to all US users, with no Labs sign-up[40]
July 16, 2025Google Search began calling businesses for pricing and availability on a searcher's behalf; businesses control it in their profile settings[41]
March 12, 2026Ask Maps, a Gemini-powered conversational feature in Google Maps, began rolling out in the US and India[42]
May 19, 2026Google said AI Mode had passed one billion monthly users, and that it can call businesses for you in select categories such as home repair, beauty and pet care[43]

In Google's words, from its guidance for site owners: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." Its list of basics includes "Checking that your Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date" [34]. Its newer AI optimization guide adds: "Where appropriate, generative AI responses can include product listings, product information, and information about local businesses." [2] As for AI text files such as llms.txt, Google says creating them "will neither harm nor help your site's visibility or rankings in Google Search, as Google Search ignores them." [2]

How often do AI answers show on local searches? It depends on who measures:

  • Google says AI Overviews "are only shown when our systems determine that it is additive to classic Search, and as such, often don't trigger." [34]
  • In our Las Vegas capture, 2 of the 12 commercial searches showed an AI Overview, and neither named a business from the map pack [3].
  • Sterling Sky's 2026 report found AI local packs (US, mobile) on about 7% of tracked keywords, surfacing only about 32% as many businesses as a traditional 3-pack [44].
  • For "near me seo", searched US-wide the same day, Google's AI Overview named two of the three map-pack businesses with their star ratings and review counts, and linked neither [45]. It is one capture, but it shows profile data traveling into AI answers.

Outside Google:

  • In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 45% of consumers had used AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier. ChatGPT was the tool most used for business recommendations (31%), followed by Google's AI Mode (23%), and 88% of AI users checked the reviews or sources the tools cited [46].
  • OpenAI says ChatGPT may use an approximate location based on your IP address, and uses unnamed third-party providers for nearby business listings [47]. It doesn't say it reads Google Business Profiles, so don't assume it does.
  • Microsoft tells local businesses to register with Bing Places for Business so that their address, hours and contact details stay eligible for its AI answers [48].

Key takeaway

There is no separate trick for AI answers; they reward the same accurate profile, genuine reviews, service pages and third-party mentions that the map pack and organic results reward.

Measuring local visibility from the right location

Direct answer

Because distance is measured from the searcher, a local ranking only means something from a stated location. Check your phrases from several points across the market you serve, not from your office, and record the map pack, the organic results and any AI answer separately. Then use your profile's Performance report and Search Console to see which searches already find you [49][50].

Google says its results depend on many signals, including "your location and settings" [51], and Search Console's help notes that your position for a specific search can differ from the average "because of many variables, such as your search history, location" [52]. National tools can mislead badly: our US-wide capture for "near me seo" placed an agency based in France in the map pack [45].

How to measure properly:

  1. Write the phrase list first. Multiply your services by the words customers add ("near me", "emergency", "open now", "cost") and by every city, neighborhood and ZIP code you serve. Our guide to local SEO keyword research walks through it.
  2. Check from fixed points. Search each phrase from several points in your market and record the date and device. Rank grids do this for the map pack; AI answers need to be captured separately.
  3. Read your own data. A verified profile's Performance report lists the search terms people used to find you, updated at the start of each month, and mixes in Google Ads activity, so allow for paid activity when you read it [49]. Search Console groups your website's data by the query people typed [50].
  4. Don't judge by calls alone. In Sterling Sky's look at 179 law-firm profiles across 34 firms, clicks-to-call declined over two years [44]. Rankings and calls are different measurements.

Measuring the competition

Measurement is also how a plan gets built. Our method, in plain terms (it is a method, not a measured result): for each phrase, record every business in the map pack and note its primary and additional categories, its distance from the search point, its review count, rating and reviews in the last 90 days, how complete its profile is, whether it has a real address in the city or a service-area profile, whether its name follows Google's rules, the links to its website, and whether it's open at the time of the search. The weakest business holding a place sets the bar. Your gap to that bar, input by input, is the work list, and the smallest gaps come first. That is what doing everything the leaders did, and more, means in practice: the plan comes from measurements of the businesses already on page one, not from guesses.

Google says third-party SEO tools "don't have access to Google's internal ranking data" [53], so every competitor measurement is an estimate of what Google rewards, and Google still decides the outcome.

Frequently asked questions

What is local SEO?

Local SEO is the work of making a business show up when nearby customers search for what it offers. It covers the map pack, where Google ranks Business Profiles on relevance, distance and popularity; the organic results for local searches, where your website ranks; and the AI answers Google writes for some searches. The core tasks are an accurate Business Profile, genuine reviews, a page for each service, consistent listings and earned links [1].

How is local SEO different from organic SEO?

Local SEO targets results tied to a place, above all the map pack, which ranks Business Profiles rather than web pages; organic SEO targets the ranked list of web pages. In Whitespark's 2026 survey, profile signals and reviews lead for the map pack, and on-page content and links for local organic results [8]. Most local businesses need both; our local SEO vs organic SEO article compares them.

How long does local SEO take?

No one can give you a fixed date. Google's current guidance says "Some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months", and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging whether a change helped [54]. Some profile edits, such as changing a category, can also trigger a fresh verification [7]. Reviews and links build up gradually, so measure monthly from fixed points in your market.

Do reviews affect local rankings?

Yes. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking [1]. Whitespark's 2026 expert survey gives reviews 20% of the map-pack weighting, second only to the Business Profile [8], and Sterling Sky's study of 8,186 businesses found that reviews gained in the current month mattered more than the total [18]. And 68% of consumers in BrightLocal's 2026 survey only use businesses rated four stars or higher [17].

Why do map results change from one part of town to another?

Because distance is one of Google's three local ranking factors, and it is measured from the searcher. Google uses the searcher's location even when the search names no place [26], so someone a few miles east sees a different map pack from someone a few miles west. Your relevance and prominence then compete with every business nearer that searcher. To see your real coverage, check from several points across your market, not just from your office.

Do I need a physical address to appear in the map pack?

You need to meet customers face to face, but they don't have to come to you [5]. Google lets service-area businesses hide their address and list up to 20 service areas within about two hours' drive, while virtual offices and P.O. boxes aren't eligible [6][15]. Whitespark's experts do score a physical address in the searched city as a strong map-pack factor [8], so a genuine storefront has an edge.

Where to start

Start with the profile and the reviews, which carry the most weight in the map pack in the experts' survey, then build the service pages and links that feed all three places.
