Google Guaranteed (Now Google Verified) vs an SEO Guarantee

Google retired its Google Guaranteed badge for one Google Verified badge in October 2025. What that ads badge is, how Local Services Ads rank, and how it differs from a first page guarantee with written terms.

Google Guaranteed no longer exists, and it was never a first page ranking promise. In October 2025, Google folded it and its other Local Services Ads badges into a single Google Verified badge, a trust mark on paid ads [1][3]. An SEO guarantee under written terms is a different thing: a promise from your agency about how much of an agreed phrase list shows on the first page of Google's unpaid results, and by when.

The short answer: an ads badge versus a first page promise

Direct answer

Google Guaranteed was a trust badge on Google's paid Local Services Ads. Since October 2025, one Google Verified badge has replaced it and the other ad badges [1][3]. A first page SEO guarantee is a written promise from an agency about unpaid results: what share of an agreed phrase list shows on page one, and by when. One is Google's label on an ad; the other is a contract.

The mix-up is easy to make: the badge and the first page promise share a word and a results page. They answer to different rules. Local Services Ads are ordered in an auction [4]. The map results, the organic results and Google's AI answers are not for sale. Google's own guidance says: "Google never accepts money to include or rank sites in our search results, and it costs nothing to appear in our organic search results." [5] For the map results, Google says there is "no way to request or pay for a better local ranking" [6].

Key takeaway

A badge on an ad tells searchers that Google checked the advertiser; it says nothing about where that business shows up in the unpaid results.

What Google Guaranteed was

Direct answer

Google Guaranteed was never a first page ranking promise. It was one of several trust badges Google showed on Local Services Ads profiles, next to Google Screened and License Verified by Google [1]. Google tied it to a reimbursement program: customers who booked a service through Local Services Ads could ask Google for reimbursement under its conditions [2]. It was a signal about the advertiser, not about rankings.

Local Services Ads are paid listings for local service providers, and Google ranks them in their own auction [4]. The badge belonged to those ad profiles, not to the map results or the organic results. Anyone offering to "get you Google Guaranteed" today is selling a retired badge, not a first page result.

What changed in October 2025: Google Verified

Direct answer

In August 2025, Google announced that one Google Verified badge would replace its Local Services Ads badges, including Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified by Google [1]; none was a first page ranking promise. The switch was set for October 20, 2025 [3]. Google also ended the reimbursement program attached to the old badge, keeping only services booked before December 7, 2025 eligible [2].

Here is what Google said, and what it means for you:

  • One badge. Google described the change as creating "a single, clear signal of a business' credibility" [1]. The Google Verified badge shows on eligible Local Services Ads profiles that have completed Google's screening and verification process [1].
  • The reimbursement program ended. Google's help page limits reimbursement requests to services booked through Local Services Ads before December 7, 2025, and says requests had to be filed within 30 days of the service being completed [2].
  • Ad ranking did not change. Google says Local Services Ads ranking is not affected by the switch [2].
  • The old help page is gone. Google's former help page for the old badge now forwards to the Google Verified page; it did when we checked on September 27, 2026 [7].

A sourcing note: Google's own announcement said only "Starting in October" [1]. The exact date comes from Google's notice to advertisers, as reported by Search Engine Roundtable [3].

How Local Services Ads rank (an auction)

Direct answer

Local Services Ads are ranked in an auction. For each search, Google orders the ad profiles by the advertiser's bid and overall profile quality [4]. Profile quality covers the rating, the number of reviews, average response time, high-quality images and the verification checks a business has completed [4]. The badge sits on a paid ad; the ad's place is bought, not earned in the map or organic results.

Before any of that, a business has to get in. Google says every business must pass its screening and verification process, which varies by business category and location and may include background, business registration, insurance and license checks, plus minimum review requirements [8].

Notice what overlaps. Reviews count toward ad profile quality [4], and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking too [6]. Genuine reviews are one asset that helps in both places, and how to get Google reviews covers asking for them within Google's rules.

Notice what doesn't. Buying ads does not lift your unpaid rankings. In Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts, taking part in Google Ads or other paid Google products is rated a myth as a map ranking factor [9].

What an SEO guarantee is, under written terms

Direct answer

An SEO guarantee is a promise from your agency, not from Google, so everything rests on its written terms. Ours is a first page guarantee: we guarantee up to 50% of the key phrases you could rank for will rank on the first page of Google, measured on set dates in three places. It is offered after an assessment, and it never promises a position.

Google's hiring advice states the limit plainly:

We agree, which is why the first page guarantee promises no position at all. Here is what the written terms set out:

  • The phrase list. Every variation your customers could type to find a business like yours, agreed with you in writing at the start. Searches for your own business name don't count.
  • The three places. Your Google Business Profile in Google's local map results, page one of the organic results, and AI suggestions and answers, meaning Google's own AI answer on the results page.
  • The schedule. At least 20% of the list on the first page within 90 days, 40% within 180 days, and 50% by the end of the first year.
  • Each place, separately. At each milestone, the share is measured separately in each of the three places, and each place has to reach it on its own. Google shows an AI answer for only some searches, so the written guarantee terms work out the AI share only over the phrases for which Google shows one when we check; if it shows none at a check, that milestone is judged in the map and organic results.
  • What it is not. Not every phrase, not a specific position, and not traffic, calls, leads or sales.
  • Who qualifies. It is offered after an assessment of your business and your market, and only if you qualify. The written terms also say what happens if a milestone is missed.

For the measurement behind those numbers, read how an SEO guarantee works. For the skeptic's version of the question, and Google's first page caveats, read can SEO be guaranteed.

Side by side: who gives it, what it covers, what backs it

Direct answer

The difference comes down to four questions: who makes the promise, where it applies, how placement is decided and what backs it. Google Verified is Google's badge on paid ads, placed by auction and backed by screening checks. A first page guarantee is your agency's written promise about unpaid results, backed by dated milestones set out in its terms.

Can you use both?

Direct answer

Yes. Local Services Ads and the unpaid results sit on the same page and follow different rules, so one does not replace the other. Ads win a paid slot through an auction [4]. The map results, the organic results and Google's AI answers cannot be bought [5][6]. Choosing between them, or running both, is the familiar trade-off between paid and unpaid search.

That trade-off is laid out in how dental SEO compares with PPC, and it applies well beyond dentistry. Either way, the profile work pays twice: reviews and ratings feed ad profile quality [4] and the map results [6], and Google Business Profile optimization is where that work starts.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google Guaranteed still exist?

No, and it was never a first page ranking promise. Google replaced it with a single Google Verified badge on Local Services Ads in October 2025, along with Google Screened and License Verified by Google [1][3]. Google's former help page for the old badge now forwards to the Google Verified page [7]. Anyone still selling that status is selling something that no longer exists.

What is the Google Verified badge?

It is the single trust badge Google now shows on Local Services Ads. Google says it appears on eligible ad profiles that have completed its screening and verification process, and that it replaced the earlier badges [1]. It belongs to paid ads. It says nothing about where a business shows up in the map results, the organic results or Google's AI answers.

Did Google end its reimbursement program for Local Services Ads?

Yes. Google discontinued the reimbursement program attached to the old badge as part of the switch [2]. Its help page limits reimbursement requests to services booked through Local Services Ads before December 7, 2025, filed within 30 days of the service being completed [2]. The new Google Verified badge is a verification signal, not a reimbursement program.

Is Google Guaranteed the same as an SEO guarantee with written terms?

No. Google Guaranteed was Google's badge on paid ads, which are ranked in an auction [4]. An SEO guarantee is your agency's promise about the unpaid results. Ours is a first page guarantee: a measured share of your agreed phrase list on the first page, checked separately in each of the three places on set dates, under written guarantee terms.

Does the Google Verified badge affect ad ranking?

Google says Local Services Ads ranking won't be affected by the change to the new badge [2]. Ads are still ordered in an auction on bid and profile quality, and profile quality includes the verification checks a business has completed, along with its rating, number of reviews, response time and images [4]. The checks behind the badge matter; the switch itself did not reorder the ads.

How does a business get the Google Verified badge?

By joining Local Services Ads and passing Google's screening and verification. Google says the checks vary by business category and location and may include background, business registration, insurance and license checks, plus minimum review requirements [8]. The badge then shows on eligible profiles that have completed that process [1]. The old badge can no longer be earned.

Which one do you need?

If you want paid leads with a trust badge beside your ad, Local Services Ads and Google Verified are Google's product. If you want to show up in the unpaid results, read how our first page guarantee works and see our client results. The free SEO audit shows where you stand today.
