The problem: the map results decide who gets the call

Search for a service and Google answers with a map and a short list of businesses, often before any website. It does this even when nobody types a place: Google says your location "helps to show you nearby results" even when the search leaves the place out. When we checked 12 commercial local searches from one U.S. city on September 27, 2026, the map results showed on 11 of them.

If your Business Profile is not in that short list, the call, the visit and the directions request go to a business that is. Google Maps alone has more than 2 billion users a month, by Google's February 2025 count, and Google is plain that nobody can buy a better spot: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

The unpaid map listings are also getting squeezed. Sterling Sky's State of Local SEO report for 2026 found ads in the local pack on almost 22% of its reports, up from 1%, and Local Services Ads on 31% of the queries it tracked, up from roughly 11%, as paid placements grew through 2025. Every paid slot is one less look at the listings you earn.

01 How we do it

What decides the map results

Google's own help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and explains the third factor under the heading "Prominence".

  • Relevance is how well your profile matches what someone searched for. Categories, services and the words on your pages drive it.
  • Distance is measured from the searcher, and Google estimates the location when the searcher does not share it. You cannot move your business, so distance is the factor nobody can work on.
  • Prominence is how well-known the business is. Google says it draws on how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 local search experts puts rough weights on the map pack: Business Profile signals 32%, reviews 20%, on-page signals 15%, links 8% and citations 6%. It is an expert-opinion survey, not a measurement of Google, and its authors say none of the experts has special access to Google's algorithm. Its top two factors are the primary category and the searcher's distance from the address.

Reviews matter because Google says so. Sterling Sky's study of 8,186 businesses in 200 cities found that the reviews a business got that month mattered more for "near me" rankings than its total count. Reviews come from your customers; our measurement counts the review count, rating and pace of every business ahead of you, so the gap is on paper.

02 How we do it

How we work your Business Profile

The order matters. First the address and hours: Google says a virtual office or P.O. box is not eligible, and hours must match the door. Then the categories, then services, photos and attributes, then the local pages and citations behind them. Google warns that editing a category can trigger a new verification.

The profile also feeds Google's AI now. Google shut down the Business Profile Q&A API on November 3, 2025, and is moving customer questions to AI answers in Google Maps. Ask Maps, a Gemini feature inside Google Maps, launched in the U.S. and India on March 12, 2026. Ask Maps and the AI answers in Google Maps do not count toward the first page guarantee, which counts the AI answer on Google's results page. Google also lists an up-to-date Business Profile among the fundamentals for its AI features, which is where our AI search optimization work picks up.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What does a local SEO agency do?

A local SEO agency works on the places a nearby customer finds you: your Google Business Profile in the map results and the local pages of your website. At First Page SEO Guarantee that means categories, services, hours and photos, a page for each service and area, consistent citations, and a measurement of the businesses already in the map results for each phrase on your list, checked on each milestone date.

How does Google decide who shows in the map results?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity, the third of which its help page explains under the heading "Prominence". Relevance is how well your profile matches the search, distance is measured from the searcher, and prominence draws on links and reviews. Google also says the categories you choose affect your local ranking, and that complete, accurate profiles are more likely to show up. Nobody can pay Google for a better local ranking.

How long does local SEO take?

Google's general guidance for search is that changes can take anywhere from a few hours to several months, and that it is worth waiting a few weeks before judging them. Our written terms set the milestones instead: at least 20% of your phrase list on the first page by day 90, 40% by day 180 and 50%+ by the end of the first year, measured in each of the three places separately, the map results included.

Can I show up in the map results in a city where I have no address?

Distance is measured from the searcher, so a business close to the search point has an edge that no one can buy. A service-area business hides its address and can list up to 20 service areas, within about two hours' drive of where it is based, but it cannot set a radius. A page on your website for each area you serve helps in the organic results, where Whitespark's experts put the city or neighborhood relevance of your content second on their list.

Does the first page guarantee count the map results?

Yes. The map results are one of the three places the first page guarantee measures, beside the organic results and Google's AI answers. A phrase counts in the map results when a search for it, made from your agreed market, shows your business there through your Google Business Profile. Your map share is measured separately from the other two places, and the written terms explain each check.
