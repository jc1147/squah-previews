The problem: traffic that leaves without a word

Every visitor costs something to win: the ranking work, the content, sometimes the ads. And clicks are scarce. SparkToro's 2024 study found that 58.5% of U.S. Google searches ended without a click, and that for every 1,000 searches only 360 clicks reached the open web, beyond Google's own properties and ads. Where Google shows an AI Overview, Advanced Web Ranking found that 74.3% of US desktop results pages ended without an organic click in July 2026, against 53.6% of pages without one.

So the visits you do get are worth more than they look. If your website has traffic and does not have conversions, it needs an upgrade. Visitors leave for reasons you can find: a page that buries the next step, a form that asks too much, a checkout with one step too many. Product checkouts are the hardest of them all, and the smallest changes can make the biggest difference.

So the first fix we reach for is not more traffic. It is finding where users leave your website altogether, and asking the bigger question: why.

01 How we do it

How we find where visitors leave

The work starts with evidence, not a redesign:

  • Read the funnel. We follow visitors from the page they land on to the step where they stop: a service page, a booking form, a checkout, a phone number they never tap.
  • Watch real sessions. Heat maps and session recordings show where people scroll past, hesitate or rage-click. User testing shows what they expected to find.
  • Ask why. Every drop gets a question and a likely answer: the offer is unclear, the form is too long, the proof is missing, the next step is hidden.
  • Compare with page one. The pages ahead of you on Google are the ones your visitors can compare you with. We measure how they present the same service, so your page answers the comparison.

The output is a list of hypotheses, each tied to one step of the funnel, sorted by how much it could change and how hard it is to build.

02 How we do it

How we test changes

Each hypothesis becomes a change, and each change is tested against the goal that matters: a lead, a booking or a sale.

  • One idea at a time. Where traffic allows, versions run side by side so the result belongs to the change, not the season.
  • Keep the winner. A version that wins becomes the page. One that loses is dropped, and what it taught goes into the next idea.
  • Ship without hand-offs. Because the same team builds and maintains your website, a winning change is live on your site without a ticket to another vendor.
  • Protect the rankings. Changes are made with your SEO in view.

Conversion rate percentages on this page would be guesses, so there are none. We measure yours instead, and report what each change did.

Frequently asked questions

What do conversion rate optimization services include?

At First Page SEO Guarantee, they include six parts: analytics that map where visitors leave, market research into what your customers value, a conversion strategy for how your offer is packaged and presented, copy and design changes, A/B testing to decide between versions, and personalization for different kinds of visitors. The same team that builds and maintains your website does all six, so every change ships without a hand-off.

When does a business need CRO?

If your website has traffic and does not have conversions, it needs an upgrade. If you are falling short of your sales or lead goals, it is probably time to see whether your website is performing at its peak. Conversion work pays off most where visitors already arrive, because every lead or sale it adds comes from traffic you have already earned or paid for.

What is A/B testing?

A/B testing shows two versions of a page, or one part of it, to different visitors at the same time and measures which one leads more people to act. The winner becomes the page, and the next test starts from there. Conversion rate optimization is a steady process built on these tests, which is why it earns better conversion rates month after month instead of all at once.

How is CRO different from a website redesign?

A redesign changes everything at once and hopes the result is better. Conversion rate optimization changes one thing at a time and measures whether it helped, so you keep every gain and learn from every loss. The two work together: our website team handles redesigns when a site needs one, and the conversion work keeps improving the new site after it launches.

Is CRO part of the first page guarantee?

Conversion work is part of the plan behind the first page guarantee, and the guarantee itself is about rankings. After an assessment, we guarantee up to 50% of the key phrases you could rank for will rank on the first page of Google, measured separately in each of the three places. It never promises traffic, leads, calls or sales; turning visits into customers is what the conversion work is for.

Can you improve the website I already have?

Yes. Conversion work starts with the site you have: its analytics, its pages and the steps where visitors stop. Our website team makes the changes on your current site, and if a test shows the site needs more than changes, we can upgrade it or build a new one. Either way, the same team builds, hosts, maintains and tests it.
