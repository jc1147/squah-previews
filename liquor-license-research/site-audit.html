SECTION 01

The site as it stands

A sitemap is the list of addresses a site hands to Google and says: these are my pages, please index them. Yours is made of ten files — one index plus nine lists — and every one of them loads correctly.

Between them those nine lists submit 1,007 addresses. We fetched all 1,007 ourselves, counted the words on each, read the tags in each page's head, and classified every response. That is a census, not a sample: there is no guesswork in the counts below.

What came back when we fetched all 1,007

A 99.9% real-page rate on a 1,007-page site is a good result. Most sites this size carry a tail of empty stubs and dead links. Yours does not.

The template mix

A template is a page design the site reuses with different data poured in. Almost your entire site is two templates: a city page and a county page. Everything else is a rounding error by count — which matters, because it means one fix applied to two templates reaches 90% of your site.

SECTION 02

The content is good, and here is the measurement that says so

The single most common failure on a site built from a template at this scale is that every page is the same page with the place name swapped. Yours is not. We measured this two ways and both agree.

First, length. Across the census, the median city detail page carries 1,348.5 visible words and the median county detail page 1,322. After we strip out every line of text that appears on 80% or more of the pages in the same template — the navigation, the footer, the standing calls to action — the median city page still has an estimated 868.5 words of its own, and the median county page 842. That second number is the one that matters, and it is labelled an estimate because the boilerplate-stripping rule is a threshold, not a certainty.

Second, overlap. We took a deterministic sample of 103 of your pages, cut each page's own text into every possible run of eight consecutive words, and compared each page against every other page of the same template — 91 pairings in a 14-page sample, 66 in a 12-page one. Two pages built from the same boilerplate with just a name swapped would share nearly every eight-word run. Your pages share almost none.

Reading the overlap score: it runs 0 to 1. A score of 1 means two pages share every eight-word run — identical writing. A score near 0 means they share almost none. Your four main templates land between 0.002 and 0.029, measured after the shared furniture was removed. Reading "own words": a labelled estimate, not a count, because it depends on the 80%-repetition threshold used to identify boilerplate.

How that compares to the competitor we measured

We ran the identical measurement — same fetcher, same parser, same every-Nth sampling rule, same eight-word comparison — over liquorlicenseauctioneers.com, the rival covered in the competitor report. This compares templates, not matched city-for-city pairs, and the two sites are not matched on anything except the method. Read it as a contrast in approach, not a like-for-like scoreboard.

Where your content genuinely is thin

No page on the site is thin by the usual test: 0 of 1,006 pages fall below 400 visible words, and 0 below 150. But 400 words of mostly-navigation is not 400 words of content. When we strip the repeated furniture, 22 of 1,006 pages carry fewer than 50 words of their own and 24 carry fewer than 150. These are the index and hub pages, not the city and county pages. Census medians of own words: the three state hubs 32, the 36 county index pages 266, the 24 city index pages 293. (The sampled table above reports the same templates at 38, 275 and 307.5 — a sample of 12 will not land exactly on a census of 36.) The thinnest single page we measured is /state/ at 481 visible words but an estimated 9 of its own.

Separately: every sampled page in the four main templates carried zero images of its own. Median 3 images per page, all of them shared site-wide — 14 of 14 sampled pages per template had no page-specific image. The homepage is the exception, with 16 images of which 13 are its own.

Other things the pages get right

Every sampled page on the four main templates carries a contact form (100%), a phone number (100%) and breadcrumb navigation (100%). Each has a median of 41 distinct internal links and 5 links out to other sites. 939 of 1,006 pages carry structured data — machine-readable tags that help search engines understand a page — in the form of breadcrumb markup on 938 of them. Median headings per city licence-type page: 6 second-level and 16 third-level. This is a well-built template set.

SECTION 03

The big defect: 908 pages disown their own address

A canonical tag is a line in a page's code that says to Google: if you find this content at more than one address, the real one is this. Google usually obeys it, drops the other addresses, and indexes only the one named.

On your site, 908 of the 1,006 working pages carry a canonical tag naming an address that is not the address the sitemap submitted. And not one of those 908 named addresses appears in any of your ten sitemap files. Zero of 908.

So the site is doing two contradictory things at once. The sitemap says index this address. The page at that address says no, index that other one instead. And the sitemap never mentions the other one.

Here is a real pair from your site. This is one page, reachable at two addresses today.

What your sitemap submits to Google /cities/tampa/tampa-cmb-license/ "Google, please index this page."
What that page's canonical tag says /cities/tampa-cmb-license/ "Actually, the real address is this one." — and this address is in no sitemap.

The only difference is the highlighted tampa/ folder in the middle. That is the whole defect, repeated 908 times. Both addresses return a working page right now; the trailing slash is kept on both sides.

The pattern is perfectly consistent, which is the good news

We tested one rule against all 908 pairs: drop the middle folder, keep the trailing slash. It reproduced the canonical address correctly on 908 of 908. Zero exceptions. The trailing slash was kept on 908 and dropped on none.

That matters commercially. A defect with 908 special cases is a research project. A defect with one rule and no exceptions is a scripted change with a checkable result.

Where every page's canonical tag points

Census, n = 1,006 working pages plus the 1 redirecting address.

  • 908 point at a different address — 90.3% of working pages. 505 city pages, 403 county pages.
  • 96 point at themselves, exactly. These are correct.
  • 2 point at themselves apart from a trailing slash.
  • 1 has no canonical tag at all — the address that redirects to your error page.

Both copies are live, and there are more than two

We probed 40 of the 908 canonical addresses, chosen by a fixed every-Nth rule. All 40 returned a working page, and on all 40 the title and word count matched the sitemap address's page to within two words. That is 40 of 40 confirmed duplicate pairs — the same content served at two live addresses, with the sitemap submitting the one the page itself disowns.

It goes one step further. During the challenge round, live fetches confirmed that the slash-less form of these addresses also returns a working page with identical content and no redirect. So each of the 908 pairs is really four live addresses for one page, and a fix that only handles the slash-bearing forms will report success while leaving duplicates behind.

SECTION 04

Why it matters: Google has already picked a side

This is the finding that turns a technical curiosity into a business problem. We pulled the complete list of search phrases your domain currently ranks for — 244 of them — and checked which address each ranking sits on.

Google is ranking you on the addresses your sitemap does not submit, and largely ignoring the ones it does.

Census of all 244 ranked keywords recorded for the domain on 12 September 2026, matched to address shape by exact string. 99 + 12 + 133 = 244.

Read the traffic column as a scale, not a count: 491.4 is DataForSEO's model of monthly visits from these 244 positions, not a figure from your analytics. We have no Google Search Console or analytics access, so nothing on this page is measured sessions.

Nearly half your rankings sit on an address you never submitted

46.7% of your ranked keywords — 114 of 244, counting 99 on canonical addresses and 15 on one address absent from every sitemap — sit on a URL your sitemap does not contain. Those 99 rankings are spread across 53 distinct addresses. The same measurement run over the rival found zero equivalent rows: every address the rival ranks on matched its own sitemap.

This is an association observed in the ranking addresses a third-party tool recorded, not a read of Google's index. It tells us which address Google showed, not why it chose it.

The honest size of the current position

So the scale is clear: across 1,007 submitted addresses, the domain currently holds 244 ranked keywords on 80 addresses, of which 10 sit in the top three results and 26 in the top ten. The rival holds 713 ranked keywords, 145 top-three and 292 top-ten, from 3,868 submitted addresses. On the 164 keywords both sites rank for — a paired comparison, so keyword-set size cannot bias it — the rival ranks better on 138 and you rank better on 26, with a median gap of 22 positions.

That gap is real and the competitor report deals with it properly. What belongs on this page is narrower: your top three results are concentrated on a handful of pages, and the pages carrying them are mostly not the 908. The home page holds 26 ranked keywords including three number-one positions (liquor license broker california, liquor license brokers california, liquor license agent). The California Type 41 page holds 10 keywords with four in the top three. Those pages are healthy and self-canonical. The 908-page estate — the bulk of the site — is where the signal is being split.

One page ranks well from outside the sitemap entirely

The address /california/type-42-liquor-license/ holds 15 ranked keywords and a modelled 60.0 monthly visits, with a best position of 2. It appears in none of the ten sitemap files. Across our capture of 197 search results it also drew 3 of the 11 citations the client domain received in Google's AI Overviews — the joint highest of any page on the site, level with the Type 47 page.

But the story moved while we were measuring it. A live fetch on the same day found that address now returns a 301 redirect to /california-type-42-on-sale-beer-and-wine-public-premises-license/ — which is in the pages sitemap, returns a working page and points its canonical at itself. Meanwhile the California hub page still carries three links to the old redirecting address and none to the destination. The remaining defect is therefore navigational, not a missing sitemap entry. We have flagged this in the unresolved list below rather than smoothing it over.

SECTION 05

The 49 pairs nobody should touch with a script

If you hand a developer "908 pairs, redirect the old to the new", 49 of those instructions will take a working page off the internet. We know because we probed all 908 destination addresses one by one.

By template: 34 of the 505 city pairs and 15 of the 403 county pairs are affected — 5.4% of all 908.

What the loop looks like, from a live trace

Take the San Francisco Type 48 page. The flat address /cities/san-francisco-type-48-full-liquor-license-for-bars-and-nightclubs/ today redirects to the nested address /cities/san-francisco/san-francisco-type-48-.../. That nested page loads fine — and its canonical tag points straight back at the flat address that just redirected away from it. A redirect and a canonical tag are already contradicting each other on this page today. Add the planned nested→flat redirect and the two addresses point at each other forever.

The exposure is not hypothetical. Six of these 41 loop-back addresses currently rank, carrying 16 keywords between them and a modelled 4.51 monthly visits. Five of the nested addresses in the loop set are linked from 1,046 of 1,057 captured pages — they sit in site-wide navigation. And the ranked keywords all sit on the flat side of these pairs; the nested side holds none of them.

The eight that land somewhere else

These are worse than a loop, because each one silently substitutes different content. All eight are city overview pages.

Why this section exists at all

The remediation work order for this site was put through a structured challenge round: eight of its ten tasks were attacked by three independent reviewers each — 24 reviews — with a brief to break the task, not to confirm it. All eight were refuted and rewritten. The 49 held pairs were found by that round and not by the original plan. The other corrections it produced are woven through this report where they change a number. We are telling you this because a plan that has survived an attack is worth more than one that has not, and because the original version of this plan would have broken 49 working pages.

SECTION 07

What is not wrong

Scope matters as much as fault. Several of the failures that most often force a rebuild are measurably absent here, and each absence was checked on a fetched page rather than assumed.

  • ✓Nothing is hidden from Google. 0 of 1,006 pages carry a noindex instruction, in the page or in the HTTP response. Your robots.txt is 139 bytes with a single rule group that allows everything except /register, /login and /sg-admin/. 0 public pages are blocked.
  • ✓No canonical points off your own site. 0 off-host canonicals and 0 staging-host canonicals. This is the version of this defect that leaks a site's ranking to somebody else's domain, or to a development server. It is not happening.
  • ✓No page is missing its canonical tag. All 1,006 working pages carry exactly one. The 908 point at the wrong address — but the tag is there, it is consistent, and the pattern has zero exceptions.
  • ✓Nothing is thin by the usual test. 0 of 1,006 pages fall below 400 visible words; 0 below 150. On the one page concept both sites build — the county licence-type page — your sampled median is 1,309 visible words against the rival's 633. The real soft spot is 22 index and hub pages with under 50 words of their own, named in section 02.
  • ✓Nothing is broken. 0 dead addresses, 0 server errors, 0 fetch failures, 0 responses blocked by rate limiting, 0 redirect chains longer than one hop. The one redirect that exists is the La Cañada Flintridge page.
  • ✓The sitemap is accurate about itself. All ten files load. The 1,007 addresses they list matched our census address for address — none added, none missing. The problem is not a stale or broken sitemap; it is a sitemap that lists the wrong half of each pair.
  • ✓The pages do not need JavaScript. Main body content was present in raw HTML on every sampled page of the four main templates. A search engine sees your content on the first pass.
  • ✓The content does not need rewriting. This is the important one. The defect list above is almost entirely addresses, tags and sitemap entries — the plumbing. The writing, which is the expensive part to fix, measures well.

What this adds up to

You have a well-written 1,006-page site with one systematic plumbing fault repeated 908 times, 49 landmines inside that fault, and a list of tag-level defects with exact counts. None of it requires new content. All of it is checkable before and after, because every number on this page came from a fetch that can be repeated.

SECTION 08

What we could not settle

This list is part of the deliverable. Anything below is either unmeasurable without access we do not have, or a genuine contradiction in the evidence that we are not going to paper over.

  • We have no Google Search Console or analytics access, so nothing here is measured traffic. Every traffic figure on this page is DataForSEO's model. We cannot tell you how many pages Google has actually indexed, which of each duplicate pair it chose, how many impressions any page gets, or what the 908 defect has cost in sessions. Search Console access for both domains is the single highest-value thing you could hand us, and the remediation plan cannot verify its own sitemap resubmission without it.
  • We infer which address Google chose from ranking data, not from Google. The claim "Google ranks you on the canonical addresses" rests on the addresses a third-party tool recorded in search results on 12 September 2026. It is an association between address shape and observed position. It is not a read of Google's index, and it does not establish that the canonical tag caused the choice.
  • Why 49 destination addresses already redirect is unknown. We can see that they do, and exactly where each one goes. We have no access to the content management system's routing configuration, so we cannot say whether these are deliberate merges, leftovers from an earlier cleanup, or a bug. Until someone reads that configuration, nobody can promise the same fault will not regenerate after a fix.
  • The Type 42 page's real status is in dispute within our own evidence. The ranking data records 15 keywords on /california/type-42-liquor-license/. A live fetch the same day found that address returning a 301 redirect. Both records are from 12 September 2026. Which address Google is currently showing cannot be settled without Search Console. Treat the 15 keywords as attached to the page's content, not reliably to that address.
  • The AI Overview citation count depends on how you count. Across 197 captured search results, the client domain appears as a cited source 11 times, spread over 10 distinct search phrases. Counting citation instances gives 11; counting queries gives 10. The Type 42 page holds 3 of the 11, level with the Type 47 page. An earlier draft of the remediation plan called it the site's single most-cited page; the re-derivation shows a tie, and we have corrected it here.
  • The "own words" figures are labelled estimates, not counts. They depend on a threshold: any line of text appearing on 80% or more of the pages in the same template is treated as furniture and subtracted. Move the threshold and the numbers move. The total word counts and the overlap scores are not affected.
  • Internal-link reachability is a lower bound. Only the 103 sampled pages contributed links to that measurement, so "0 links to the county index template" means 0 from those 103 pages. It is strong evidence, not proof. A full crawl of all 1,006 pages would settle it.
  • Several templates hold a single page, so they have no spread. The home page, the store page, the cities hub and the counties hub are one page each, and the finer classification adds two more single-page hybrids. Their figures are single observations, not medians of anything, and no confidence interval can be put around them.
  • How many live addresses the site really serves is not finally counted. We censused 1,007 sitemap addresses and probed all 908 canonical destinations, and 0 of those 908 appear in any sitemap — so the site serves at least 1,915 distinct addresses. Live checks then showed the slash-less forms also serve identical pages, which pushes the true figure higher. We did not enumerate it. Any fix has to handle both slash shapes or it will pass its own test while leaving duplicates in place.

Method, and how to read the numbers

What was a census. All 1,007 addresses in the ten sitemap files were fetched and classified — status, redirect chain, canonical tag, title, meta description, headings, word count, links, structured data. All 908 canonical destination addresses were fetched separately with redirect-following switched off. All 244 ranked keywords recorded for the domain were pulled and each matched to its address shape. These counts are exact. One qualification on the last of them: it is a census of what DataForSEO's index holds for this domain in the United States, in English, for standard organic results — which is a complete pull, but not the same thing as a census of everything Google has ever ranked the site for.

What was sampled. The page-anatomy measurements — own-word share, the eight-word overlap score, heading and image counts, form and phone presence, raw-HTML content checks and internal-link reachability — come from 103 client pages selected by a fixed every-Nth rule over each template's sorted address list. No randomness is used anywhere, so a re-run selects the same pages. Sample sizes are stated in each table. The competitor comparison used the identical method on 119 pages of liquorlicenseauctioneers.com and compares templates, not matched place-for-place pairs.

What is modelled. Every traffic and search-volume figure on this page is a DataForSEO model estimate, not measured sessions and not a Google Ads figure. We have no access to this site's Search Console or analytics. Read modelled numbers as a relative scale.

What this evidence can and cannot show. All of it is observation of public search results and public pages. It shows which things sit together. It does not establish that any measured property causes any ranking, and no claim on this page says otherwise. Every zero here is backed by a fetched page whose surface was parsed for the thing reported missing.
