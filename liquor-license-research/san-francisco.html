The worked example

San Francisco, end to end

One city, taken all the way through, as the template for the rest. Thirteen San Francisco pages exist today across two competing address trees. Five should exist. This is every URL, canonical tag, title, description, heading, paragraph and internal link that changes — written out, not described.

Census pulled 12 September 2026. All thirteen pages re-fetched live 13 September 2026. Every figure re-verified against the source files before it was printed here.

Defect one — the wrong-folder bug

San Francisco County Type 47 is filed inside the Los Angeles County folder. The canonical tag on it is right; the address it lives at is wrong.

The address in the sitemap /counties/los-angeles-county/san-francisco-county-type-47/ Live HTTP 200, 13 September 2026. Indexable, in the sitemap, and inside another county’s folder.
The canonical it declares /counties/san-francisco-county-type-47/ Live HTTP 200, 13 September 2026. Correct, and not in any sitemap.

How far this reaches, measured — and a correction

Searching the census for every two-level geo address whose slug does not match its folder returns 84 pages. That number is misleading and should not be reported as the bug count. Eighty-two of the 84 sit in /counties/arizona-county/ (15) and /counties/florida-county/ (67) — folders named after a state, holding that state’s counties. Neither folder has a page of its own, and every slug inside them names a county of that state. That is an inconsistent naming convention, not a misfiling.

Filter to folders proven real by their own sibling pages, and the genuine cross-jurisdiction misfiles across the whole site are two: this San Francisco Type 47 page under Los Angeles County, and /cities/la-mirada/laguna-woods-type-61-license/ — a Laguna Woods page inside the La Mirada folder. A third address, /counties/pinellas-county/liquor-license-agents-supports-2cop/, is an article with no geography in its slug sitting in a county folder: a stray, not a misfile.

Defect two — the doubled H1

On /counties/san-francisco-county/san-francisco-county-liquor-licenses/ the heading text is printed twice inside a single H1 element. Verbatim from the live HTML, 13 September 2026:

<h1 class="h1 article-title sg-mb-5">San Francisco County Liquor Licenses for Restaurants, Bars, and RetailersSan Francisco County Liquor Licenses for Restaurants, Bars, and Retailers</h1>

One element, 146 characters, the sentence repeated with no separator. The census recorded it, and the live page still serves it.

Defect three — the truncated description, which is not a page defect

The data overruled the brief here

The census records the description on that same page as 41 characters, cut mid-sentence: San Francisco County is one of California. The live page does not serve that. It serves the complete sentence, 220 characters:

“San Francisco County is one of California’s most active hospitality and retail markets, with liquor licensing needs that range from neighborhood restaurants to high volume nightlife districts, hotels, and package stores.”

The 41-character value is an instrument artefact. The census parser terminated the content attribute at the first apostrophe — the one in California’s — and 41 characters is exactly the text up to that point. The same page’s Type 20 sibling is recorded at 20 characters, San Francisco County, which is exactly the text before the apostrophe in County’s; its real description is 146 characters and perfectly fine.

Control test. The census records /cities/beverly-hills/beverly-hills-type-20-license/ at 13 characters, Beverly Hills. Fetched live, that page serves a 197-character description beginning Beverly Hills’s grocery stores. The artefact reproduces. 85 of the 1,007 census rows carry a description under 100 characters, and every single one of them ends without terminal punctuation on a word that would be followed by an apostrophe — a place name, or California, County, Florida, Arizona. Treat all 85 recorded lengths as unreliable and re-measure them from live HTML before acting.

There is still a real defect on that page. Its description is 220 characters, roughly 60 past the point Google truncates. So is Type 21 at 243, Type 48 at 211 and Type 47 at 186. Seven of the thirteen exceed 160 characters, and the county hub has no description at all.

Defect four — the county hub is empty

/counties/san-francisco-county is the page that should be the front door for the whole county. It carries no H1 element at all, an empty description, 700 total words and 228 of its own — the weakest page of the thirteen by both measures, and the only one below the rival hub template’s median on either. It is also the only one of the thirteen whose canonical points at itself.

Defect five — titles and descriptions past the limits

Seven of the thirteen titles run past the roughly 60 characters Google will display: 73, 72, 70, 66, 66, 65 and 64. All seven are on the /counties/ set, except the 65-character Type 41 city page. Seven descriptions exceed 160 characters — 243, 220, 211, 186, 171, 169, 168 — and one is empty. The six /cities/ titles are all inside the limit; the shape that fails is the county naming pattern, which prefixes every heading with the words “San Francisco County” and then spells the licence out in full.

03

Why it fails

San Francisco is one jurisdiction, and the site built two

San Francisco is a consolidated city and county. The City and County of San Francisco is a single government covering a single 47-square-mile territory, and one California ABC district licenses all of it. There is no San Francisco city licence that differs from a San Francisco county licence. They are the same licence, issued by the same authority, for the same ground.

So the /cities/san-francisco/ set and the /counties/san-francisco-county/ set are not complementary coverage of two places. They are two page sets built for the same place, answering the same questions, for the same searchers. This is the root architectural error. Every other defect on this page is downstream of it or independent of it; this one is the reason thirteen pages exist where five will do.

The duplication is total and deliberate-looking. Both sets cover the same five licence types — 20, 21, 41, 47 and 48 — plus an overview page each. Both sets are built from the same template family. Both sit in the sitemap. The only structural difference is which of the two folders they live in.

The cannibalisation is visible in which page wins

When two pages answer the same query, a search engine picks one. Here it picks badly, and the pattern is legible. The generic city query — the most valuable term in the set — is not answered by the San Francisco overview page, nor by the county hub. It is answered by the page about bars and nightclubs.

The writing is the strongest asset in this set, and none of it should be thrown away. What it needs is to be on fewer addresses, with stock attached.

A qualification on the “substance floor”

The figure of 1,099 total words and 528 own words is the median of a census of eight rival state hubs. Four of those eight fall below 1,099 words and four below 528 own words, which is what a median of eight does by construction. It is a useful reference point and it is not a floor, and the reviewers’ own note on the file says so: “A floor that half the reference population fails is not a floor.” It also belongs to the state hub template. San Francisco’s destination is a county page, whose rival median is 724 words and 339 own words. Both numbers are already cleared several times over by the pages being consolidated, so substance is not the binding constraint in San Francisco. Do not spend the budget there.

What the rival has that the client does not

Fetched live on 13 September 2026, the rival’s single San Francisco page carries a 51-character title, a 154-character description, one H1 — “San Francisco Liquor Licenses for Sale” — four H2 sections, a county filter, a licence-type filter, and a table headed Auction / Asking Price / Action with three priced rows on it and a “Make An Offer” button on each.

Behind that page, the listing census counted 30 San Francisco County licences on the rival’s shelf: Type 47 ×18, Type 21 ×10, Type 48 ×1, Type 20 ×1. Prices run from $15,000 to $350,000 with a median of $205,000. Within Type 47, eighteen licences from $150,000 to $350,000, median $235,000. Within Type 21, ten from $15,000 to $240,000, median $27,000. All three listings shown on the county page today are among those thirty.

Note what that means for the build: the page beating thirteen client pages by 5.3× shows three rows, not thirty. The lever is not volume of stock on the page. It is that the page has a price on it at all.

Confidentiality holds on both sides of this comparison

Across all 30 San Francisco listings the rival publishes county, licence type, price and availability, and nothing else. Zero publish a licence number, a licensee name or a premises address; all 30 display the brokerage as the seller rather than a person. The target architecture in section 4 keeps that rule and is not loosened by anything in this document. One difference worth knowing: the rival publishes no date of any kind on any of the 30 — no listed date, no days on market. Publishing days on market, as the New Jersey preview does, is a point of difference, not a copy.

Type 41 does not get a page

Both existing Type 41 pages — city and county — rank for nothing. There is not a single Type 41 licence in the 30-strong San Francisco market census. There is no stock to put on it and no demand reaching it. Its 883 and 850 own words are good writing, so they are not deleted: they become the Type 41 block inside the hub’s licence-type section, at /california/counties/san-francisco/#type-41, and both old addresses redirect there. If San Francisco Type 41 stock appears later, promote the block to its own page at /california/counties/san-francisco/type-41/ using the rule this document sets: a licence type earns its own address when there is stock to put on it.

What the destination looks like once built is the New Jersey preview — a state hub carrying priced rows on the page itself, with the listing pages beneath it. Every licence, price and date in that preview is invented sample data published so the layout could be reviewed. San Francisco takes the same shape one level down, at county rather than state, because in California the county is where an ABC licence actually transfers.

Redirecting only the thirteen sitemap addresses would leave all 4.2 modelled visits pointing at addresses that still resolve to the old content. Redirect both shapes to the same destination, in the same release.

Canonicals and sitemap

Is any San Francisco address in the 49-page hold list? Yes — two.

The answer, with the evidence

The site-wide canonical rule is /{cities|counties}/{folder}/{slug}/ → /{cities|counties}/{slug}/ — drop the middle folder segment, keep the trailing slash. Re-derived against the census, it reproduces on all 908 affected pairs with zero exceptions. Of the 908 destinations probed, 859 return 200 and are safe; 49 return a 3xx and must be held (41 of them loop back to their own nested address, 8 go elsewhere).

Two of the thirteen San Francisco pages are in that 49. Both are on the /cities/ path, and both loop:

  • /cities/san-francisco/san-francisco-type-21-full-liquor-license-for-liquor-stores/
  • /cities/san-francisco/san-francisco-type-48-full-liquor-license-for-bars-and-nightclubs/

Probed live on 13 September 2026 with redirect-following off, each one’s canonical target returns 301 with a Location header pointing straight back at the nested page it came from. The other ten targets returned 200. The hold list reproduces exactly, one day on.

Why it matters here. The Type 48 page is the most valuable address in San Francisco — six of the nine ranked terms, including all three phrasings of the generic city query. A bulk canonical repoint applied without the hold filter would put the site’s best-performing San Francisco address into a redirect loop.

The instruction

  1. Take all thirteen San Francisco addresses out of the bulk canonical repoint batch entirely. They are being rebuilt, and repointing a canonical this week at an address that gets redirected next week is churn that costs a re-crawl for nothing. The remaining 895 pairs proceed as the sitemap plan specifies.
  2. On each of the five new pages, set a self-referencing canonical to its own address with the trailing slash, on the https://www.liquorlicenseagents.com host. Five pages, five canonicals, each pointing at itself. No page in the new San Francisco tree names any other page as its home.
  3. Remove all thirteen old addresses from the sitemap in the same release as the redirects go live. A 301 that stays in a sitemap tells Google the address is still a destination.
  4. Add the five new addresses to the sitemap. This is the first time in San Francisco that the address the site submits, the address the canonical names and the address that ranks will all be the same string. Today they are three different strings, and the one that ranks is in no sitemap at all.
  5. Do not add the twelve canonical targets to the sitemap. They are being redirected, not kept.
  6. Retire the folders. Once the redirects are live, /cities/san-francisco/ and /counties/san-francisco-county/ hold nothing. The wrong-folder Type 47 page is resolved by this and needs no separate fix — it is redirected off the Los Angeles folder along with everything else.

One thing to verify before release, not assume

The site serves at least 1,915 distinct addresses across the two shapes, and the earlier audit found the slash-less forms also serve identical pages. Confirm that each of the 25 redirects fires on both the trailing-slash and the slash-less form of its source, and that none of the 25 produces a chain longer than one hop. A redirect map that passes its own test while leaving the slash-less duplicates live has not fixed anything.

Three deliberate choices in these strings

“For Sale”, not “Essential Guide”. Two of the current titles end “Essential Guide”, which promises an article. The searcher typing “liquor license san francisco” is trying to get one, and the rival’s winning title is “Buy San Francisco Liquor Licenses Online 24/7”. Match the intent.

No “County” in any title. The word costs seven characters, pushes every current county title past 60, and describes a distinction that does not exist in San Francisco. “Prices” in every title. It is the one thing on the new pages that is not on the old ones, and it is the difference between the two sites.

A Type 47 is the licence a San Francisco restaurant needs to pour spirits as well as beer and wine. Formally it is an On-Sale General Eating Place licence, and the “eating place” half is the part that catches people out: the kitchen has to stay open and serving whenever alcohol is served, and food has to be a real part of the business rather than a bowl of crisps at the bar. Get that wrong and the licence is at risk, which is why a Type 47 and a Type 48 are not interchangeable even though both allow spirits.

It is also the deepest part of the San Francisco market. Type 47 licences here trade between $150,000 and $350,000, and the spread inside that band is mostly location and condition rather than anything about the licence itself. The rules for the new tree

  1. R1Down: the hub links to all four type pages, in the same block, on every page view. Not a sidebar that appears on some templates. One block, in the licence-type section, with the same four links in the same order, each on the descriptive anchor — “San Francisco Type 47 licences”, not “read more”.
  2. R2Up: every type page links back to the hub, from two places. Once in the breadcrumb, once in a closing block. The breadcrumb is California › San Francisco › Type 47, and each segment is a real link to a real page.
  3. R3Sideways: each type page links to the other three type pages, and to nothing else in San Francisco. Four pages, each carrying three sibling links. Type 47 and Type 48 link to each other prominently, because that is the choice buyers are genuinely making.
  4. R4No page in the San Francisco tree links to any retired address. All thirteen old addresses and all twelve canonical targets are gone from the navigation in the same release as the redirects. A 301 in a navigation block is a link to a redirect, and it will still be crawled as one.
  5. R5Up again: the California hub links to the San Francisco hub, and only to the hub. Not to the four type pages. The state hub’s job is to route to counties; the county’s job is to route to types. One link per level.
  6. R6Across: the hub links to neighbouring county hubs, and type pages never do. A Type 47 page linking to another county’s pages is how a set of pages loses its own subject. Cross-county routing happens once, at hub level.
  7. R7Every listing row links to its own listing page, and every listing page links back to the type page and the hub. This is the path that carries a buyer from a search result to an offer, and it is the only reason the tree exists.
  8. R8The fixed cross-links to Los Angeles, San Diego, Orange and Ventura come off these pages. They appear on all thirteen pages today, irrespective of relevance, and they are the largest single block of outbound internal links on a San Francisco page. Replace them with rule R6’s neighbouring counties on the hub only.

The wrong-folder pattern is 2 pages site-wide, not 84. Method, and how to read the numbers

What was a census. All 1,007 addresses in the site’s ten sitemap files were fetched and classified on 12 September 2026 — status, redirect chain, canonical tag, title, description, headings, word count, links and structured data. All 908 canonical destinations were probed separately with redirect-following switched off: 859 returned 200, 49 returned a 3xx. All 244 ranked keywords recorded for the client domain and all 713 for the rival were pulled and each matched to its address. All 1,227 of the rival’s listing pages were fetched and parsed, which is where the 30 San Francisco County listings and their prices come from. These counts are exact for what was fetched; the keyword counts are a complete pull of what DataForSEO’s index holds for each domain in the United States, in English, for standard organic results, which is not the same as everything Google has ever ranked.

What was checked live for this document. On 13 September 2026 all thirteen San Francisco addresses were re-fetched with a real desktop User-Agent at under two requests a second, and their titles, descriptions, canonicals, headings and internal links re-parsed. All twelve canonical targets were probed with redirect-following off. One control page outside San Francisco was fetched to test the description-length artefact. The rival’s San Francisco page was fetched and parsed by the same method. Every live result either reproduced the census or is reported above as a disagreement.

What was sampled. The own-word medians by template come from 103 client pages and 119 rival pages selected by a fixed every-Nth rule over each template’s sorted address list, so a re-run selects the same pages. They compare templates, not matched place-for-place pairs. “Own words” strips text appearing on 80% or more of that template’s sampled pages, which makes it a labelled estimate rather than a raw count.

What is modelled. Every traffic figure here — 4.2, 22.2, 6.81, 0.79 and the rest — is a DataForSEO estimate calculated from ranking position and search volume. It is not measured sessions and not a Google Ads figure. No analytics or Search Console data from either site entered this research. Search volumes are estimates built on Google Ads data, not Google Ads figures. Positions and keyword counts are exact.

Association, not cause. This is observation of public search results and public pages throughout. It shows which kinds of page sit in the positions that earn the clicks. It does not establish that any page property causes any ranking, and no recommendation above is presented as a guaranteed outcome. The list of what this cannot settle is in section 10 and is part of the deliverable.

Measurement dates: census 12 September 2026; live re-verification 13 September 2026. No paid search API was called for this document. Figures will drift as the site and the search results change — re-run before acting on any count more than a few weeks old.
