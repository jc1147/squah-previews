Evidence 04 · The architecture question

Publish the content, gate the deal

Private listings look like a problem: publish them and they stop being private, hide them and they cannot rank. The rival has already solved this, and the solution is measurable.

All 1,227 of their offer buttons resolve to a login. Yet every listing renders its asking price, county, licence type and description to an anonymous visitor. Across all 3,868 URLs there are zero noindex tags, zero robots blocks. The gate is a form, never an index barrier.

Tier 1 · public, indexed

State and county hubs

Live inventory rows inline with count, price, county and type, plus original guidance on the same URL. Floor is 1,099 total words and 528 unique, the measured median of the template carrying 57% of their traffic.

Tier 2 · public item pages

Individual listings

Built for conversion and trust, not rankings. They earn 1.8% for the rival, so do not over-invest, but they make the hub credible.

Tier 3 · teased, gated

The private book

Publish the honest aggregate: a real count, a real price band, a real county and type. Withhold the identifying detail behind a qualification form. The page keeps genuine substance and the listing stays private.

Tier 4 · never rendered

Confidential stock

Not published in any form, not counted, not teased.

One hard rule: never show a crawler something a visitor cannot see. That is cloaking and it is not on the table.

Evidence 06 · The wedge

What they cannot answer

I fetched all 1,227 listings rather than sampling. The blanket "fake listings" claim is contradicted: prices are coherent and varied, stock genuinely turns over, and their seller intake demands an ABC licence number. But the specific failures are where Clint wins.

Their weakness

  • No listing carries any date. Zero of 1,227 declare one, so a licence posted last week looks identical to one from 2020.
  • Six distinct images serve all 1,227 pages, so no listing shows the buyer what the place could be.
  • 132 description texts cover 1,198 listings. New Jersey's 131 share four. Michigan's 19 share two.
  • The view counter is fabricated: bounded 0 to 36, every integer present, no correlation with listing age.
  • 28 listings of 1,227 have ever taken a bid.

His answer

  • Genuine posted and updated dates, and a visible "added this week" surface. First access only shows if the date is on the page.
  • A venue concept image on every listing, so a buyer can picture the business. Never the actual premises, which stays confidential.
  • Per-licence description written once per asset, not once per state.
  • Honest activity or none at all. Do not copy an invented counter.
  • More stock, earlier, plus a private book they structurally cannot match.

A free gift while you build: all 1,227 of their titles currently render $0k. Archived pages show real prices in titles as recently as June 2026, so this is a fresh regression on their highest-value template.

Evidence 07 · Fix regardless

90% of the site points away from itself

Of 1,006 live client pages, 908 carry a canonical tag pointing at a different URL, and not one of those 908 targets is in the sitemap. I probed 40 of them: all returned 200, and all 40 served an identical title and word count to their sitemap twin.

So the same page sits at two live URLs, and the sitemap submits the one the site itself disowns. This is why 114 of the client's 244 ranking keywords land on URLs absent from the sitemap. Google is following the canonicals correctly. The sitemap is simply pointed at the wrong set.

It is a configuration fix, not a content project, and it should happen whatever else you decide.

Before you commit

What this does not settle

He publishes nothing today. Measured live: the only commerce URL on the site returns "No products found" in server-rendered HTML while sitting in the sitemap at priority 0.95. The inventory exists in the business, not on the website. Everything here depends on closing that gap.
First access is a conversion edge, not a ranking one. Getting stock early wins trust and deals. On the search side it only counts if listings carry visible dates and get crawled quickly, and the rival shows no dates at all.
Compliance needs a lawyer, not me. Advertising licence transfers carries state regulatory exposure, and a gated private tier may carry disclosure obligations of its own. Flagging, not ruling.
