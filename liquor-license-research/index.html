The research bundle

Four reports, one question

Two companies sell liquor licences into the same eight American states, and one of them takes roughly eleven times the search traffic of the other. We measured both sites in full on 12 September 2026 — every address either one submits to Google, every search term either one ranks for, all 1,227 of the rival’s listing pages, and 197 live searches — to find out why.

The answer is not page count, keyword choice, state coverage or links: each of those was tested and each failed to explain the gap. What separates the two sites is that eight plain state-level pages on the rival’s domain hold 49 top-three positions and carry 57.0% of its modelled traffic, while the client’s eight equivalent pages hold none — and the client’s own writing, page for page, measures better than the rival’s.

All data pulled 12 September 2026; reports written 13 September 2026. Every figure below is quoted from the report it sits under. Read that report’s own method note before quoting a number onward.

How this was measured

Dates
Every measurement was taken on 12 September 2026, including all live page fetches. The reports were written on 13 September 2026. Anything described as live state — the rival’s on-page stock counts, the 49 held addresses — will have drifted since, and each report says where.
What was a census
A census counts every item, so the figure is exact. All 1,007 of the client’s sitemap addresses. All 1,227 of the rival’s listing pages, and all 3,868 addresses in its sitemap. All 713 search terms the rival ranks for and all 244 the client ranks for, the 549-term gap between them, the 71-month history of both, and the link counts. All 908 duplicate address pairs, with each target address probed one at a time: 859 came back clean, 49 held. A census is complete for the supplier’s index or for the site fetched — which is not the same as complete for the web.
What was sampled
A sample checks part of the whole, so it carries uncertainty. The 197 live searches were chosen by a published priority rule rather than at random, which tilts them towards terms one of these two sites already touches. Also sampled: the live check on the 50 highest-volume gap terms, the page-anatomy and content-overlap measurements at 12–16 pages per template, and the 38 live page fetches behind the work order’s premise checks.
What is estimated
“Modelled traffic” is a calculation from ranking position and search volume, not measured sessions. No analytics and no Search Console data from any site entered this research, on either side. Monthly search volumes, click costs and difficulty scores are DataForSEO estimates built on Google Ads data — they are not Google Ads figures. Treat all of these as a scale for comparing pages and markets against each other, never as a count of visitors or a forecast.
Association, not cause
This is observational search data throughout. The reports can show which kinds of page sit in the positions that earn the clicks. They cannot show that the page design put them there, and they do not claim it. Each report carries its own list of what its evidence does not settle, and those lists are part of the deliverable rather than an admission.
What it cost
$5.11 of search-data API spend across 364 billed calls, against the $20 ceiling set for this engagement — summed from the cost figure each response returned, not estimated.

No licence numbers, licensee names, premises addresses or any other licence-identifying detail appears anywhere in this bundle, and no confidential term of any mandate is reproduced. Where a figure is a calculation of ours rather than a field published by the data supplier, the report carrying it says so and names the file it came from.
