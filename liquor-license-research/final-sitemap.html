Why no redirects are needed

A canonical tag, not a 301, is what consolidates a duplicate. Most of this site's pages exist at two URLs: a nested one and a flat one. The nested page already tells Google the flat one is the real page. That arrangement is fine and needs no redirect. The only thing wrong with it is that the sitemap submits the nested URL, which is the one the site disowns.

So the fix is to submit the flat URL instead and leave the nested page completely alone. 855 pages need no edit at all.

The exception, and the reason a blanket rule would have broken things. For 47 pages the flat URL is broken — it redirects, and most of those redirect straight back to the nested page. For those, the nested URL is the real page. Their canonical is changed to point at themselves and they go in the sitemap instead. Doing it the other way would have built a redirect loop.
